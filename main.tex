\begin{abstract}
\itshape

\noindent
Let \(X\) be a smooth curve over a field of characteristic zero and
let \(\cA\) be a complete augmented chiral algebra on \(X\).  This
volume studies the passage from the local collision operations of
\(\cA\) to its Ran-space bar construction, its completed cobar, and
its derived chiral centre.  The source and target of every comparison
are fixed before the comparison is made: the bar is a complete
conilpotent factorization coalgebra, the cobar is an augmented
factorization algebra, and the derived centre is an endomorphism
complex carrying its own deformation operations.

On a coordinate disc \(U\subset X\), write
\(\eta_{ij}=d\log(z_i-z_j)\).  The first calculation is
\[
\eta_{12}\wedge\eta_{23}+\eta_{23}\wedge\eta_{31}
+\eta_{31}\wedge\eta_{12}=0
\qquad\text{on }\operatorname{Conf}_3(U).
\]
This identity is the configuration-space calculation.  On the tensor
coalgebra, the arity-three bar cancellation is associativity with the
desuspension signs.  A geometric chiral bar comparison must assemble
these two facts with the chiral Jacobi identity, residue orientation
lines, and Stokes compatibility.  Their separation fixes the local
proof obligation for the constructions that follow.

Over a point, the bar--cobar unit and counit are quasi-isomorphisms
for augmented dg algebras and conilpotent dg coalgebras.  Francis and
Gaitsgory's pro-nilpotence theorem gives the enhanced associative
analogue in
\(\bigl(D(\operatorname{Ran}X),\otimes^{\mathrm{ch}}\bigr)\).
Re-unitalization yields the
universal reconstruction map
\(\Omega_XB_X(\cA)\xrightarrow{\sim}\cA\).  Its restriction to the
factorization subcategories and its completed chain realization are
formulated with explicit closure and convergence packages.  For a
quadratic presentation
\(\cA=T_X(V)/(R)\), the canonical map
\[
 q_\cA\colon \cA^{\mathrm i}=C_X(s^{-1}V,s^{-2}R)
 \longrightarrow B_X(\cA)
\]
is the Koszul comparison: under the chiral Comparison Lemma package,
its being a quasi-isomorphism is equivalent to
\(\Omega_X(\cA^{\mathrm i})\to\cA\) being a quasi-isomorphism and to
acyclicity of the quadratic twisted tensor products.  Verdier duality
forms the algebra
\(\mathbb D_{\operatorname{Ran}}B_X(\cA)\) under a separate
dualizability and continuity package.

Symmetrisation is treated as homotopy descent for the configuration
torsors.  Positive genus begins after a cyclic trace or perfect pairing
supplies loop contractions and stable-graph sewing.  In that setting a
complete graph filtration carries the modular Maurer--Cartan equation,
and a final trace produces scalar classes.  Chiral Hochschild support is
likewise family-indexed: a family datum consisting of an explicit
comparison and strong deformation retract transports bounded
vertex-cohomology calculations to the curve-level chart.  In
particular, Bakalov--De Sole--Kac compute the bounded vertex
cohomology of the rank-one even free superboson in degrees \(0,1\)
with dimensions \(2,1\), and the bounded vertex cohomology of the
universal Virasoro algebra in degrees \(0,2,3\), one-dimensional in
each degree.

The book therefore presents a tower of reconstruction theorems with
four coordinates attached to every claim: quadrant, presentation,
Beilinson level, and hypothesis package.  The local collision identity
and the classical bar--cobar resolution form the proved foundation.
The completed Ran, Verdier, modular, holographic, and
Calabi--Yau-to-chiral passages appear with the precise comparison data
that carry them.
\end{abstract}

a smooth curve, $D$ is a divisor, and $\tau$ is the chosen tangential
datum.  A factorization dg-category $\cC^{\mathrm{op}}$ with chosen
object $b$ determines the augmented chart
$A_b=\operatorname{RHom}_{\cC}(b,b)$.  These data generate the
successive constructions displayed below.

Vol I treats the Open quadrant of the four-quadrant theory: a
factorisation dg-category $\cC^{\op}$ on a tangential log curve
$(X, D, \tau)$, a chosen vacuum $b \in \cC^{\op}$, and the chart
algebra
$A_b=\operatorname{RHom}_{\cC}(b,b)$.  The open Beilinson tower is
the sequence of typed constructions
\[
 (\cC^{\op},b)
 \longmapsto A_b
 \longmapsto B_X(A_b)
 \longmapsto Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_b)
 \longmapsto \mathsf{Line}(A_b)

\]
Each arrow is a construction.  A reverse arrow is supplied by a named
reconstruction theorem with its own hypothesis package.  Part~I fixes
levels~$0$ and~$1$.  Part~II constructs the full bar coalgebra,
universal reconstruction, the quadratic comparison, and the Verdier
algebra at level~$2$.  Part~III constructs the derived chiral centre at
level~$3$.  Part~IV records the finite family atlas and its
recognition problems.  Part~V introduces cyclic trace and clutching
at levels~$4$ and~$5$.  Part~VI states the comparison functors and the
remaining reconstruction obligations.

The reader entering this part has already met the tangential log
curve, the factorisation dg-category, and the augmented chart algebra
in the Heisenberg overture
(Chapter~\ref{ch:heisenberg-frame}). Part~I supplies the
foundations needed to formulate those constructions: the ordinary
bar--cobar resolution, quadratic Koszul recognition, the three depth
indices, and the logarithmic configuration-space carrier.

On a coordinate disc, the first geometric calculation is Arnold's
relation
\[
\eta_{12} \wedge \eta_{23}
\;+\; \eta_{23} \wedge \eta_{31}
\;+\; \eta_{31} \wedge \eta_{12}
\;=\; 0
\]
on $\operatorname{Conf}_3(X)$.  The algebraic arity-three calculation
is the signed associativity cancellation in the tensor bar.  A global
chiral differential requires the logarithmic residue, orientation,
Jacobi--Borcherds, Stokes, and boundary-of-boundary maps collected in
$H_{\mathrm{loc}}$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:preface-local-square-zero}
states the resulting square-zero criterion.

The five theorem packages are:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\Alph*)},leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{Theorem~A: enhanced Ran reconstruction.}
 Francis--Gaitsgory give inverse enhanced associative bar and cobar

\item \textbf{Theorem~B: quadratic recognition.}
 For $A=T_X(V)/(R)$, the map

 quasi-isomorphism exactly when the equivalent twisting and PBW
 criteria hold under $H_{\mathrm{CL}}$.
\item \textbf{Theorem~C: centre-local-system complementarity.}
 On the strict flat C0 surface, the degree-zero fibre comparison gives
 the ordinary centre local system~$\mathcal Z(A)$.  C1 splits
 $\mathbf C_g(A)=R\Gamma(\overline{\mathcal M}_g,\mathcal Z(A))$
 by the represented Verdier involution; C2 supplies the
 shifted-symplectic refinement under its pairing package.
\item \textbf{Theorem~D: modular trace.}
 A perfect cyclic pairing, stable-graph chains, signed clutching, and

 multi-weight lanes.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge,row sep=large]
B_X^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)
  \arrow[r,"\operatorname{Av}"]
  \arrow[d,"(\phi_j^{\mathrm{ord}})_{j\in J}"']
&
B_X^\Sigma(\cA)
  \arrow[d,"(\phi_j)_{j\in J}"]
\\
\displaystyle\prod_{j\in J}Y_j
  \arrow[r,equal]
&
\displaystyle\prod_{j\in J}Y_j .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Consequently every class represented by a morphism
\(\phi_j\) on the symmetric target has a canonical ordered pullback.
The homotopy fiber of \(\operatorname{Av}\) lies in the kernel of all
these composites.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Completeness problem for symmetric invariants;
\ClaimStatusOpen]
A complete list of invariants surviving averaging requires a
conservativity theorem for a specified family
\(\{\phi_j\}_{j\in J}\) on the symmetric target.  The scalar trace,
Hochschild comparison, genus class, cross-channel correction, and
finite-atlas census each provide candidate target maps on their own
hypothesis packages.  Their joint conservativity is a separate
reconstruction problem.
\end{remark}
