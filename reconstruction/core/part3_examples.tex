\chapter{Free and square-zero ordered chiral algebras}

\section{Algebraic coefficients and factorization}
The free and square-zero calculations use a specified tensor product and augmentation.
Let $k$ be a field.
The ordinary calculations below take place in complexes of $k$-vector spaces.
An exact strong monoidal dg functor to an ambient coefficient category transports the displayed bar formulas when it preserves their direct sums and normalization.
This is a statement about that ambient tensor.

Tensoring a factorization object by a constant algebra requires further structure.
For example, $k^2\otimes\omega_{\Ran}$ has rank two on both a singleton and a separated pair.
Its singleton tensor square has rank four, so the required factorization equivalence cannot exist.
The generic formula $B\otimes F$ therefore does not by itself construct a factorizing ordered chiral algebra.
The actual examples of Theorem~\ref{cbc:two:independence} prove independence of the two products on a fixed factorizing family.

\section{The free algebra}
Let $V$ be a vector space in degree zero, and give $T(V)$ its usual augmentation.
Write $s=[1]$ for the cohomological suspension lowering degree by one.
Equip $k\oplus sV$ with primitive coproduct on its reduced summand.

\begin{theorem}[Bar of a free algebra]\label{thm:free-bar}
The inclusion of a generator as a one-input bar is a dg coalgebra quasi-isomorphism
\[
 k\oplus sV\longrightarrow\Barop(T(V)).
\]
Its underlying complexes admit a natural deformation retract.
In homological bar degrees,
\[
 \operatorname{Tor}^{T(V)}_0(k,k)=k,\qquad
 \operatorname{Tor}^{T(V)}_1(k,k)=V,\qquad
 \operatorname{Tor}^{T(V)}_r(k,k)=0\quad(r\ge2).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a generator word of length $n$, index its bar decompositions by subsets of the $n-1$ possible cuts.
The differential removes a cut, with alternating sign according to its position among the present cuts.
When $n>1$, insert the first cut if it is absent and send a word already containing that cut to zero.
Removing this inserted cut gives the identity, and every other removal cancels with the opposite insertion order.
Only weights zero and one remain.

This is the degree-zero instance of Theorem~\ref{cbc:free:bar}, whose proof also treats complexes with nonzero internal differential.
The inclusion preserves the primitive coproduct.
\end{proof}

For $V=kx$, the chain projection need not preserve coproducts: on $[x|x]$ its reduced coproduct survives after projection to $sx\otimes sx$, while the projected word is zero.
Thus the coalgebra comparison uses the inclusion specified in the theorem.
For finite-dimensional $V$, dualizing that map gives the square-zero dg algebra model $k\oplus V^\vee[-1]$ for derived endomorphisms.

\section{The square-zero algebra}
Let $M$ be a cochain complex and let $S(M)=k\oplus M$ have square-zero augmentation ideal.

\begin{proposition}[Bar of a square-zero algebra]\label{prop:square-zero}
The multiplication part of the normalized bar differential vanishes, and
\[
 \Barop(S(M))=T^c(sM)
\]
as dg coalgebras, with only the internal differential.
If $M$ is a $d$-dimensional vector space in degree zero, then
\[
 \dim_k\operatorname{Tor}^{S(M)}_r(k,k)=d^r,\qquad
 P_{S(M)}(z):=\sum_{r\ge0}d^rz^r=\frac1{1-dz}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every adjacent multiplication in $M$ is zero, so the multiplication terms vanish.
The unary term remains $sm\mapsto-s(d_Mm)$.
In the stated degree-zero case it is zero as well, and the tensors of length $r$ form the entire homology in bar degree $r$.
This proves both formulas.
\end{proof}

For a bounded finite-dimensional complex $M$, the full linear dual is the completed tensor algebra on $M^\vee[-1]$.
Its convolution multiplication models the derived endomorphism product by Theorem~\ref{cbc:free:duality}.
The completion is a product over tensor lengths, and odd generators have no imposed exterior relation.
The free and square-zero algebras therefore have different bars and dual products even when their generator complexes agree.

\section{A geometric realization in both directions}
A free geometric example in both associative directions requires more than a tensor product of its proposed state spaces.
Given transverse generators $V_\perp$ and chiral generators $M_X$, construct the coefficient objects, their factorization maps and both associative operations with their mixed coherences.
Construct the augmentation needed for each bar and specify the coefficient tensor used by it.
If a field description is used, supply its geometric realization and the comparisons with iterated operations.
Theorem~\ref{thm:formal-disc} recognizes fields from such a realization and does not construct the reverse functor.

When the transverse bar is identified with the tensor-algebra coefficient model, Theorem~\ref{cbc:free:bar} gives its primitive comparison.
A square-zero replacement gives the tensor coalgebra of Proposition~\ref{cbc:free:square-zero} under the corresponding realization hypotheses.
Keeping the second associative bar unchanged requires an operation-preserving comparison of the remaining chiral structures.
Constructing that comparison, and establishing the free geometric universal property, remain the two-direction realization problem.

\chapter{The quantum and Jordan planes}\label{chap:planes}

The quantum and Jordan planes have the same graded dimensions and augmentation Betti numbers.
Their bar coproducts distinguish the relations, and their Yoneda products distinguish the resulting augmentation boundaries.
We work over a field $k$, with zero differential and polynomial weight one on the algebra generators.

\section{The quantum plane}

Fix $q\in k^\times$ and set
\[
 A_q=k\langle x,y\rangle/(yx-qxy),\qquad
 \epsilon(x)=\epsilon(y)=0.
\]
The ordered monomials $x^ay^b$ form a basis, with product
$(x^ay^b)(x^cy^d)=q^{bc}x^{a+c}y^{b+d}$, by Theorem~\ref{cbc:qplane:pbw}.
In particular, $H_{A_q}(z)=(1-z)^{-2}$.
Write $A_q(-r)$ for a free graded left module whose generator has polynomial weight $r$.

\begin{theorem}[Quantum-plane Koszul resolution]\label{thm:qplane}
The augmented complex
\[
 0\longrightarrow A_q(-2)\xrightarrow{d_2}A_q(-1)^{\oplus2}
 \xrightarrow{d_1}A_q\xrightarrow{\epsilon}k\longrightarrow0,
\]
with $d_1(u,v)=ux+vy$ and $d_2(c)=(cy,-qcx)$, is exact.
Its augmentation Tor coalgebra is
\[
 k\oplus k\{sx,sy\}\oplus k\{\omega_q\},\qquad
 \omega_q=[y|x]-q[x|y],
\]
where $sx,sy$ have homological degree one and $\omega_q$ has degree two.
The degree-one classes are primitive, and
\[
 \overline\Delta\omega_q=sy\otimes sx-q\,sx\otimes sy.
\]
The Poincare polynomial is $1+2t+t^2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{cbc:qplane:resolution} proves exactness by solving the coefficient equation $ux+vy=0$ as $(u,v)=(cy,-qcx)$.
Right multiplication by $y$ is injective on the ordered basis, which proves injectivity at the left end.
Tensoring with $k$ kills both differentials.
Proposition~\ref{cbc:qplane:coalgebra} identifies this calculation with the displayed subcoalgebra of the normalized bar.
The bar cohomological degrees are the negatives of the homological degrees used here.
\end{proof}

For degree-one Yoneda generators $\xi,\eta$ and a nonzero degree-two class $\zeta$, Theorem~\ref{cbc:qplane:yoneda} gives
\[
 \xi^2=\eta^2=0,\qquad \xi\eta=q\zeta,\qquad \eta\xi=-\zeta.
\]
Thus the quadratic dual is
\[
 Q_q=k\langle\xi,\eta\rangle/(\xi^2,\eta^2,\xi\eta+q\eta\xi).
\]
The parameter survives in the product even though the Betti numbers agree with those of the commutative plane.

\section{The Jordan plane}

Set
\[
 A_J=k\langle x,y\rangle/(yx-xy-x^2),\qquad
 \epsilon(x)=\epsilon(y)=0.
\]
The rule $yx\mapsto xy+x^2$ decreases lexicographic order with $y>x$ among words of fixed length.
It therefore expresses every word as a sum of ordered monomials.

Their independence has a concrete realization on $k[t,z]$:
\[
 X(f)=tf,\qquad Y(f)=zf+t^2\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}.
\]
These operators satisfy $YX-XY=X^2$, and $X^aY^b(1)=t^az^b$.
The ordered monomials are therefore independent over any field.
Consequently $H_{A_J}(z)=(1-z)^{-2}$.

\begin{theorem}[Jordan-plane Koszul resolution]\label{thm:jordan}
The augmented complex
\[
 0\longrightarrow A_J(-2)\xrightarrow{d_2}A_J(-1)^{\oplus2}
 \xrightarrow{d_1}A_J\xrightarrow{\epsilon}k\longrightarrow0,
\]
with
\[
 d_1(u,v)=ux+vy,\qquad d_2(c)=(c(y-x),-cx),
\]
is exact.
Its augmentation Tor groups have Poincare polynomial $1+2t+t^2$.
The degree-two bar class has representative
\[
 \omega_J=[y|x]-[x|y]-[x|x].
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The composite is $c((y-x)x-xy)=0$.
Filter the ordered basis by the exponent of $y$.
The correction $x^2$ in the relation has smaller filtration degree, so the associated graded algebra is $k[x,y]$.
Give the two middle free generators filtration degrees zero and one, respectively, and the left generator filtration degree one.
The associated graded differential is then the quantum-plane resolution at $q=1$.
It is exact by Theorem~\ref{cbc:qplane:resolution}.

At each fixed polynomial weight, this filtration is finite.
For a cycle, lift a preimage of its leading filtered class and subtract its boundary.
The filtration degree drops, so repeating the procedure terminates.
This proves exactness.
The same leading-class argument gives injectivity at the left end.
The image at $A_J$ is the augmentation ideal.

Tensoring with $k$ kills both differentials.
The left bar comparison sends the middle generators to $-[x],-[y]$ and the left generator to $\omega_J$.
Its chain-map equation is
\[
 d_P(1\otimes\omega_J)
 =-y\otimes[x]+x\otimes[y]+x\otimes[x].
\]
The free-resolution comparison used in Proposition~\ref{cbc:qplane:coalgebra} therefore identifies the indicated cycle with the nonzero Tor class.
\end{proof}

\section{Products beyond the Betti numbers}

Put the Jordan resolution in cohomological degrees $0,-1,-2$, with generators $1,e_x,e_y,e_r$.
Let $\xi,\eta,\zeta$ be the dual augmentation cochains.
Closed degree-one lifts of $\xi$ and $\eta$ are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
       &e_x&e_y&e_r\\ \hline
 F_\xi&1&0&e_x-e_y\\
 F_\eta&0&1&e_x
 \end{array}
\]
and vanish on degree zero.
Their equations $dF+Fd=0$ reduce on $e_r$ to
$(x-y)+(y-x)=0$ and $x-x=0$.

Composition, in the same Yoneda convention as above, gives
\[
 \xi^2=\xi\eta=\zeta,\qquad
 \eta\xi=-\zeta,\qquad \eta^2=0.
\]
These products generate the four-dimensional Ext space, so
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}_{A_J}^*(k,k)
 \cong k\langle\xi,\eta\rangle/
       (\eta^2,\xi\eta+\eta\xi,\xi^2+\eta\xi).
\]
Indeed, the relations reduce every word of length at least three to zero and leave at most four basis elements.
They also annihilate the tensor $y\otimes x-x\otimes y-x\otimes x$, identifying this algebra with the quadratic dual.

In the Jordan dual, $(a\xi+b\eta)^2=a^2\zeta$, so the degree-one elements with square zero form the single line $k\eta$.
For the quantum dual, the square is $ab(q-1)\zeta$.
Its zero set is the union of the two coordinate lines when $q\ne1$, and the whole degree-one space when $q=1$.
Thus the graded Yoneda algebras are not isomorphic over any field.

In particular, no exact $k$-linear equivalence of the derived module categories can carry one augmentation module to the other.
Such an equivalence would preserve this graded endomorphism algebra.
This conclusion concerns that specified module, without asserting a classification of unrestricted module-category equivalences.

\section{Finite bar dimensions and ranks}

At polynomial weight $w>0$, let $C_{w,r}$ be the normalized bar tensors with $r$ nonunit monomial inputs.
The bar differential is $b_r:C_{w,r}\to C_{w,r-1}$.
Its matrix merges adjacent monomials with signs $(-1)^{j-1}$ at the $j$th join.
The common Hilbert series gives
\[
 c_{w,r}:=\dim C_{w,r}
 =[z^w]\bigl((1-z)^{-2}-1\bigr)^r.
\]

Write $\rho_{w,r}=\operatorname{rank}b_r$ and $h_{w,r}=\dim H_r(C_{w,\bullet})$.
Then
\[
 h_{w,r}=c_{w,r}-\rho_{w,r}-\rho_{w,r+1}.
\]
The exact resolutions determine $h_{1,1}=2$, $h_{2,2}=1$ and all other positive-weight homology dimensions as zero.
Starting from $\rho_{w,1}=0$, the displayed identity determines every rank.

Thus both algebras have the following table, for every allowed $q$ and over every field:
\[
\begin{array}{cc|rrrr}
 w&r&c_{w,r}&\rho_{w,r}&\rho_{w,r+1}&h_{w,r}\\ \hline
 1&1&2&0&0&2\\
 2&1&3&0&3&0\\
 2&2&4&3&0&1\\
 3&1&4&0&4&0\\
 3&2&12&4&8&0\\
 3&3&8&8&0&0\\
 4&1&5&0&5&0\\
 4&2&25&5&20&0\\
 4&3&36&20&16&0\\
 4&4&16&16&0&0\\
 5&1&6&0&6&0\\
 5&2&44&6&38&0\\
 5&3&102&38&64&0\\
 5&4&96&64&32&0\\
 5&5&32&32&0&0
\end{array}
\]
The finite table illustrates the all-weight resolution theorems.
It does not identify the coproducts of the two bar coalgebras or the products of their duals.

\section{Internal chiral realization}

To realize either plane geometrically, construct its coefficient objects, factorization equivalences, augmentation and transverse multiplication with the indicated relation.
Then compare the entire geometric bar with the coefficient bar, including its coproduct and realization hypotheses.
The formulas above give the algebraic side of that comparison.

Tensoring a constant plane algebra with a factorizing object is insufficient by the rank example of Chapter~\ref{chap:cbc:free-square-zero}.
The actual marked-point construction of Theorem~\ref{cbc:two:independence} remains available.
A second associative chiral operation requires its own construction and the mixed coherences with transverse multiplication.
These maps are the additional content of a quantum- or Jordan-plane ordered chiral realization.

\chapter{Augmentation obstructions, Weyl curvature, and matrix sectors}\label{chap:augmentation}

A bar construction with scalar endpoints requires a character of the algebra.
The Weyl relation forbids such a character, while a matrix algebra replaces the scalar endpoint by a projective module.
An augmented algebra can nevertheless contain a full matrix sector.
We compute an example in which the scalar bar and a cyclic trace retain different parts of that algebra.

\section{The Weyl obstruction}
Let $k$ be a field and
\[
 W_1=k\langle x,y\rangle/(yx-xy-1).
\]
\begin{theorem}[No scalar augmentation for the Weyl algebra]\label{thm:weyl-noaug}
There is no unital algebra map $W_1\to k$.
Consequently $k$ cannot be made a left or right $W_1$-module by a scalar augmentation, and the derived tensor with those prescribed endpoints is undefined.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Applying such a map to the relation gives
$\epsilon(y)\epsilon(x)-\epsilon(x)\epsilon(y)=1$.
The left side is zero in $k$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The polynomial family
\[
 W_\hbar=k[\hbar]\langle x,y\rangle/(yx-xy-\hbar)
\]
has a $k$-algebra character sending $x,y,\hbar$ to zero.
Every $k$-algebra character sends $\hbar$ to zero, by the same commutator calculation.
There is no $k[\hbar]$-algebra augmentation to $k[\hbar]$ fixing $\hbar$.
Its fiber at zero is the polynomial plane.
Its fiber at one is $W_1$.
The genuine word-Rees algebra of $W_1$ has relation $YX-XY=z^2$, as proved in Chapter~\ref{cbc:weyl:chapter}.

The curved dual of $W_\hbar$ is
\[
 E=\Lambda_{k[\hbar]}(\xi,\eta),\qquad d=0,
 \qquad c=\hbar\xi\eta,
\]
where $\xi,\eta$ have cohomological degree one and are dual to $[x],[y]$.
With $\rho=[y|x]-[x|y]$, the cobar differential is
\[
 D(t\rho)=yx-xy-\hbar.
\]
Theorem~\ref{cbc:weyl:cobar} proves that the resulting unital dg algebra resolves $W_\hbar$.
Since $c$ is central, $d^2=[c,-]=0$.
Ordinary cohomology of the underlying complex exists and equals $E$.
It forgets the curvature and hence the constant Weyl relation.

Modules also provide valid bar endpoints.
For example, $W_1$ acts on $k[t]$ by $x=t$ and $y=\partial_t$.
For a specified right module $M$ and left module $N$, the two-sided bar computes $M\otimes^{\mathbb L}_{W_1}N$.
This construction uses their actual module actions, without a scalar character.

\section{The matrix obstruction}
\begin{theorem}[No scalar augmentation for a full matrix algebra]\label{thm:matrix-noaug}
For $n>1$, there is no unital algebra map $M_n(k)\to k$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $E_{ij}$ be the matrix units.
Under a hypothetical map $\phi$, the orthogonal idempotents $E_{ii}$ have values zero or one and sum to one.
Exactly one, say $E_{ii}$, has value one.
Choose $j\ne i$.
The equations $E_{ij}E_{ji}=E_{ii}$ and $E_{ji}E_{ij}=E_{jj}$ would give the same scalar product both values one and zero.
\end{proof}

Put $A=M_n(k)$ and $e=E_{11}$.
The right module $eA$ and left module $Ae$ are projective, and multiplication gives
\[
 eA\otimes_A^{\mathbb L}Ae
       =eA\otimes_A Ae\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }eAe\cong k.
\]
The inverse sends $a\in eAe$ to $e\otimes a$.
Indeed, $er\otimes se=e\otimes rse=e\otimes erse$ by balancing.
This is the scalar corner selected by these modules.

The ordinary trace is instead a cyclic linear functional.
It does not supply their module actions or an algebra augmentation.
A geometric realization by matrix-valued fields still requires the factorization coefficients and their compatible operations.

\section{An augmented algebra with a matrix sector}
Let
\[
 D=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2),\qquad B_n=D\times M_n(k),\qquad
 \varepsilon_B(a+b\epsilon,M)=a.
\]
This is a unital augmented algebra, noncommutative for $n>1$.
Its scalar augmentation is unrelated to the cyclic functional
\[
 \tau_B(a+b\epsilon,M)=b+\operatorname{Tr}(M).
\]

The pairing $\tau_B(uv)$ is symmetric and nondegenerate.
In $D$, the ordered basis $(1,\epsilon)$ has dual basis $(\epsilon,1)$.
In the matrix summand, $E_{ij}$ is dual to $E_{ji}$.
These dual bases prove nondegeneracy in every characteristic, including when the characteristic divides $n$.

For a symmetric Frobenius algebra with dual bases $(u_a),(u^a)$, define its handle element to be $e=\sum_a u_a u^a$.
This definition is independent of the dual bases, since their tensor is the inverse of the pairing.

\begin{proposition}[Bar and handle element of $B_n$]\label{prop:Bn}
Projection $\pi:B_n\to D$ induces a quasi-isomorphism of coaugmented dg coalgebras
\[
 B\pi:B(B_n)\longrightarrow B(D)=T^c(s k\epsilon).
\]
Consequently
\[
 k\otimes_{B_n}^{\mathbb L}k\simeq k\otimes_D^{\mathbb L}k,
 \qquad \dim_k\operatorname{Tor}^{B_n}_r(k,k)=1\quad(r\geq0).
\]
The handle element and its contractions are
\[
 e_{B_n}=(2\epsilon,nI_n),\qquad
 \tau_B(1)=n,\qquad \tau_B(e_{B_n})=2+n^2,\qquad
 \tau_B(e_{B_n}^g)=n^{g+1}\quad(g\geq2),
\]
with the integer coefficients interpreted in $k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\varepsilon_D:D\to k$ be its augmentation.
The map
\[
 \sigma:D\longrightarrow B_n,\qquad
 \sigma(d)=(d,\varepsilon_D(d)I_n),
\]
is unital, multiplicative and augmented, and $\pi\sigma=\id_D$.
Thus $B\pi$ and $B\sigma$ are dg coalgebra maps whose composite is the identity on $B(D)$.

The periodic resolution of the augmentation module over $D$ is
\[
 \cdots\xrightarrow{\epsilon}D\xrightarrow{\epsilon}D
       \xrightarrow{\epsilon}D\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_D}k\longrightarrow0.
\]
Its exactness follows from $\ker(\epsilon:D\to D)=k\epsilon=\operatorname{im}(\epsilon)$.
Via $\pi$, each term is the projective $B_n$-module $B_n(1,0)$.
It therefore remains a projective resolution over $B_n$.
Tensoring with the right augmentation module kills every differential and leaves one copy of $k$ in every nonnegative homological degree.

The reduced bar of $D$ has zero differential because $(k\epsilon)^2=0$.
Both bar homologies thus have dimension one in every homological degree.
Since $B\pi$ has the section $B\sigma$, it is an isomorphism on homology.
This proves the asserted dg coalgebra comparison and the derived tensor calculation.

The displayed dual bases give
\[
 e_D=1\epsilon+\epsilon1=2\epsilon,
 \qquad e_{M_n}=\sum_{i,j}E_{ij}E_{ji}=nI_n.
\]
Products and powers in $B_n$ are componentwise.
For $g\geq2$, $(2\epsilon)^g=0$, while
$\operatorname{Tr}((nI_n)^g)=n^{g+1}$.
The remaining trace values follow directly from the definition of $\tau_B$.
\end{proof}

The scalar bar retains the dual-number sector.
The Frobenius contractions also detect the matrix sector.
These are algebraic contraction values.
A closed oriented two-dimensional field theory requires a commutative closed state algebra and compatible sewing maps.
Those data are not supplied by the noncommutative Frobenius algebra alone.
Likewise, interpreting these values as one genus variable of a mixed theory requires its two families of operations and their interchange maps.

\begin{nogobox}[title={No-go theorem 13: a trace is not an augmentation}]
A cyclic functional satisfies $\tau(ab)=\tau(ba)$, while an augmentation must satisfy $\varepsilon(ab)=\varepsilon(a)\varepsilon(b)$ and $\varepsilon(1)=1$.
The bar endpoint actions use the latter identities.
Replacing them by cyclic pairings changes the construction and does not define the same derived tensor product.
\end{nogobox}

\chapter{Relative quiver bars and boundary conditions}\label{chap:quiver}
The relative bar retains the objects at both ends of a composable sequence.
For a chain of two arrows whose composite is zero, the relation gives a degree-two bar class and a nonzero Yoneda product.
Keeping the vertex idempotents makes both statements visible.

\section{The natural base}

Let $k$ be a field, $Q$ a finite quiver and
\[
 S=\bigoplus_{i\in Q_0}ke_i,\qquad
 M=\bigoplus_{a:i\to j}ka,\qquad a=e_jae_i.
\]
The path algebra is $T_S(M)$, with paths multiplied from right to left.
Give arrows weight one and vertex idempotents weight zero.
Its augmentation fixes the vertex idempotents and kills the arrows.
For a quotient $A$ by homogeneous relations of positive weight, the same augmentation $\epsilon:A\to S$ remains defined.
Write $\bar A=\ker\epsilon$.

Use the direct-sum relative bar
\[
 B_SA=\bigoplus_{r\ge0}(s\bar A)^{\otimes_S r},\qquad
 s=[1],\qquad |sa|=|a|-1,
\]
with deconcatenation and the normalized differential of Theorem~\ref{cbc:internal:normalized}.
For ordinary inputs, $b[a|a']= [aa']$.
The relative bar resolution identifies this complex with $S\otimes_A^{\mathbb L}S$, where the first input is a right $A$-module and the second a left $A$-module.
In particular, $H^{-r}(B_SA)=\operatorname{Tor}^A_r(S,S)$.

To name individual endpoints, let $R_j=e_jS$ be the right simple and $L_i=Se_i$ the left simple, both regarded as $A$-modules through $\epsilon$.
Taking vertex summands of the bar resolution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cbc:quiver:corners}
 e_jH^{-r}(B_SA)e_i
 \cong\operatorname{Tor}^A_r(R_j,L_i).
\end{equation}
The balancing over $S$ admits only composable inputs, and the two outer idempotents retain their target and source.
When the quiver is realized by boundary conditions, these are the two boundary labels.

\section{A relation and its coproduct}

Consider
\[
 1\xrightarrow{a}2\xrightarrow{b}3,\qquad A=kQ/(ba).
\]
This algebra has basis $e_1,e_2,e_3,a,b$.
Its augmentation ideal is $ka\oplus kb$, and its only composable two-input tensor is $[b|a]$.
There is no composable triple.

\begin{theorem}[Relative bar of the relation quiver]\label{thm:quiver-bar}
The relative bar has zero differential and underlying $S$-bimodule
\[
 B_SA=S\oplus k[a]\oplus k[b]\oplus k\omega,
 \qquad \omega=[b|a].
\]
The cohomological degrees of $[a],[b],\omega$ are $-1,-1,-2$.
Its reduced coproduct vanishes on $[a],[b]$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cbc:quiver:relation-coproduct}
 \overline\Delta\omega=[b]\otimes_S[a].
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{Tor}^A_0(S,S)=S,\qquad
 \operatorname{Tor}^A_1(S,S)=k[a]\oplus k[b],\qquad
 \operatorname{Tor}^A_2(S,S)=k\omega,
\]
and all higher Tor groups vanish.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The basis and composability list exhaust the normalized relative bar.
Its sole possible merger is $b[b|a]=[ba]=0$, and there is no internal differential.
Thus every listed element is a cycle and there are no boundaries.
Deconcatenation at the unique internal cut gives~\eqref{eq:cbc:quiver:relation-coproduct}.
The endpoint terms in the full coproduct of $\omega$ are $e_3\otimes\omega$ and $\omega\otimes e_1$.
This proves the assertion as an $S$-coring as well as a homology calculation.
\end{proof}

The nonzero corners in positive homological degrees are therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Tor}^A_1(R_2,L_1)=k[a],\qquad
 \operatorname{Tor}^A_1(R_3,L_2)=k[b],\qquad
 \operatorname{Tor}^A_2(R_3,L_1)=k\omega.
\]
The class $\omega$ records the relation and retains the two arrows in its coproduct.

For the free path algebra $kQ$, the augmentation ideal also contains $c=ba$.
Its bar has the same two-input tensor, but now
\[
 b[b|a]=[c]\ne0.
\]
The pair $[b|a],[c]$ is contractible.
Only the two arrow classes survive in positive homological degree, both in degree one.
Thus the relation changes the actual differential and creates the degree-two class.

\section{Simple modules and the Yoneda product}

Return to $A=kQ/(ba)$ and put $P_i=Ae_i$.
The left projectives have bases
\[
 P_1=\langle e_1,a\rangle,\qquad
 P_2=\langle e_2,b\rangle,\qquad P_3=ke_3.
\]
The following augmented complexes resolve the left simples:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K_1:&\quad 0\longrightarrow P_3
   \xrightarrow{\,u\mapsto ub\,}P_2
   \xrightarrow{\,v\mapsto va\,}P_1\longrightarrow L_1\longrightarrow0,\\
 K_2:&\quad 0\longrightarrow P_3
   \xrightarrow{\,u\mapsto ub\,}P_2\longrightarrow L_2\longrightarrow0,\\
 K_3:&\quad P_3\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }L_3.
 \end{aligned}
\]
In each complex, the projective immediately before the simple is in cohomological degree zero.

Right multiplication by $a$ has image $ka$ and kernel $kb$.
Right multiplication by $b$ identifies $P_3$ with that kernel, and the same map resolves the radical of $P_2$.
This proves exactness of all three complexes.
Assigning weight one to the degree-minus-one generators and weight two to the top generator of $K_1$ makes these resolutions linear, so $A$ is Koszul.

Applying $\operatorname{Hom}_A(-,L_j)$ kills every differential, because arrows annihilate the simples.
Besides the identity classes, the only Ext generators are
\[
 \alpha\in\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(L_1,L_2),\qquad
 \beta\in\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(L_2,L_3),\qquad
 \gamma\in\operatorname{Ext}^2_A(L_1,L_3).
\]
Normalize each generator by the functional taking the corresponding projective generator to the vertex basis of its target simple.

To compute composition, lift $\alpha$ to a degree-one map $F_\alpha:K_1\to K_2$.
Its components are
\[
 F_\alpha^{-1}=\id_{P_2},
 \qquad F_\alpha^{-2}=-\id_{P_3},
\]
with every other component zero.
Lift $\beta$ to $F_\beta:K_2\to K_3$ by $F_\beta^{-1}=\id_{P_3}$ and zero elsewhere.

The differential of a homogeneous Hom map is $\partial F=dF-(-1)^{|F|}Fd$.
For $F_\alpha$, its only potentially nonzero component is
\[
 (dF_\alpha+F_\alpha d)(u)=-ub+ub=0,
 \qquad u\in K_1^{-2}=P_3.
\]
The other closure equations, including those for $F_\beta$, have zero source or zero target.
Thus these are closed lifts of the normalized Ext classes.
Their composite on $K_1^{-2}$ is $-\id_{P_3}$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cbc:quiver:yoneda}
 \beta\alpha=-\gamma\ne0.
\end{equation}
This convention includes characteristic two, where the displayed minus sign equals a plus sign.

The sign also follows from the relative bar.
The resolution comparison sends the arrow generators to $-[a]$ and $-[b]$, and the relation generator to $[b|a]$.
Its compatibility with the top differential is the identity
$d(1\otimes[b|a])=-b\otimes[a]$ in the left bar resolution.
In signed evaluation on deconcatenation, the degree-one functional on $[a]$ crosses the degree-minus-one tensor $[b]$, contributing $-1$.
The two minus signs from the arrow comparisons cancel, leaving~\eqref{eq:cbc:quiver:yoneda}.

Every other product of two positive-degree generators vanishes for lack of matching endpoints or a nonzero Ext group in its degree.
For the free quiver, the degree-two group vanishes and hence $\beta\alpha=0$.
The two algebras therefore have different Yoneda multiplication, as well as different Tor groups.

\section{Scalar boundary objects and geometric families}

A graded scalar augmentation $A\to k$, with $k$ in weight zero, sends every arrow to zero.
On $S$, it sends orthogonal idempotents to orthogonal idempotents in a field.
Their images are zero or one and sum to one, so exactly one vertex is selected.
For that vertex $i$, the scalar derived tensor is
\[
 R_i\otimes_A^{\mathbb L}L_i\simeq e_iB_SA e_i.
\]
In the relation example, each such diagonal corner is just $ke_i$.
It omits all three positive-degree fields, whose endpoints are distinct.

\begin{nogobox}[title={Scalar augmentation and vertex labels}]
The relative bar $S\otimes_A^{\mathbb L}S$ retains all boundary labels.
A graded scalar augmentation specifies one vertex and its diagonal derived corner.
It cannot retain the off-diagonal boundary-changing fields of the full relative bar.
\end{nogobox}

To realize these algebras over $X$, first construct the vertex and arrow coefficient objects with their disjoint factorization maps.
They must carry a compatible transverse product and augmentation to the vertex object.
The relation maps must extend through collisions, and the full geometric bar must be compared with the relative coefficient bar above.
The module interpretation then requires the marked block-Ran comparison for those actual coefficient objects.

A second associative chiral operation must act on composable coefficients and satisfy the mixed coherences of Chapter~\ref{chap:presentations}.
Operations on the separate arrow objects do not by themselves supply these compositions.
The relative calculation determines the vertex-labeled bar and Yoneda data that such a geometric realization must preserve.

\chapter{Enveloping algebras and planar-sequence obstructions}\label{chap:enveloping}

\section{Cartan--Eilenberg comparison}
Let \(\mathfrak g\) be a dg Lie algebra object in the coefficient category and \(U(\mathfrak g)\) its augmented enveloping algebra.

\begin{theorem}[Enveloping bar]\label{thm:enveloping-bar}
The antisymmetrisation twisting map induces a natural quasi-isomorphism
\[
 \CE_*(\mathfrak g)
 =\bigl(\Sym^c(s\mathfrak g),d_{\mathfrak g}+d_{[-,-]}\bigr)
 \xrightarrow{\sim}
 \Barop^\perp(U(\mathfrak g)).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Filter both complexes by PBW degree.  The associated graded of \(U(\mathfrak g)\) is \(\Sym(\mathfrak g)\).  The comparison becomes the standard Koszul quasi-isomorphism from the exterior/symmetric coalgebra to the bar of a polynomial algebra.  The complete, bounded-below filtration spectral sequence lifts the equivalence.
\end{proof}

For a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra over \(\mathbb C\),
\[
 H^*(\mathfrak g;\mathbb C)
 \cong\Lambda(\zeta_{2m_1+1},\ldots,\zeta_{2m_r+1}),
\]
where \(m_i\) are the exponents.  By duality the same Poincare polynomial describes homology.

\begin{calcbox}[title={The \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) chain matrix}]
Take \([h,e]=2e\), \([h,f]=-2f\), \([e,f]=h\).  In bases
\[
 C_1=(e,f,h),\qquad C_2=(e\wedge f,e\wedge h,f\wedge h),
\]
the Chevalley differential is
\[
 d_2=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&-2&0\\
 0&0&2\\
 1&0&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad d_3=0.
\]
Thus \(d_2\) has rank three and
\[
 H_0\cong\mathbb C,
 \quad H_1=H_2=0,
 \quad H_3\cong\mathbb C,
 \qquad P(t)=1+t^3.
\]
\end{calcbox}

The standard exact values are
\begin{align*}
 U(\mathfrak{sl}_2):&\quad 1+t^3,\\
 U(\mathfrak{sl}_3):&\quad(1+t^3)(1+t^5)=1+t^3+t^5+t^8,\\
 U(\mathfrak g_2):&\quad(1+t^3)(1+t^{11})=1+t^3+t^{11}+t^{14}.
\end{align*}

\section{Three chain complexes}
Three objects must be distinguished:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the interval bar \(\Barop^\perp(A)\), whose differential uses transverse multiplication;
\item chiral Chevalley chains, whose differential uses a diagonal-supported chiral Lie bracket;
\item a screen-enhanced bicomplex
\[
 B^{p,q}_{\mathrm{scr}}(A)=
 (s\widebar A)^{\tensorpt p}\otimes C^q_{\mathrm{scr}}
\]
with total differential \(b_A+d_A+d_{\mathrm{scr}}+\delta_{\mathrm{int}}\).
\end{enumerate}
Only the third can contain both bar words and configuration-space cohomology, and only after the interaction differential is supplied and shown to square to zero.

Filtering by screen degree gives an \(E_1\)-page
\[
 E_1^{p,q}=H^q(C_{\mathrm{scr}})\otimes B_p^\perp(A),
\]
while filtering by bar length gives
\[
 {}'E_1^{p,q}=\Tor_p^A(\one,\one)\otimes C^q_{\mathrm{scr}}.
\]
A product of dimensions on an \(E_1\)-page is not automatically the dimension of total homology.

\section{The planar sequences}
The Motzkin generating function is
\[
 M(z)=1+zM(z)+z^2M(z)^2
 =\frac{1-z-\sqrt{1-2z-3z^2}}{2z^2}.
\]
For \(a_n=M_{n+1}-M_n\),
\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}a_nz^n
 =\frac{4z}{\bigl(1-z+\sqrt{1-2z-3z^2}\bigr)^2},
\]
with first values
\[
 1,2,5,12,30,76,196,512,1353,3610.
\]
The Riordan series is
\[
 R(z)=\frac{1+z-\sqrt{1-2z-3z^2}}{2z(1+z)}.
\]
Both have dominant singularity \(z=1/3\) and exponential growth rate three.  These are correct combinatorial facts.

\begin{theorem}[Non-identification of Motzkin and Riordan sequences with ordered bar homology]\label{thm:legacy-audit}
The displayed sequences are not the ordered bar homology of \(U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\), and a Virasoro chiral coefficient does not determine a transverse bar whose dimensions are Motzkin differences.  The former is contradicted by \cref{thm:enveloping-bar}; the latter fails because the same Virasoro coefficient can be tensored with free, square-zero, enveloping, or quantum-plane transverse algebras having different bars.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\), the exact Chevalley calculation gives only degrees zero and three.  For the Virasoro statement, choose a fixed Virasoro factorisation coefficient \(F_{\mathrm{Vir},c}\) and form \(B\otimes F_{\mathrm{Vir},c}\) for the four transverse algebras listed.  The coefficient and central charge are unchanged while the interval bar varies with \(B\).
\end{proof}

\Cref{thm:legacy-audit} refutes the \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) claim by computing the
answer and the Virasoro claim by non-determination.  The Virasoro row admits the
stronger statement: in its transverse enveloping presentation the bar homology is
known exactly, and it leaves no room for a Motzkin difference.

\section{The Virasoro row computed}
The transverse presentation of the Virasoro family is the enveloping algebra of
the positive Witt algebra
\[
 L_1=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}\kfield\,e_n,
 \qquad
 [e_i,e_j]=(j-i)e_{i+j},
\]
to which \cref{thm:enveloping-bar} applies: \(L_1\) is positively graded with
one-dimensional weight spaces, so every weight has finitely many decompositions.

\begin{theorem}[Pentagonal rigidity]\label{thm:pentagonal}
The bar homology of \(U(L_1)\) satisfies
\[
 \dim H_n\bigl(\Barop^{\perp}U(L_1)\bigr)=2
 \qquad\text{for every }n\ge1,
\]
one dimension in each of the weights \((3n^2-n)/2\) and \((3n^2+n)/2\).  Its
graded Euler character is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witt-euler}
 \chi_q\bigl(\Barop^{\perp}U(L_1)\bigr)=\prod_{n\ge1}\bigl(1-q^n\bigr),
\end{equation}
and \eqref{eq:witt-euler} is accounted for degree by degree with no residual
freedom.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:enveloping-bar} the bar of \(U(L_1)\) is quasi-isomorphic to \(\CE_*(L_1)\), whose
weight subcomplexes are finite-dimensional. Their homology dimensions agree
with those of the weightwise dual cohomology, which Goncharova computes
\cite{Goncharova1973}: two dimensions in each degree \(n\ge1\), in the
weights \((3n^2\mp n)/2\).  Identity \eqref{eq:witt-euler} is
\cref{thm:bar-denominator} applied to \(L_1\), where every root space is one
dimensional and even, so \(\sdim(L_1)_n=1\).  Euler's pentagonal number theorem
expands the product as \(\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^mq^{m(3m-1)/2}\), whose support
is exactly \(\{(3n^2\pm n)/2\}\) with coefficient \((-1)^n\) at each of
\((3n^2-n)/2\) and \((3n^2+n)/2\).  Comparing with \(\sum_n(-1)^n\dim H_{n,w}\),
each weight of the Euler character is saturated by a single class in the degree
Goncharova predicts, so no further classes and no cancellation are available.
\end{proof}

\begin{statusbox}
Finite calculation. In homological degrees $n=1,2,3,4$, the Chevalley complex of $L_1$ has one-dimensional homology in each of the weight pairs $(1,2)$, $(5,7)$, $(12,15)$, $(22,26)$. Its differential squares to zero. The nonzero coefficients of $\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)$ in these weights and weight zero have signs $+,-,-,+,+,-,-,+,+$ at $0,1,2,5,7,12,15,22,26$. The all-degree assertion follows from Goncharova's theorem and Euler's identity.
\end{statusbox}

A Motzkin path is a lattice path from $(0,0)$ to $(n,0)$, staying at
nonnegative height, with steps $(1,1)$, $(1,0)$, and $(1,-1)$.
Let $M(n)$ count all such paths of length $n$, and let $R(n)$ count those with
no horizontal step at height zero.  Both counts include the empty path:
$M(0)=R(0)=1$, while $M(1)=1$ and $R(1)=0$.
For $n\ge1$ consider the two sequences
\[
 a_n=M(n+1)-M(n)=1,2,5,12,30,76,\ldots,
 \qquad
 b_n=R(n+3)=3,6,15,36,91,\ldots.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Motzkin and Riordan identities]
\label{prop:one-sequence}
For every $n\ge0$,
\[
 M(n)=R(n)+R(n+1),\qquad
 M(n+1)-M(n)=R(n+2)-R(n).
\]
The ordinary generating functions of the two path counts, and hence those
of $a_n$ and $b_n$, belong to
$\mathbb Q(z,\sqrt{1-2z-3z^2})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Work in the formal power-series ring $\mathbb Q[[z]]$, and write
$M(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}M(n)z^n$ and $R(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}R(n)z^n$.
A nonempty Motzkin path either starts with a horizontal step followed by
an arbitrary Motzkin path, or has a unique first-return decomposition
$U P D Q$, where $U$ and $D$ are the up and down steps,
$P$ and $Q$ are arbitrary Motzkin paths, and $P$ is drawn
one unit above the horizontal axis.  Thus
\[
 M=1+zM+z^2M^2.
\]
A nonempty path counted by $R$ has no initial horizontal step.  Its first
return similarly has the form $U P D Q$, with $P$ arbitrary and $Q$ counted
by $R$.  Consequently
\[
 R=1+z^2MR,\qquad R=(1-z^2M)^{-1}.
\]
The inverses here exist because their denominators have constant term one.
Using $z^2M^2=M-1-zM$, compute
\[
 (1+zM)(1-z^2M)
 =1+zM-z^2M-z^3M^2=1+z.
\]
It follows that $(1+z)R=1+zM$.  Comparing coefficients of $z^{n+1}$ gives
$R(n+1)+R(n)=M(n)$ for all $n\ge0$.  Subtracting consecutive identities
gives the difference formula.

Solving the quadratic equation for the formal series with constant term
one gives
\[
 M(z)=\frac{1-z-\sqrt{1-2z-3z^2}}{2z^2},\qquad
 R(z)=\frac{1+zM(z)}{1+z},
\]
where the square root has constant term one.  The apparent pole in the
formula for $M$ is removable as a formal series.  Finite index shifts and
differences preserve the indicated quadratic function field, proving the
last assertion.  These identities are consequences solely of the path
decompositions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Low-degree homology]
Theorem~\ref{thm:pentagonal} gives homology dimensions $2,2,2$ in degrees $1,2,3$, whereas $a_1,a_2,a_3$ are $1,2,5$. These triples agree only in degree two. The degree-one discrepancy already excludes equality of the full homology dimension sequences.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Enveloping bars and planar path counts]
\label{thm:carrier-planar-reconstruction}
Let $B_\bullet(A)$ be the ordinary normalized bar over $\mathbb C$, with
trivial coefficients and homological degree given by bar length.  With
$a_n=M(n+1)-M(n)$ and $b_n=R(n+3)$ indexed by $n\ge1$ as above,
\[
 \dim H_1 B_\bullet(U(\mathfrak{sl}_2))=0\ne b_1=3,
 \qquad
 \dim H_1 B_\bullet(U(L_1))=2\ne a_1=1,
\]
where $L_1=\bigoplus_{m\ge1}\mathbb C e_m$ and
$[e_i,e_j]=(j-i)e_{i+j}$.
Hence neither sequence is the bar homology dimension sequence of the
corresponding enveloping algebra.  Neither is its full bar chain dimension
sequence: in both cases $B_n$ is countably infinite-dimensional for every
$n\ge1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any augmented algebra $A$ with augmentation ideal $I$, the bar
differential out of $B_1=I$ is zero and the image from $B_2=I\otimes I$ is
$I^2$.  Therefore
\[
 H_1B_\bullet(A)=I/I^2.
\]
For an ordinary Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, the inclusion of generators
induces an isomorphism
\[
 \mathfrak g/[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 I/I^2,\qquad I=\ker(U(\mathfrak g)\to\mathbb C).
\]
Indeed the generators span $I/I^2$, and
$[x,y]=xy-yx$ vanishes there.  For injectivity, equip
$\mathbb C\oplus\mathfrak g/[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]$ with the square-zero
product on its second summand.  The quotient map from $\mathfrak g$ to that
summand is a Lie map into the commutator Lie algebra of this associative
algebra.  The universal property of $U(\mathfrak g)$ extends it to an
augmented algebra map, which kills $I^2$ and provides an inverse on the
displayed quotient.

The brackets $[h,e]=2e$, $[h,f]=-2f$, and $[e,f]=h$ show that
$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ equals its commutator algebra.  For $L_1$, every nonzero
bracket has weight at least three.  Conversely, for $m\ge3$,
\[
 e_m=\frac{1}{m-2}[e_1,e_{m-1}].
\]
Thus $[L_1,L_1]=\bigoplus_{m\ge3}\mathbb C e_m$, and its abelianization
has basis the classes of $e_1,e_2$, in weights one and two.  This proves
both degree-one assertions without using any higher-degree calculation.

PBW gives a countably infinite basis of the augmentation ideal of either
enveloping algebra.  In the $L_1$ case the basis consists of nonempty
ordered monomials in the countable set $e_1,e_2,\ldots$, each monomial
having finite length.  Finite tensor powers of either augmentation ideal
are therefore countably infinite-dimensional.  The path counts are finite
in every degree, so they cannot count these full chain spaces.
\end{proof}

In particular, a complex whose homology dimensions are $b_n$ cannot be
quasi-isomorphic to $B_\bullet(U(\mathfrak{sl}_2))$, and one whose homology
dimensions are $a_n$ cannot be quasi-isomorphic to $B_\bullet(U(L_1))$,
with the stated degree convention.  A discrepancy of chain dimensions
alone would not imply this conclusion; the degree-one homology discrepancy
does.

The weight grading gives a different, finite comparison.  An ordered PBW
monomial of weight $w$ specifies a partition of $w$, so for $n\ge1$,
\[
 \sum_{w\ge0}\dim B_n(U(L_1))_w q^w
 =\left(\prod_{m\ge1}(1-q^m)^{-1}-1\right)^n.
\]
Each coefficient is finite.  Thus a weightwise dimension, a dimension
after a specified weight cutoff, and an ungraded dimension are distinct
invariants.  In a weight completion, dimensions must likewise be interpreted
weightwise; the countable algebraic dimensions above refer to the ordinary
direct-sum enveloping algebras.

A sequence of integers becomes bar homology only after a chain complex, differential, grading convention, and quasi-isomorphism or rank computation are specified.  Matching initial values, discriminants, or growth rates does not construct such a map.
\end{nogobox}

\chapter{Finite-label exchange and its bar coalgebra}\label{chap:zf}

\section{The exchange algebra and its ordered basis}
Fix finitely many spectral labels $t_1,\ldots,t_m$.
The notation $t_1<\cdots<t_m$ denotes a chosen order of colors,
independent of any ordering of the coefficient field.  For copies $V_a$ of a finite-dimensional vector space,
put $c_{ab}=P R(t_a-t_b):V_a\otimes V_b\to V_b\otimes V_a$ for $a>b$,
where $P$ interchanges the tensor factors.  Work over a coefficient ring
in which these matrices are defined.  Impose only the descending exchange
relations of \eqref{cbc:exchange:algebra}, and impose no same-color relation.

\begin{theorem}[Confluence is Yang--Baxter]\label{thm:zf-diamond}
The ordered-word map \eqref{cbc:exchange:ordered-module} is an isomorphism
precisely when \eqref{cbc:exchange:spectral-ybe} holds on every three
distinct chosen labels.  This gives a basis for all words,
including repeated labels.  Reverse exchanges preserve this basis
precisely when their maps are the inverses of the descending exchanges.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A descending exchange lowers the color-inversion number.  The only
intersecting pair of possible exchanges has three strictly descending
colors.  Its two complete reductions are
\eqref{cbc:exchange:colored-braid}.  Removing their common permutation
gives Yang--Baxter.  The induction and the inverse normal-form map of
Theorem~\ref{cbc:exchange:normal} apply to every word.

Reversing an ordered pair and sorting it gives $c_{ab}c_{ba}$,
which must be the identity on the independent ordered quadratic tensors.
These are square matrices over a commutative ring, so the prescribed maps
are inverse in both directions.
\end{proof}

For $R(u)=1-\hbar P/u$, Proposition~\ref{cbc:exchange:rational-result}
proves Yang--Baxter and computes the reverse product $1-\hbar^2/u^2$.
Thus the raw matrix gives the oriented algebra.  The matrix
$\widehat R(u)=(u-\hbar P)/(u-\hbar)$ gives reversible exchanges over the
specified localization.  Their oriented algebras are related by the
explicit color-count twist \eqref{cbc:exchange:twist}.
These are statements about ordinary algebras with spectral coefficients.
Collision extensions and state-field maps require further constructions.

\section{The scalar algebra and its linear resolution}
Let $K$ be a field and fix units $q_{ab}\in K^\times$ for $1\leq a<b\leq m$.
The scalar exchange algebra is the augmented graded algebra
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:zf:scalar}
 A=K\langle z_1,\ldots,z_m\rangle/
       (z_bz_a-q_{ab}z_az_b:a<b),\qquad
 \epsilon(z_a)=0,\quad \deg z_a=1.
\end{equation}
Scalar exchanges satisfy the three-color equation because their
coefficients commute.  Hence the monomials
$z^\alpha=z_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots z_m^{\alpha_m}$ form a $K$-basis, and
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:zf:product}
 z^\alpha z^\beta
 =Q(\alpha,\beta)z^{\alpha+\beta},\qquad
 Q(\alpha,\beta)=\prod_{a<b}q_{ab}^{\alpha_b\beta_a}.
\end{equation}
Each factor counts crossings of a color~$b$ from the first monomial
with a color~$a$ from the second.

For $I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_n\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$, introduce a symbol
$e_I$ of homological degree and internal degree~$n$.
Set $e_\varnothing=1$ and define free left $A$-modules and an $A$-linear map
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:zf:koszul}
 \begin{split}
 L_n&=\bigoplus_{|I|=n} A e_I,\\
 d(e_I)&=\sum_{r=1}^n(-1)^{r-1}
       \left(\prod_{s<r}q_{i_s i_r}^{-1}\right)
       z_{i_r}e_{I\setminus\{i_r\}}.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
For two labels, this reads
$d(e_{\{a,b\}})=z_a e_b-q_{ab}^{-1}z_b e_a$.
Its second differential is zero by the defining relation.

\begin{proposition}\label{cbc:zf:resolution}
The complex $L_\bullet\xrightarrow{\epsilon}K$ is a minimal graded free
resolution of the trivial left $A$-module.  In particular, $A$ is Koszul:
its trivial module has a resolution whose degree-$n$ generators all have
internal degree~$n$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any two removed indices, the two terms of $d^2$ have opposite signs.
After removing the common unit factor, their coefficient is
$z_a z_b-q_{ab}^{-1}z_b z_a$ for $a<b$.  Thus $d^2=0$.

Give $e_I$ the multidegree $\mathbf1_I$, the indicator vector of~$I$.
For a fixed multidegree $\nu\in\mathbb N^m$, the complex has basis
\[
 v_I=z^{\nu-\mathbf1_I}e_I,
 \qquad I\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\nu).
\]
Every coefficient $\lambda_{I,i}$ of $v_{I\setminus\{i\}}$ in $d(v_I)$
is a unit: explicitly, for $i=i_r\in I$ it is
\[
 \lambda_{I,i}=(-1)^{r-1}
       \prod_{s<r}q_{i_s i}^{-1}
       \prod_{b>i}q_{ib}^{\nu_b-\mathbf1_I(b)}.
\]

If $\nu\ne0$, fix $j\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)$ and put
\[
 H(v_I)=
 \begin{cases}
  \lambda_{I\cup\{j\},j}^{-1}v_{I\cup\{j\}},&j\notin I,\\
  0,&j\in I.
 \end{cases}
\]
In $dH+Hd$, removing the added index~$j$ contributes $v_I$.
The terms involving any other index cancel by the corresponding two-face
identity in $d^2=0$.  If $j\in I$, only the term removing and then
reintroducing~$j$ survives.  Thus $dH+Hd=1$ in every positive multidegree.

Multidegree zero consists of $K e_\varnothing$, on which the augmentation
is an isomorphism.  This proves exactness.  Finally, every differential
coefficient lies in the augmentation ideal, so the resolution is minimal.
\end{proof}

\section{The actual bar coalgebra}
Use homological bar degree: $[a_1|\cdots|a_n]\in B_nA$ has degree~$n$,
where $B_nA=(\ker\epsilon)^{\otimes_K n}$ and $B_0A=K$.
The normalized bar differential and coproduct are
\[
 \begin{split}
 b[a_1|\cdots|a_n]
   &=\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{r-1}
       [a_1|\cdots|a_ra_{r+1}|\cdots|a_n],\\
 \Delta[a_1|\cdots|a_n]
   &=\sum_{r=0}^n[a_1|\cdots|a_r]\otimes[a_{r+1}|\cdots|a_n].
 \end{split}
\]
An empty bar is the coaugmentation~$1$.
For an ordered subset $I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_n\}$ define
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:zf:bar-cycles}
 \omega_I=\sum_{\pi\in S_n}(-1)^{\ell(\pi)}
   \left(\prod_{r<s,\ \pi(r)>\pi(s)}
                     q_{i_{\pi(s)}i_{\pi(r)}}^{-1}\right)
     [z_{i_{\pi(1)}}|\cdots|z_{i_{\pi(n)}}],
 \qquad \omega_\varnothing=1,
\end{equation}
where $\ell(\pi)$ is the number of inversions of the permutation~$\pi$.
For example, $\omega_{\{a,b\}}=[z_a|z_b]-q_{ab}^{-1}[z_b|z_a]$.
Let $C_n$ be the span of the $\omega_I$ with $|I|=n$.

\begin{theorem}\label{cbc:zf:bar-model}
The spaces $C_n$ form a differential graded subcoalgebra
$C\subseteq BA$ with zero differential.  The inclusion is a
quasi-isomorphism.  Its coproduct is
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:zf:coproduct}
 \Delta\omega_I=\sum_{J\sqcup H=I}
   \left(\prod_{j\in J,\ h\in H,\ j>h}(-q_{hj}^{-1})\right)
            \omega_J\otimes\omega_H.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\operatorname{Tor}^A_n(K,K)$ has one basis element for each
$n$-element subset of labels, and dimension $\binom mn$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pair the terms of $b\omega_I$ obtained from permutations differing by
one adjacent transposition.  Their weights differ by $-q_{ab}^{-1}$,
while their adjacent products differ by $q_{ab}$, so they cancel.
For a fixed cut of a bar word, its inversions split into those internal
to each side and those crossing the cut.  The crossing inversions depend
only on the two subsets of labels.  Factoring their weights gives
\eqref{cbc:zf:coproduct}.  The coefficient of the increasing bar word
in $\omega_I$ is~$1$, so these elements are linearly independent.

For the quasi-isomorphism, use the normalized free left bar resolution
$P_n=A\otimes_K B_nA$ with differential
\[
 d_P(a_0\otimes[a_1|\cdots|a_n])
 =-a_0a_1\otimes[a_2|\cdots|a_n]
   +a_0\otimes b[a_1|\cdots|a_n].
\]
It is exact: writing $\bar a_0=a_0-\epsilon(a_0)$, the map
$-1\otimes[\bar a_0|a_1|\cdots|a_n]$, together with the unit in
augmentation degree, is a contracting homotopy over~$K$.

Grouping \eqref{cbc:zf:bar-cycles} by its first entry shows that
\[
 L_n\longrightarrow P_n,\qquad
 e_I\longmapsto(-1)^n(1\otimes\omega_I)
\]
is a chain map lifting the identity of~$K$.
The sign compensates for the negative first face of~$d_P$.

Both complexes are free resolutions.  A reverse comparison map and
homotopies are constructed degree by degree, lifting cycles through the
surjections onto boundaries.  Hence this map is a
chain homotopy equivalence.  Tensoring with the right augmentation
module~$K$ gives a quasi-isomorphism with image~$C$ in~$BA$.
The factors $(-1)^n$ are invertible in each degree, so the inclusion
$C\hookrightarrow BA$ itself is a quasi-isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\section{The Yoneda product and its variance}
Applying $\operatorname{Hom}_A(-,K)$ to~$L$ kills the differential.
Let $\xi_i\in\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(K,K)$ be dual to~$e_{\{i\}}$.
Products in Ext require lifts on the resolution.  Define the $A$-linear
map $F_i:L_n\to L_{n-1}$ by zero when $i\notin I$, and by
\[
 F_i(e_I)=(-1)^{r-1}
       \left(\prod_{j\in I,\ j>i}q_{ij}^{-1}\right)
       e_{I\setminus\{i\}},\qquad i=i_r\in I.
\]

For a removal of any $j\ne i$, the two orders of removal give opposite
coefficients.  Thus $dF_i+F_id=0$.  Composing $F_i:L_1\to L_0$ with the
augmentation gives the cochain~$\xi_i$.

With Yoneda multiplication represented by composition, the same
coefficients give
\[
 F_i^2=0,\qquad F_bF_a=-q_{ab}^{-1}F_aF_b\quad(a<b).
\]
For each subset $I$, the corresponding ordered product has a nonzero
coefficient on~$e_I$.  It vanishes on all other subsets of the same size.  These products therefore give all the dual basis
classes.  We obtain the algebra presentation
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:zf:yoneda}
 \operatorname{Ext}^{*}_A(K,K)
 =K\langle\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m\rangle/
       (\xi_i^2,\ \xi_b\xi_a+q_{ab}^{-1}\xi_a\xi_b:a<b),
 \qquad |\xi_i|=1.
\end{equation}

The bar model~$C$ is a coalgebra, whereas
\eqref{cbc:zf:yoneda} is an algebra.  Their comparison uses graded duality:
bar homological degree~$n$ is cohomological degree~$-n$, and its dual
has degree~$n$.  Convolution uses the Koszul tensor sign.  For degree-one
functionals $f,g$, $(f\otimes g)([z_a]\otimes[z_b])=-f([z_a])g([z_b])$.
Formula~\eqref{cbc:zf:coproduct} then gives exactly the relations in
\eqref{cbc:zf:yoneda}.  Under the displayed resolution comparison,
$\xi_i$ corresponds to minus the functional dual to~$\omega_{\{i\}}$.

Thus the subset basis, its coproduct, and the dual Yoneda product are all
specified.  This computes ordinary associative Koszul duality for the
scalar exchange algebra.  Extending the calculation to associative
chiral Koszul duality still requires geometric coefficient objects and
maps preserving the collision and substitution operations.

\chapter{RTT Yangians and the bar filtration}\label{chap:yangian}

The two Yangian filtrations answer different questions.  Positive
weight gives a polynomial associated graded and proves flatness.
Loop degree gives a current enveloping algebra and controls the bar
spectral sequence.  Over the polynomial deformation ring, the current
bracket retains a factor of $\hbar$.  Its homology detects that factor
before any higher differential is considered.

\section{The augmented coefficient algebra}

Fix $B=\mathbb C[\hbar]$ and $n\geq1$.  Let
$A=Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{gl}_n)$ be the coefficient algebra of
\eqref{cbc:rtt:cleared}, with unscaled generators $t_{ij}^{(r)}$ and
augmentation $\varepsilon(t_{ij}^{(r)})=0$ for $r\geq1$.
Theorem~\ref{cbc:rtt:hopf} gives its coproduct in the ordinary tensor
product $A\otimes_B A$ and its polynomial antipode coefficients.
Its ordered PBW monomials form a $B$-basis.

For $n=2$ the central group-like series is
\[
 D(u)=t_{11}(u)t_{22}(u-\hbar)-t_{21}(u)t_{12}(u-\hbar).
\]
The determinant-one quotient and its central tensor factor are those of
Proposition~\ref{cbc:rtt:qdet}.  They require this column order.

Put $L=\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb C)[z]$ and
$L_B=L\otimes_{\mathbb C}B$.  Write $L_\hbar$ for this $B$-module with
bracket $[x,y]_\hbar=\hbar[x,y]$.
Give $t_{ij}^{(r)}$ loop degree $r-1$ and give $B$ degree zero.
The resulting increasing filtration $F$ has
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:yangian:current}
 \operatorname{gr}_F A\cong U_B(L_\hbar),\qquad
 \overline t_{ij}^{(r)}\longleftrightarrow e_{ij}z^{r-1},
\end{equation}
by Theorem~\ref{cbc:rtt:pbw}.  The scalar counit on both sides makes
this an isomorphism of augmented algebras.

\section{The current bar differential}

Let $\overline A=\ker\varepsilon$.  Use the normalized relative bar
complex over $B$, in homological degrees:
\[
 C_q=\mathrm B_B(A)_q=\overline A^{\otimes_B q},\qquad C_0=B,
\]
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:yangian:bar-differential}
 b[a_1|\cdots|a_q]
 =\sum_{i=1}^{q-1}(-1)^{i-1}
       [a_1|\cdots|a_i a_{i+1}|\cdots|a_q].
\end{equation}
The empty word has degree zero.  The internal algebra differential is
zero.  Since $A$ and $\overline A$ are $B$-free, the normalized left bar
resolution is a free $A$-resolution of the augmentation module $B$.
Consequently $H_q(C)=\operatorname{Tor}^A_q(B,B)$.

The current analogue has a smaller complex.  On
$\Lambda_B^qL_B$ define the Chevalley differential by
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:yangian:chevalley}
 \partial(x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge x_q)
 =\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j+1}[x_i,x_j]\wedge
 x_1\wedge\cdots\widehat{x_i}\cdots\widehat{x_j}\cdots\wedge x_q.
\end{equation}
The entries remaining after the bracket keep their original order.
In particular, $\partial(x\wedge y)=[x,y]$.
Jacobi gives $\partial^2=0$.
The bracket of $L_\hbar$ therefore gives the complex
$K_\hbar=(\Lambda_B^\bullet L_B,\hbar\partial)$.

\begin{proposition}[Current bar comparison]\label{cbc:yangian:ce-bar}
The antisymmetrizer
\[
 \operatorname{Alt}(x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge x_q)
 =\sum_{\sigma\in S_q}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
       [x_{\sigma(1)}|\cdots|x_{\sigma(q)}]
\]
is a quasi-isomorphism from $K_\hbar$ to
$\mathrm B_B U_B(L_\hbar)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Pair the permutations in the bar differential that differ by
interchanging the two adjacent entries being multiplied.  Their products
differ by $xy-yx=\hbar[x,y]$.  Moving this bracket to the first wedge
position gives the sign in \eqref{cbc:yangian:chevalley}.  Hence
$b\operatorname{Alt}=\operatorname{Alt}\,\hbar\partial$.

Filter the enveloping algebra by PBW length and its bar by total PBW
length.  Filter $\Lambda_B^qL_B$ by assigning weight $q$ to its elements.
Both differentials preserve the increasing nonnegative filtrations,
and $\hbar\partial$ lowers weight by one.  On associated gradeds the
map is the antisymmetrizer from the exterior module, with zero
differential, to the bar of $\operatorname{Sym}_B(L_B)$.

For finitely many basis vectors this is the Koszul bar comparison:
the polynomial Koszul resolution is exact by successive contraction
in each variable.  For the left bar resolution with negative first face,
the comparison in degree $q$ is $(-1)^q(1\otimes\operatorname{Alt})$.
After tensoring with $B$, the exterior differential
is zero, so multiplication by this degree sign does not affect the
quasi-isomorphism assertion for $\operatorname{Alt}$.
Every polynomial and bar word involves finitely many basis vectors.
Taking their filtered union proves the same statement for $L_B$.

The mapping cone of $\operatorname{Alt}$ thus has acyclic associated
graded.  A cone cycle has a finite largest filtration degree.  Exactness
in that degree subtracts a boundary and lowers the degree of the cycle.
Repeating finitely many times makes it zero.  The cone is acyclic,
which proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

Already in degree one,
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:yangian:first-homology}
 H_1(K_\hbar)=L_B/\hbar[L_B,L_B].
\end{equation}
For $n=2$, the decomposition into scalar and traceless matrices gives
\[
 H_1(K_\hbar)
 \cong (\mathbb C I[z]\otimes_{\mathbb C}B)
       \oplus(\mathfrak{sl}_2[z]\otimes_{\mathbb C}B/(\hbar)).
\]
Indeed $[L,L]=\mathfrak{sl}_2[z]$: traces of commutators vanish,
and brackets with constant matrix units generate each traceless
current.  Thus the traceless summand is genuine $\hbar$-torsion.
For example,
\[
 b\bigl([e_{11}|e_{12}]-[e_{12}|e_{11}]\bigr)
       =\hbar[e_{12}].
\]
Replacing $\hbar\partial$ by $\partial$ in the same comparison
would leave the nonzero residual $(\hbar-1)[e_{12}]$.

For $n=2$, this torsion also occurs in the Yangian's actual bar homology.
The first-mode inclusion
$U_\hbar(\mathfrak{gl}_2)\longrightarrow A$ and
$\operatorname{ev}_0$ are augmentation-preserving maps whose composite
is the identity.  Applying the relative bar and then homology gives
a split injection on $\operatorname{Tor}$.  The current-bar proof also
applies to the constant Lie algebra.  Its degree-one class of $e_{12}$
is nonzero and killed by $\hbar$.  Its image in
$\operatorname{Tor}^A_1(B,B)$ has the same property.

\section{The algebraic spectral sequence}

Filter $C_q$ by the sum of the loop degrees of its PBW entries.
The differential preserves this filtration, $F_{-1}C=0$, and every
chain lies in some $F_pC$.  The PBW basis also identifies the associated
graded tensors without a flatness ambiguity.

\begin{theorem}[Yangian bar spectral sequence]\label{thm:yangian-bar-ss}
There is a spectral sequence of $B$-modules.  Its first two pages are
\[
 E^0_{p,q}=(\mathrm B_B U_B(L_\hbar))_{p+q,p},\qquad
 E^1_{p,q}=H_{p+q}(K_\hbar)_p.
\]
The last subscript on each right-hand side denotes total current degree.
The differential has bidegree $(-r,r-1)$.

Its limiting terms satisfy
\[
 E^\infty_{p,q}
 \cong\operatorname{gr}^F_p\operatorname{Tor}^A_{p+q}(B,B)
\]
for the exhaustive filtration induced by $F$ on homology.
Here the limit uses eventual cycles modulo the union of page boundaries.
No finite-page stabilization is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{cbc:yangian:current} identifies the associated graded
bar with the current bar.  Proposition~\ref{cbc:yangian:ce-bar}, which
preserves total current degree, identifies its homology and proves the
first-page formula.  The usual filtered-complex differential sends a
representative in $F_pC_{p+q}$ to its leading boundary in
$F_{p-r}C_{p+q-1}$, giving the stated bidegree.

The limiting assertion can be checked directly.  Inside
$\operatorname{gr}_p^F C_n$, put
\[
 Z_r^p=\frac{(F_pC_n\cap b^{-1}F_{p-r}C_{n-1})+F_{p-1}C_n}
                  {F_{p-1}C_n},\qquad
 B_r^p=\frac{(F_pC_n\cap b(F_{p+r-1}C_{n+1}))+F_{p-1}C_n}
                  {F_{p-1}C_n}.
\]
For $r\geq1$ the page is $Z_r^p/B_r^p$.

When $r>p$, $F_{p-r}=0$, so $Z_r^p$ consists of the leading terms of
actual cycles.  A boundary in $F_pC_n$ has a primitive in some finite
$F_mC_{n+1}$, and hence lies in $B_r^p$ for all sufficiently large $r$.
Conversely every $B_r^p$ is represented by such a boundary.
The quotient by their union is exactly the associated graded of
homology.  Its filtration is exhaustive because every cycle is a
finite chain.
\end{proof}

At $\hbar=1$, the same construction has the ordinary current
Chevalley homology as its first page.  Over $B$, the first page is
$H(K_\hbar)$, including the torsion in
\eqref{cbc:yangian:first-homology}.  These are separate coefficient
statements.  Specialization of homology need not commute with base change.

The displayed spectral sequence is one of modules.  For example, the
signed shuffle of the degree-one cycles $[e_{11}]$ and $[e_{12}]$
has the nonzero boundary computed above.  That shuffle therefore does
not define a product on this noncommutative bar complex.

Finite calculations can instead use the positive weight
$\deg t_{ij}^{(r)}=r$.  The bar subcomplex of total weight at most $D$
is finite free over $B$: only finitely many PBW monomials occur.
Bar length is at most $D$, and loop degree in length $q$ is at most $D-q$.
Its loop spectral sequence has a finite filtration.
A bound on current indices alone leaves arbitrarily long products
and bar words, and supplies no such finite filtration.

\section{Completion and the inverse limit}

Let $B_N=B/(\hbar^N)$, $A_N=A/(\hbar^N)$, and
$C^{(N)}=\mathrm B_{B_N}(A_N)$.  The continuous relative bar complex
of $\widehat A=\varprojlim_N A_N$ is, by definition,
\[
 \widehat C=\varprojlim_N C^{(N)}.
\]
Thus its tensors are completed by this specific inverse limit.
This construction distinguishes continuous bar homology from the
ordinary algebraic $\operatorname{Tor}$ of $\widehat A$.
PBW makes the transition maps on every chain group surjective.

For the induced transition $f_N:C^{(N+1)}\to C^{(N)}$, define
$\sigma((x_N)_N)=(f_N(x_{N+1}))_N$ on the product complex.
There is a short exact sequence of complexes
\[
 0\longrightarrow\widehat C\longrightarrow\prod_N C^{(N)}
\xrightarrow{\,1-\sigma\,}\prod_N C^{(N)}\longrightarrow0.
\]

Surjectivity of $1-\sigma$ follows by solving its equations successively
using the surjective chain transitions.  Products of modules are exact,
so product homology is the product of homologies.
For an inverse system of homology modules, let $\varprojlim^1$ denote
the cokernel of $1-\sigma$ on their product.
The long exact sequence consequently gives
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:yangian:milnor}
 0\longrightarrow\varprojlim\nolimits_N^1 H_{q+1}(C^{(N)})
 \longrightarrow H_q(\widehat C)
 \longrightarrow\varprojlim_N H_q(C^{(N)})\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The sequence states the exact inverse-limit obstruction.
It does not assume that this cokernel vanishes.

\section{The abelian calculation and its geometric scope}

For $n=1$, induction in \eqref{cbc:rtt:commutator} makes every
commutator zero: its right side consists of commutators of smaller
positive weight.  PBW then gives
\[
 Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{gl}_1)=B[t^{(1)},t^{(2)},\ldots],\qquad
 \operatorname{Tor}^{Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{gl}_1)}_*(B,B)
   =\Lambda_B(s t^{(1)},s t^{(2)},\ldots).
\]
The symbol $s t^{(r)}$ has homological degree one.  For the first $M$
variables the polynomial Koszul resolution gives the exterior algebra
on their $M$ degree-one classes, represented in the bar by alternating
tensors.  Taking the filtered union over $M$ proves the formula for
all coefficients.  If $t$ records homological degree, the Poincar\'e
polynomial for the first $M$ variables is $(1+t)^M$.

The same Koszul argument works over every $B_N$.  Its exterior homology
modules have surjective transitions, so the $\varprojlim^1$ term in
\eqref{cbc:yangian:milnor} vanishes.  The continuous bar homology is
the $\hbar$-adic completion of this exterior module: at each finite
precision an element has finite exterior-monomial support.

These computations concern the augmented associative coefficient
algebra.  The rational intertwiner
\eqref{cbc:rtt:intertwiner} also gives an actual exchange on tensor
products of evaluation modules.  An associative chiral realization
requires geometric coefficient objects with collision and substitution
maps.  Its bar must then be compared with the coefficient bar by a map
preserving these operations.
Formal matrix series and a module intertwiner alone do not provide
that reconstruction.

\chapter{The \(A_2\) Hall algebra in complete detail}\label{chap:hall}
The order of the factors in a Hall product records the order of quotients in
a flag.  Reversing that order changes the structure constants.  We make both
length-three flag counts explicit, then describe the correspondence that gives
associativity and the additional data required over a space of supports.

\section{Untwisted products and the two flag tables}
Let $k=\mathbb F_q$ and $Q:1\to2$.  Use the representations
$S_1=(k\to0)$, $S_2=(0\to k)$ and $M=(k\xrightarrow{1}k)$ of
Chapter~\ref{cbc:hall:main}.  For this section, juxtaposition means the
untwisted Hall product $*$: $[A]*[B]$ counts subobjects isomorphic to $B$
with quotient $A$.  Put $e_i=[S_i]$.  Directly from the possible ranks,
\[
 e_1e_2=[S_1\oplus S_2]+[M],\qquad
 e_2e_1=[S_1\oplus S_2],\qquad
 e_i^2=(q+1)[S_i^2].
\]
The last coefficient counts lines in $k^2$.

Set $C_0=S_1^2\oplus S_2$ and $C_1=S_1\oplus M$.  These are precisely
the rank-zero and rank-one classes of dimension $(2,1)$.  Reading successive
quotients from the top of a flag gives
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:hall:table21}
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &112&121&211\\ \hline
 C_0&q+1&q+1&q+1\\
 C_1&q+1&1&0.
\end{array}
\end{equation}
For the rank-one arrow, a flag of type $121$ singles out its kernel line.
A flag of type $211$ would require a subrepresentation $(k^2\to0)$, which
cannot occur.  In type $112$, every line in the source is allowed.

Thus
\begin{align*}
 e_1^2e_2&=(q+1)([C_0]+[C_1]),\\
 e_1e_2e_1&=(q+1)[C_0]+[C_1],\\
 e_2e_1^2&=(q+1)[C_0].
\end{align*}

For dimension $(1,2)$ put $D_0=S_1\oplus S_2^2$ and $D_1=M\oplus S_2$.
The companion table is
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:hall:table12}
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &221&212&122\\ \hline
 D_0&q+1&q+1&q+1\\
 D_1&0&1&q+1.
\end{array}
\end{equation}
For the rank-one arrow, type $221$ would end in a subobject $S_1$ and would
force the arrow to vanish.  Type $212$ forces the image line in the target.
Type $122$ allows every line in that target.  The zero arrow imposes no
condition in either table.

\begin{theorem}[Untwisted Hall--Serre relations]\label{thm:hall-serre}
The untwisted products satisfy
\begin{align*}
 e_1^2e_2-(q+1)e_1e_2e_1+qe_2e_1^2&=0,\\
 qe_2^2e_1-(q+1)e_2e_1e_2+e_1e_2^2&=0.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On $C_1$ the first coefficient is $(q+1)-(q+1)=0$.  On $C_0$ it is
$(q+1)-(q+1)^2+q(q+1)=0$.

On $D_1$ the second coefficient is $-(q+1)+(q+1)=0$.  On $D_0$ it is the
same scalar cancellation as before.
The rank classification exhausts both dimension vectors.
\end{proof}

For example, at $q=2$ the three products in dimension $(2,1)$ have coordinate
vectors $(3,3)$, $(3,1)$ and $(3,0)$ in the ordered basis $([C_0],[C_1])$.
Their Serre combination is $(3,3)-3(3,1)+2(3,0)=(0,0)$.

\section{The twist and divided powers}
Put $v=\sqrt q>0$ and work over $K_q=\mathbb Q(v)$.  With the Euler form
$\langle(a,b),(c,d)\rangle=ac+bd-ad$, use the twisted product
\[
 [A]\diamond[B]=v^{\langle\dim A,\dim B\rangle}[A]*[B].
\]
The total Euler exponents for $112,121,211$ are $-1,0,1$, while those for
$221,212,122$ are $1,0,-1$.  Multiplying the two untwisted relations by
$v^{-1}$ converts them into the two symmetric quantum Serre relations of
Theorem~\ref{cbc:hall:serre}.  The middle coefficient changes because
$v^{-1}(q+1)=v+v^{-1}$.

Now all powers use $\diamond$.  Set
$e_{12}=e_1\diamond e_2-v^{-1}e_2\diamond e_1=v^{-1}[M]$.
For an element $u$ define $u^{(m)}=u^m/[m]_v!$, with the nonzero symmetric
factorial defined in Chapter~\ref{cbc:hall:main}.  The full normalization is
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:hall:divided}
 e_2^{(a)}e_{12}^{(b)}e_1^{(c)}
 =v^{a(a-1)+b(b-1)+c(c-1)-b+ab+bc}
 [S_2^a\oplus M^b\oplus S_1^c].
\end{equation}
This follows from \eqref{cbc:hall:monomial}.

Each ordered monomial gives a
single class.  Together with the normal-form proof of
Proposition~\ref{cbc:hall:normal}, it identifies the whole twisted Hall
algebra with the specialization of the positive quantum group.

\section{Flags as correspondences}
Let $\mathcal R$ be the groupoid of finite $Q$-representations and
isomorphisms.  Let $\mathcal E$ be the groupoid of inclusions $W\subset C$,
with isomorphisms preserving the inclusion.  There is a span
\[
 \mathcal R\times\mathcal R
 \xleftarrow{\ \pi\ }\mathcal E\xrightarrow{\ \rho\ }\mathcal R,
 \qquad \pi(W\subset C)=(C/W,W),\quad \rho(W\subset C)=C.
\]
On finitely supported functions on isomorphism classes, its counting
convolution is the actual formula
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:hall:convolution}
 (f*g)(C)=\sum_{W\subset C}f(C/W)g(W).
\end{equation}
The sum is over subobjects of the fixed representation $C$, each with weight
one.  This convention reproduces $g_{A,B}^C$ when $f,g$ are the indicator
functions of $[A],[B]$.

Composing the span in either order gives the groupoid of flags
$0\subset W\subset U\subset C$.  On subobjects, the comparison sends a
subobject of $C/W$ to its inverse image in $C$.  Its inverse takes the
quotient by $W$.  The resulting bijection identifies the two expansions of
\eqref{cbc:hall:convolution}.

For longer products, every parenthesization
is a way of successively forgetting stages of one flag.  The comparison
maps agree under further forgetting.  In particular, the five bracketings
of four factors give the same map around the associativity pentagon.
The zero representation supplies the unit.  The Euler twist is compatible
with every such comparison because its total exponent is
$\sum_{i<j}\langle\alpha_i,\alpha_j\rangle$ for the ordered quotient
dimensions $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n$.

\section{Coefficients over a space of supports}
The flag construction suggests a geometric Hall algebra when representations
carry support on a curve $X$.  Such a construction starts with a coefficient
category, a tensor operation, and objects on the spaces parametrizing finite
collections of supports.  Disjoint unions of supports must give specified
factorization isomorphisms.  An extension correspondence must then define a
pull--push map in that category, with its shifts and orientation data fixed.
Multiplication requires base-change
identifications with the correspondences of longer flags.  These must be
coherent under every refinement and compatible with the zero-object unit.

If the coefficient category has an ordered chiral tensor, these
identifications must also commute with its collision and substitution maps.
Under these hypotheses, the flag comparisons give associativity and its
higher coherences, while disjoint-support restriction gives the prescribed
factorization.  The resulting multiplication is an algebra object for the
supplied ordered chiral tensor.  Suppose the coefficient objects already carry a second associative product.
The extension maps must preserve it and its interchange with chiral
multiplication to produce a doubly associative chiral object.

Constructing these coefficient objects and compatible maps is the remaining
geometric problem.  Recovering the finite-field constants requires a trace
or comparison map that carries the geometric pull--push operations to
\eqref{cbc:hall:convolution}, with the Euler normalization
\eqref{cbc:hall:twist}.  The flag counts determine the target of such a map.
Its construction and compatibility with collisions require the geometric data.

\chapter{Necklace Hamiltonians and the mixed trace algebra}\label{chap:trace}
Matrix traces give Hamiltonian functions on matrix spaces and their
commuting quotients.  Their vector fields also act on the complex imposing
the commuting equation.  We retain the rank constant and distinguish
algebraic conjugation invariants from an infinitesimal BRST complex.

\section{The derived commuting equation}
Fix $N\geq1$ and use the polynomial coordinate ring
$O_N=\mathbb C[X_{ij},Y_{ij}]$ of Chapter~\ref{cbc:neck:main}.  Put
$\mu=[X,Y]$ and let $I_N\subset O_N$ be the ideal generated by its entries.
The Koszul differential graded algebra of this equation is
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:neck:koszul}
 K_N=O_N\otimes\Lambda(\psi_{ij}),\qquad
 |\psi_{ij}|=-1,\qquad dX=dY=0,\qquad d\psi=\mu.
\end{equation}
We use cohomological degrees, so $d$ has degree one.  All matrix entries
supercommute with the signs dictated by their degrees.  The equality $d^2=0$
follows from $d\mu=0$.

This algebra is the derived zero fiber of the map $(X,Y)\mapsto[X,Y]$.
It retains relations among the equations, without a regular-sequence
assumption.  For example, at $N=1$ the equation vanishes identically and
$K_1=\mathbb C[X,Y]\otimes\Lambda(\psi)$ has zero differential.

The group $G=\mathrm{GL}_N$ acts on all three matrices by conjugation, and
$d$ is equivariant.  The algebraic quotient has the complex $K_N^G$ of
equivariant global functions.  Over $\mathbb C$, invariants under this
reductive group are exact, so
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:neck:quotient-h0}
 H^0(K_N^G)=(O_N/I_N)^G.
\end{equation}
Here the quotient means the algebraic group quotient of the derived zero
fiber.

\section{An infinitesimal BRST complex}
Introduce a second odd matrix $c$ of degree one and define
$B_N=K_N\otimes\Lambda(c_{ij})$.  Its degree-one derivation $Q$ is specified by
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:neck:brst}
 QX=[c,X],\qquad QY=[c,Y],\qquad
 Q\psi=\mu+c\psi+\psi c,\qquad Qc=c^2.
\end{equation}
The sum $c\psi+\psi c$ is the graded commutator of two odd matrices.

\begin{proposition}\label{cbc:neck:brst-square}
The derivation $Q$ squares to zero.  The inclusion $K_N^G\to B_N$ is a chain
map, but need not be a quasi-isomorphism.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Leibniz gives $Q\mu=[c,\mu]$.  For any homogeneous matrix $Z$, the graded
Jacobi identity gives $[c,[c,Z]]=\tfrac12[[c,c],Z]=[c^2,Z]$.
Consequently
\[
 Q^2X=[Qc-c^2,X]=0,\qquad Q^2Y=[Qc-c^2,Y]=0,
 \qquad Q^2c=c^2c-cc^2=0.
\]
On $\psi$, the remaining terms are
\[
 Q^2\psi=[c,\mu]+[Qc,\psi]-[c,\mu]-[c,[c,\psi]]=0.
\]
The conjugation part of $Q$ annihilates $G$-invariant elements of $K_N$, so
its restriction there is $d$.

At $N=1$, conjugation is trivial and both differentials vanish.  Thus
$K_1^G=\mathbb C[X,Y]\otimes\Lambda(\psi)$, whereas
$B_1=K_1^G\otimes\Lambda(c)$.  The latter has a nonzero degree-one class $c$,
and the former has no degree-one cohomology.  The inclusion is therefore
not a quasi-isomorphism.
\end{proof}

The complex $B_N$ describes the infinitesimal conjugation action in these
BRST conventions.  Equation~\eqref{cbc:neck:quotient-h0} refers to algebraic
$G$-invariants.  The rank-one example fixes the distinction between the two
constructions.

\section{The necklace action on the complexes}
Use $F_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ and the cyclic derivatives from
Chapter~\ref{cbc:neck:main}.  Extend the derivation associated to
$f\in F_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ by
\[
 \delta_fX=-D_yf(X,Y),\qquad \delta_fY=D_xf(X,Y),
 \qquad \delta_f\psi=\delta_fc=0.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Necklace Lie algebra and trace action]\label{thm:necklace}
The necklace Lie algebra $F_{\mathrm{cyc}}/\mathbb C[1]$ acts by degree-zero
differential graded derivations on $K_N$, $K_N^G$ and $B_N$.  Its action obeys
\[
 [\delta_f,\delta_g]=\delta_{\{f,g\}_{\mathrm{neck}}},
 \qquad \delta_fJ_N(g)=J_N(\{f,g\}_{\mathrm{neck}}).
\]
Every $J_N(g)$ is a degree-zero cycle.  The trace map itself is defined on
$F_{\mathrm{cyc}}$, with $J_N([1])=N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Lie identity is \eqref{cbc:neck:commutator}.  The cyclic Euler identity
\eqref{cbc:neck:euler} gives $\delta_f\mu=0$, so $[d,\delta_f]=0$ on $K_N$.
Each polynomial matrix $D_xf(X,Y)$ or $D_yf(X,Y)$ is conjugation equivariant.
Thus the action preserves $K_N^G$ and commutes with the conjugation part of
$Q$, including its action on $\psi$ and $c$.

The trace formula follows from Theorem~\ref{cbc:neck:trace}, since
$\delta_f$ evaluates to $\{J_N(f),-\}$.  Moreover
$QJ_N(g)=\operatorname{Tr}[c,g(X,Y)]=0$, and $dJ_N(g)=0$.
Constants give zero derivations, while their traces retain the stated scalar.
\end{proof}

\section{The ordinary commuting quotient}
Give $O_N$ its canonical matrix Poisson bracket and set
$C_N=(O_N/I_N)^G$.  For $a\in\mathfrak{gl}_N$, the function
$\operatorname{Tr}(a\mu)$ generates conjugation, up to the choice of
infinitesimal-action sign.  Hence its bracket with an invariant function is
zero.  The ideal $I_N$ is therefore stable under bracketing with invariants.
Exactness of invariants lets us represent every element of $C_N$ by an
invariant function in $O_N$, and this defines a Poisson bracket on $C_N$.

\begin{theorem}[Diagonal restriction]\label{cbc:neck:diagonal}
Restriction to diagonal matrices gives an isomorphism of Poisson algebras
\[
 C_N\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 R_N=\mathbb C[x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_N,y_N]^{S_N}.
\]
It sends $\operatorname{Tr}(X^aY^b)$ to $p_{ab}^{(N)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The algebra isomorphism is the characteristic-zero restriction theorem for
the commuting scheme \cite[Theorem~3]{VaccarinoCommuting}.  Its hypotheses
allow the scheme-theoretic ideal generated by all entries of $[X,Y]$.
No reducedness assumption on that scheme is needed.

The orbit-sum argument below shows that the positive-degree power sums
generate $R_N$.  Modulo $I_N$, cyclic derivatives of $x^ay^b$ become the ordinary
derivatives of the commutative monomial.  Theorem~\ref{cbc:neck:trace} therefore
identifies their trace brackets with
\eqref{cbc:neck:particle-bracket}.  This proves Poisson compatibility on a
generating set, and hence on the whole algebra.
\end{proof}

\section{The stable commutative algebra and its rank-dependent bracket}
For $N\geq2$, setting $(x_N,y_N)=(0,0)$ defines maps $R_N\to R_{N-1}$.
Let $(R_N)_D$ denote the homogeneous component of total polynomial degree
$D$.  Define the bounded-degree stable algebra
\[
 \Lambda_2=\bigoplus_{D\geq0}\varprojlim_N(R_N)_D.
\]
The compatible positive-degree power sums define elements $p_{ab}\in\Lambda_2$
for $a,b\geq0$, $a+b>0$.

\begin{proposition}[Stable generators]\label{cbc:neck:stable-generators}
The algebra $\Lambda_2$ is freely generated by these $p_{ab}$.  For every
$N$, their images generate $R_N$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a list of nonzero exponent pairs $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_\ell$, put
\[
 M_{\boldsymbol\alpha}^{(N)}
 =\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_\ell\ \mathrm{distinct}}
 \prod_{j=1}^{\ell}x_{i_j}^{(\alpha_j)_1}y_{i_j}^{(\alpha_j)_2}.
\]
Each orbit sum of monomials is one such expression divided by the factorials
of repeated exponent-pair multiplicities.  Thus these expressions span
$R_N$ over $\mathbb C$.

Let $\Pi_\ell$ be the set of partitions of $\{1,\ldots,\ell\}$.  Grouping
particle indices by equality and inverting this grouping gives
\begin{equation}\label{cbc:neck:orbit-inversion}
 M_{\boldsymbol\alpha}^{(N)}
 =\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_\ell}
 \prod_{B\in\pi}(-1)^{|B|-1}(|B|-1)!
 \prod_{B\in\pi}p_{\sum_{j\in B}\alpha_j}^{(N)}.
\end{equation}
To verify the coefficient, fix a partition specifying equal particle indices.
The sum over its refinements factors over its blocks.  For a block of size
$m$, the signed sum counts permutations by cycles, giving zero for $m>1$
and one for $m=1$.  This proves \eqref{cbc:neck:orbit-inversion} and finite-rank
generation.

In total degree $D$, every monomial uses at most $D$ nonzero particle
positions.  Its orbit-sum basis therefore stabilizes for $N\geq D$.
A product of power sums indexed by a multiset of exponent pairs has that
orbit sum as its term of greatest support size.  Its coefficient is the
product of the multiplicity factorials, and the remaining terms use fewer
positions.  This triangular change of basis is invertible in each degree.
It proves algebraic independence and the stated polynomial presentation.

This agrees with the characteristic-zero stable presentation in
\cite[Corollary~3.5]{VaccarinoMultisymmetric}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Trace brackets with a rank parameter]\label{thm:stable-trace}
Let $\mathcal P=\mathbb C[\nu,p_{ab}:a+b>0]$, with nonnegative indices and
$p_{00}=\nu$.  The formulas
\[
 \{\nu,p_{ab}\}=0,\qquad
 \{p_{ab},p_{cd}\}=(ad-bc)p_{a+c-1,b+d-1}
\]
define a Poisson algebra.  The maps $\pi_N$ of
\eqref{cbc:neck:rank-evaluation} are surjective Poisson maps, so their
finite-rank relation ideals are Poisson ideals.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
This is the symmetric Poisson algebra of the Lie algebra
$\mathbb C[x,y]$, as constructed in \eqref{cbc:neck:universal-rank}.
The one-particle bracket gives the formula, and different particles Poisson
commute.  Proposition~\ref{cbc:neck:stable-generators} supplies surjectivity.
The kernel of a Poisson homomorphism is a Poisson ideal.
\end{proof}

The range $0\leq a,b\leq3$, $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$ gives $15^3=3375$
ordered Jacobi expressions.  Each vanishes, with the resulting constant
label retained.  The proof above establishes Jacobi for all labels.

The stable commutative algebra $\Lambda_2$ also admits the Poisson bracket
obtained from $\mathcal P/(\nu)$.  Its two linear generators commute.
At rank $N$, their particle bracket is $N$.  Thus the usual zero-particle
restriction maps defining $\Lambda_2$ do not preserve the particle Poisson
brackets.  The family $\mathcal P$ specifies each rank separately.

\section{Two particles and the nilpotent cone}
For $N=2$, set
\[
 u=\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\quad w=\frac{y_1+y_2}{2},\qquad
 r=x_1-x_2,\quad s=y_1-y_2.
\]
Then $\{u,w\}=1/2$, $\{r,s\}=2$, and every cross bracket vanishes.
The transposition fixes $u,w$ and negates $r,s$, so there is a Poisson
splitting
\[
 R_2=\mathbb C[u,w]\otimes\mathbb C[r,s]^{\{\pm1\}}.
\]
This splits off the center-of-mass plane as a tensor factor.

Put $A=r^2/2$, $B=rs/2$, $C=s^2/2$.  Every invariant monomial has even
total degree.  If both exponents are even it is a monomial in $A,C$.
If both are odd it is $B$ times such a monomial.  Hence
\[
 \mathbb C[r,s]^{\{\pm1\}}
 =\mathbb C[A,B,C]/(B^2-AC).
\]
There are no further relations: reduction by $B^2=AC$ leaves distinct
monomials with $B$-exponent zero or one, whose images in $r,s$ are independent.

Differentiating with $\{r,s\}=2$ gives
\[
 \{A,B\}=2A,\qquad\{B,C\}=2C,\qquad\{A,C\}=4B.
\]
Thus $e=A/2$, $f=-C/2$, $h=-B$ satisfy the
$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ brackets and $h^2+4ef=0$ of
Chapter~\ref{cbc:neck:main}.  The gradient of $B^2-AC$ vanishes only at the
origin.  Away from the origin at least one displayed bracket is nonzero,
so the Poisson tensor has rank two on this smooth surface.

\section{The formal closed comparison}
The formal Hamiltonian Lie algebra is a different carrier.  Define
\[
 R=\mathbb C[[z_1,z_2]],\qquad
 \{f,g\}=\partial_{z_1}f\,\partial_{z_2}g
         -\partial_{z_2}f\,\partial_{z_1}g,
 \qquad\mathfrak h=R/\mathbb C.
\]
Use the $(z_1,z_2)$-adic topology.  The continuous dual
$\mathfrak h^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}}$ consists of finite-jet linear functionals.
The bracket is continuous.  For fixed $f$, bracketing lowers vanishing
order by at most one, so its coadjoint action preserves the continuous dual.

The shifted cotangent Lie algebra for this datum is
\[
 \mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\ltimes\mathfrak h^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}}[1].
\]
Here $\mathfrak h$ is in degree zero, the shifted dual is in degree $-1$,
and the dual summand is abelian.  The mixed bracket is the shifted
coadjoint action, $(\operatorname{ad}_f^*\lambda)(g)=-\lambda(\{f,g\})$.
This supplies the formal Lie datum used in Hamiltonian BF theory.

Abelianization gives a Lie map $F_{\mathrm{cyc}}\to\mathbb C[z_1,z_2]$,
because cyclic derivatives become ordinary derivatives.  Its kernel need
not act trivially by the strict derivations of the unreduced matrix complex.
For example, the cyclic class
\[
 f=[x^2y^2-xyxy]_{\mathrm{cyc}}
\]
abelianizes to zero, whereas
\[
 \delta_f(x)=[\mu,x],\qquad\delta_f(y)=[\mu,y].
\]
At $X=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ and $Y=E_{12}+E_{21}$, the second expression
is $\operatorname{diag}(2,-2)$.  Thus this strict action does not factor
through abelianization on that chain carrier.  A homotopy-coherent action
after reduction remains a separate possibility.

A full comparison with closed observables must specify their continuous
cochain complex and the boundary derived center.  It must construct a
chain map preserving the required operations and prove that its comparison
cone is acyclic.  The necklace action supplies the explicit matrix
Hamiltonians and their differential compatibility.  The formal closed
comparison, its completion and its higher operation coherences remain to be
constructed.

\chapter{Modules, orbifolds, and higher \(\En\) directions}

\section{Pointed bars and interfaces}
For a right module \(M_R\) and left module \(M_L\),
\[
 \Barop_A(M_R,M_L)=
 \bigl|[r]\mapsto M_R\tensorpt\widebar A^{\tensorpt r}\tensorpt M_L\bigr|
 \simeq M_R\otimes_A^{\mathbb L}M_L.
\]
A marked chiral insertion gives left and right coactions of \(\Barop(A)\) by cutting the interval on either side.  Interfaces are therefore bimodules and compose by relative tensor product; after Koszul transform, they compose by cotensor product.

\section{Crossed products}
Let a finite group \(G\) act by augmented automorphisms of \(A\).  Then
\[
 \Mod_{A\rtimes G}\simeq(\Mod_A)^G.
\]
The crossed-product bar is the diagonal totalization of the group bar and the \(A\)-bar.  In characteristic zero, averaging contracts positive group-homology rows.  The fixed algebra \(A^G\) and crossed product \(A\rtimes G\) remain different: the first describes invariant operators, the second equivariant modules and symmetry defects.  A Galois or freeness theorem is needed for Morita equivalence.

\section{Symmetric products and DMVV}
For a graded vector space \(V\) with character \(f(q)\),
\[
 \sum_{N\ge0}p^N\operatorname{ch}\Sym^N(V)
 =\exp\left(\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{p^r}{r}f(q^r)\right).
\]
For a suitable seed elliptic genus \(\sum c(m,\ell)q^my^\ell\), the symmetric-product orbifold trace is
\[
 \sum_{N\ge0}p^N\operatorname{Ell}_{\mathrm{orb}}(\operatorname{Sym}^NY)
 =\prod_{n\ge1,m\ge0,\ell\in\mathbb Z}
 (1-p^nq^my^\ell)^{-c(nm,\ell)}.
\]
The proof is cycle decomposition in symmetric groups.  This is a trace identity.  The orbifold operator algebra additionally requires twisted sectors, defect composition, and OPE maps.  It must not be identified with a bar complex from the product formula alone.

\section{Higher transverse dimensions}
Dunn additivity gives
\[
 \Alg_{\E_{n+1}}(\mathcal V)
 \simeq\Alg_{\Eone}(\Alg_{\E_n}(\mathcal V)).
\]
An \(\E_n\)-ordered chiral algebra has \(n\) locally constant transverse directions while retaining its chiral coefficient.  Its \(n\)-fold bar is factorisation homology of the pointed \(n\)-disk and produces an \(\E_n\)-coalgebra under suitable positivity conditions.  The derived centre is an \(\E_{n+1}\)-object.

Independently, one can form \(\E_n\)-associative chiral algebras for the chiral product.  The same theorem-of-two-bars warning persists: ``higher \(\En\)'' must specify which monoidal direction is being iterated.

\begin{nogobox}[title={No-go theorem 18: dimension loss does not reconstruct a quantum group}]
A list of graded dimensions, even if it matches a root multiplicity or partition function, contains no coproduct, antipode, associator, or \(R\)-matrix.  Tannaka reconstruction requires a tensor category and a fibre functor; Hall reconstruction requires extension correspondences and orientations.  Scalar agreement is a shadow, not the structured object.
\end{nogobox}
