**C01 — Finite-cochain base change.**  
Anchors: §17.2, Lemma 17.1; physical pp.85–86; required units 77–78. Dependency: ordinary diagonal bar resolution and the foundational brace/\(E_2\) comparison.

The question is whether the base changes used to detect ramification actually preserve the complete Hochschild centre. If the ordinary unital \(R\)-algebra \(A\) is finite projective over \(R\), then every
\[
C_R^n(A)=\operatorname{Hom}_R(A^{\otimes_R n},A)
\]
is finite projective, with one arity per cochain degree. Flat scalar extension therefore commutes termwise with these Homs, the differential, products, and insertions.

For \(S=R/(r_1,\dots,r_d)\), with a finite regular sequence, resolve \(S\) by
\[
K=R\otimes\Lambda(\epsilon_1,\dots,\epsilon_d),\quad
|\epsilon_i|=-1,\quad d\epsilon_i=r_i.
\]
The derived tensor is represented by \(K\otimes_R C_R^\bullet(A)\). Only \(d+1\) coefficient degrees occur. Tensoring the bounded acyclic cone of \(K\to S\) with each flat cochain module gives a bounded-direction double complex with acyclic columns; finite total diagonals prove that its total complex is acyclic. Its quotient is precisely \(C_S^\bullet(A\otimes_RS)\), with the same insertions. Successive one-element cones give the same proof.

Thus the base changes actually used in §17 are justified. The rejected route is an unrestricted interchange of tensor with arbitrary complete Hochschild totalizations. This proof does not establish that route, especially for internally graded inputs whose arities accumulate in one degree.

**C02 — Socle derivations and the exact finite-etale refinement locus.**  
Anchors: §§17.3–17.4, Lemma 17.2 and Theorem 17.3; physical pp.86–87; units 77–79. Dependencies: C01, Artinian decomposition, separability in characteristic zero, Nakayama, and the ordinary \(E_2\) bracket.

The precise statement is:
\[
A/R\text{ finite etale}
\Longleftrightarrow A\xrightarrow{\sim} Z_R(A)\text{ as }E_2
\Longleftrightarrow Z_R(A)\text{ admits an }E_\infty\text{ refinement over }R,
\]
for \(R\) regular noetherian, a \(\mathbf Q\)-algebra of finite Krull dimension, and \(A\) finite locally free commutative over \(R\). “Refinement” must restrict to the specified \(E_2\) structure.

Here is the deciding obstruction. Let \(B\) be a finite-dimensional commutative characteristic-zero field algebra with a nonreduced local factor. Its maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) is nonzero and nilpotent. The separable residue field \(L\) embeds into that factor: lift a primitive element, then apply finitely many Newton corrections, since the derivative of its separable polynomial is a unit and the error ideal becomes nilpotently small. Choose a nonzero \(L\)-linear map
\[
\phi:\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Soc}(B)\cap\mathfrak m.
\]
Both spaces are nonzero. Define \(D(\lambda+u)=\phi(\bar u)\). Products of two elements of \(\mathfrak m\) disappear in \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\), and \(\mathfrak m\) annihilates the image. Hence \(D\) is a nonzero derivation. Since \(B\) is commutative, inner derivations vanish, and
\[
[D,b]=D(b)\ne0
\]
is a nonzero Hochschild bracket for some \(b\).

An \(E_\infty\) refinement has zero induced degree-\(-1\) Gerstenhaber bracket. C01 permits passage to every residue-field fibre of \(A/R\), so every fibre must be separable. Then \(\Omega_{A/R}\) has zero fibres and is zero by Nakayama. With \(T=A\otimes_RA\) and multiplication kernel \(J\), this gives \(J/J^2=0\). A finitely generated idempotent ideal is generated by an idempotent: a determinant applied to relations among generators produces \(1-e\) annihilating \(J\), with \(e\in J\), whence \(e^2=e\) and \(J=Te\). Thus the diagonal is projective and \(A/R\) is finite etale. Conversely that projective diagonal has derived endomorphisms \(A\), and its commutative central action gives the stated \(E_2\) equivalence.

The smallest deciding example is \(B=k[x]/x^2\), \(D(x)=x\). It refutes a refinement from mere parity or bracket vanishing before derived specialization.

Disposition: proof reconstructed. Regularity is used in the fibre comparison and has not been removed. Novelty unverified.

**C14 — Characteristic-\(p\) matrix quotient and the ambient extension.**

\[
\Pi_0=\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}(-1)^nx^ny^n/n!
\]
projects the \(S\)-basis \(1,x,\dots,x^{p-1}\) onto \(1\). Hence
\[
E_{ij}=\frac{x^i}{j!}\Pi_0y^j
\]
satisfies \(E_{ij}(x^\ell)=\delta_{j\ell}x^i\). This proves surjectivity of
\(W/(y^p)\to\operatorname{End}_S(P)\). PBW gives both sides rank \(p^2\) over \(S\), and the determinant argument makes the map an isomorphism. Matrix units establish the equivalence with \(S\)-modules by \(\ker y\). The central action cannot be forgotten: \(k[x]/(x^p-a)\) has a one-dimensional vacuum with \(x^p\) acting as \(a\).

In the full \(W\)-category, the resolution by right multiplication by \(y\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Ext}_W^0(P,P)=S,\quad
\operatorname{Ext}_W^1(P,P)=Sx^{p-1},\quad
\operatorname{Ext}_W^{j\ge2}(P,P)=0.
\]
The derivative misses exactly exponents congruent to \(p-1\). In the matrix quotient \(P\) is projective.

On \(k[x]\otimes k[\epsilon]/\epsilon^2\), take \(y=\partial_x+\epsilon x^{p-1}\). An \(x\)-linear splitting requires \(h'=-x^{p-1}\), impossible. Moreover \([y^p,x]=0\), and \(y^p(1)=\epsilon(p-1)!=-\epsilon\); thus \(y^p=-\epsilon\) everywhere. This explicitly exhibits the ambient normal extension that the quotient category excludes.

**C16 — Integral Massey obstruction.**

**C18 — Divided powers, Taylor reconstruction, supported operators, and all Frobenius digits.**  
Anchors: §§21.3–21.5, Theorem 21.1; physical pp.102–104; units 92–95.

\[
\gamma_a\gamma_b=\binom{a+b}{a}\gamma_{a+b},\quad
\partial\gamma_n=\gamma_{n-1}.
\]
Pascal proves Leibniz. The complex
\[
\Gamma_R(Q)\otimes\Lambda(\theta),\quad d\gamma_n=\gamma_{n-1}\theta

\[
E=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j\gamma_jP^j.
\]
The relation \([P,\gamma_j]=\gamma_{j-1}\) telescopes to \(PE=0\).
For horizontal \(v\),
\(E\gamma_nv=\delta_{n0}v\), by the alternating binomial sum. Expanding gives \(\sum_n\gamma_nEP^n=1\). Thus
\[
\Gamma_R(Q)\otimes_R\ker P\longrightarrow N
\]
is an isomorphism with inverse \(w\mapsto\sum_n\gamma_n\otimes EP^nw\). Freeness and horizontal projection prove exactness over every \(R\). At nonunit level \(\lambda\), the module \(R/(\lambda)\) with all operators zero disproves the same reconstruction.

\[
P\mapsto x,\qquad\gamma_n\mapsto(-1)^n\partial^{[n]}
\]
identifies \(W_\Gamma\) with finite-order relative differential operators on \(R[x]\): successive prescribed values on \(1,x,\dots,x^m\) yield the Hasse expansion with unit triangular diagonal. The vacuum corresponds to \(R[x,x^{-1}]/R[x]\), via \(\gamma_n\mapsto x^{-n-1}\). Indeed
\((-1)^a\partial^{[a]}x^{-b-1}=\binom{a+b}{a}x^{-a-b-1}\).

\([P^p,\gamma_p]=1\), so this is not the ordinary Weyl \(p\)-centre. Set
\[
\sigma(P)=P^p,\quad \sigma(\gamma_j)=\gamma_{pj},\quad
X=\gamma_1,\quad D_0=P-\gamma_{p-1}P^p.

Consequently \(X^p=D_0^p=0\), \([D_0,X]=1\), and they give a first-digit matrix algebra commuting with \(\sigma(W_\Gamma)\). The identities
\(\gamma_{pj+i}=\gamma_{pj}X^i/i!\) and

**C19 — Scalar homotopy annihilators and the actual extension.**  
Anchor: §21.6; physical p.104; units 95–96.

Let \(R=\mathbf Z_p[z]\), \(a=b+r\), \(b,r\ge1\), \(n\ge1\), \(c=r(n-1)\), \(g=\min(b,c)\), and
\[
C_n:\quad R\xrightarrow{(z^{rn},-nz^a)^t}R^2
\xrightarrow{(nz^b,z^c)}R
\]
in degrees \(-1,0,1\). The composite is zero since \(a+c=b+rn\).

The first map is injective. If \(c\ge b\), the middle kernel is generated by \((z^{c-b},-n)\), and the image is \(z^a\) times that generator. If \(c<b\), it is generated by \((1,-nz^{b-c})\), with image \(z^{rn}\) times it. Thus
\[
H^0=R/(z^{r+g}),\qquad H^1=R/(nz^b,z^c).
\]
The scalar null-homotopy ideal is exactly \((z^{rn},nz^a)\). Necessity follows on degree \(-1\). Sufficiency follows from the two homotopies
\[
H_1^0(u,w)=u,\ H_1^1(1)=(0,z^r),\qquad
H_2^0(u,w)=-w,\ H_2^1(1)=(z^r,0):
\]
multiplication of these displayed matrices gives \(dH_1+H_1d=z^{rn}\mathrm{id}\) and \(dH_2+H_2d=nz^a\mathrm{id}\).

For \(p=2,a=3,b=1,r=2,n=2\), \(z^3\) kills both cohomology modules, but
\(z^3\notin(z^4,2z^3)\), so it is not null-homotopic.

For \(c>b\), the actual short exact sequence is
\[
0\to R/(n,z^{c-b})\xrightarrow{z^b}
R/(nz^b,z^c)\to R/(z^b)\to0.
\]
Its extension class is \(1\) in
\[
\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(R/(z^b),R/(n,z^{c-b}))
=R/(n,z^{\min(b,c-b)}).
\]
It is nonzero exactly when \(p\mid n\).

Disposition: the complete complex, homotopies, and extension reconstructed; no cohomology-annihilator shortcut survives.
