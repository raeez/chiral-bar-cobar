**C01 — Algebraic reconstruction and the completion distinction.** Anchors: Appendix A.4/A.6; Chapter 2, text `[45258,53796)`, physical pp.17–20.

For an augmented dg \(k\)-algebra \(A=k\oplus\bar A\), take the algebraic direct-sum constructions
\[
BA=T^c(s\bar A),\qquad \Omega C=T(s^{-1}\bar C).
\]
The algebraic adjunction is \(\Omega\dashv B\). The counit \(\Omega BA\to A\) is a quasi-isomorphism without a quadratic or positive-weight hypothesis.

Proof: filter \(\Omega BA\) increasingly by the number of original \(\bar A\)-letters. Internal differentials preserve this number; bar multiplication lowers it; cobar splitting preserves it. In letter number \(n\), the associated graded consists of the \(n\) letter factors interleaved with \(n-1\) copies of
\[
K=(k\alpha\xrightarrow{1}k\beta),\qquad |\alpha|=-1,\quad|\beta|=0.
\]
The bar/cobar Koszul signs agree with the interleaved tensor differential. Contract its first gap for \(n\ge2\), using the sign \((-1)^{|a_1|}\) when passing the first letter. The \(n=0,1\) pieces map identically to \(k,\bar A\). Every element lies in a finite increasing-filtration stage, so successive removal of the highest filtration component proves acyclicity of the counit cone. No infinite primitive is required.

For positive integral weight, every fixed-weight word has bounded length. The analogous decreasing-filtration argument proves the unit and preservation of weightwise quasi-isomorphisms; taking products over weight uses precisely that completed tensor convention.

The deciding counterexample to preservation of **arbitrary underlying coalgebra quasi-isomorphisms** is \(A=k\times k\), augmented by first projection. Its reduced idempotent \(e\) gives bar words \(e_n\), with differential \(e_n\mapsto e_{n-1}\) for even \(n\), zero for odd \(n\). Thus \(BA\to k\) is a quasi-isomorphism. Nevertheless
\[
H^0(\Omega BA)=k\times k,\qquad H^0(\Omega k)=k.
\]
This refutes preservation by algebraic cobar. It does not refute a different higher-categorical adjunction with a totalization cobar.

Primary-source comparison: Francis–Gaitsgory’s Proposition 4.1.2 assumes pro-nilpotence; Proposition 4.3.3 and Theorem 5.1.1 concern the corresponding Lie/commutative-coalgebra and chiral categories. Their hypotheses do not identify that cobar with the free tensor construction above. [Francis–Gaitsgory, §§4.1–5.1](https://sites.math.northwestern.edu/~jnkf/writ/chiral.pdf).

Disposition: independently supported; completion/carrier comparison remains a separate obligation.

**C02 — Species, units and factorization sums.** Anchors: Appendix A.2/A.4; Chapter 8, `[115817,121036)`, physical pp.42–44.

\[
(F*G)(I)=\bigoplus_{I=S\sqcup T}F(S)\otimes G(T),\qquad
(F\odot G)(I)=F(I)\otimes G(I),
\]
where decompositions are ordered and empty blocks are permitted. The Cauchy unit is supported at \(\varnothing\); the Hadamard unit is constant \(k\).

Expanding the interchange source gives two independent decompositions; the aligned target gives one. Inclusion and projection satisfy \(\pi\iota=1\), while \(\iota\pi\) kills mismatched decompositions. For singleton-supported \(F\),
\[
(F*F)(\{1,2\})=k^2,
\]
from the two nonempty ordered decompositions. Thus the opposite count is false.

For factorization objects \(F,G\), the two-point value of their pointwise direct sum has two summands. Tensoring the one-point sums has four, including \(F_1\otimes G_2\) and \(G_1\otimes F_2\). Taking both objects constantly one-dimensional already distinguishes dimensions \(2\) and \(4\). Hence pointwise sums do not generally preserve factorization.

The projection-direction interchange with both units forces positive-arity multiplication to vanish by applying it to \((I,\varnothing)\) against \((\varnothing,I)\); the Hadamard unit then forces the object itself to vanish there. This refutes those particular axioms, not all mixed structures.
