\subsection{Balancing and the two-sided bar complex}

Let $\kk$ be a field and let $A$ be an augmented associative
differential graded $\kk$-algebra.  We use homological degrees:
$d_A:A_j\to A_{j-1}$.  The unit has degree zero, and the augmentation
$\epsilon:A\to\kk$ is a unital morphism of differential graded algebras.
Put $\overline A=\ker\epsilon$.

Let $M$ be a unital right differential graded $A$-module and let $N$
be a unital left differential graded $A$-module.  Their actions have
degree zero and satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
 d_A(ab)&=d_A(a)b+(-1)^{|a|}a\,d_A(b),\\
 d_M(ma)&=d_M(m)a+(-1)^{|m|}m\,d_A(a),\\
 d_N(an)&=d_A(a)n+(-1)^{|a|}a\,d_N(n).
\end{aligned}
\]
All tensor products and direct sums below are algebraic.

A bilinear map on $M\times N$ descends to $M\otimes_A N$ precisely
when it identifies $(ma,n)$ with $(m,an)$.  Thus
\[
 M\otimes_A N
 =
 (M\otimes_\kk N)/
 \left\langle (ma)\otimes n-m\otimes(an)\right\rangle.
\]
No factors are interchanged in this relation, so it carries no
additional Koszul sign.  The differential on the quotient is
\[
 d(m\otimes_A n)
 =
 d_M(m)\otimes_A n+(-1)^{|m|}m\otimes_A d_N(n).
\]
The module identities make this differential well defined.

The bar complex retains the balancing relations as boundaries.
Define
\[
 (s\overline A)_j=\overline A_{j-1},
 \qquad
 d_{s\overline A}(sa)=-s(d_Aa),
 \qquad
 B(M,A,N)=
 \bigoplus_{q\geq0}
 M\otimes_\kk(s\overline A)^{\otimes q}\otimes_\kk N.
\]
Write
\[
 m[a_1|\cdots|a_q]n
 =
 m\otimes sa_1\otimes\cdots\otimes sa_q\otimes n,
 \qquad
 m[]n=m\otimes n.
\]
The total degree of this element is
\[
 |m|+|n|+\sum_{j=1}^{q}|a_j|+q.
\]
Thus bar length $q$ and total degree are distinct gradings.
For homogeneous inputs, put
\[
 \sigma_i=|m|+\sum_{j=1}^{i}(|a_j|+1),
 \qquad 0\leq i\leq q.
\]

Define $b=b_{\mathrm{int}}+b_{\mathrm{bar}}$ by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 b_{\mathrm{int}}\bigl(m[a_1|\cdots|a_q]n\bigr)
 ={}&
 d_M(m)[a_1|\cdots|a_q]n\\
 &+\sum_{i=1}^{q}(-1)^{\sigma_{i-1}+1}
 m[a_1|\cdots|d_Aa_i|\cdots|a_q]n\\
 &+(-1)^{\sigma_q}
 m[a_1|\cdots|a_q]d_N(n).
\end{aligned}
\]
For $q\geq1$, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 b_{\mathrm{bar}}\bigl(m[a_1|\cdots|a_q]n\bigr)
 ={}&
 (-1)^{|m|}(ma_1)[a_2|\cdots|a_q]n\\
 &+\sum_{i=1}^{q-1}(-1)^{\sigma_i}
 m[a_1|\cdots|a_i a_{i+1}|\cdots|a_q]n\\
 &+(-1)^{\sigma_{q-1}+1}
 m[a_1|\cdots|a_{q-1}](a_qn).
\end{aligned}
\]
Set $b_{\mathrm{bar}}=0$ on bar length zero.
Every summand of $b$ has total degree $-1$.

The first two bar lengths show how the signs encode balancing and
associativity:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 b_{\mathrm{bar}}(m[a]n)
 &=
 (-1)^{|m|}
 \bigl((ma)[]n-m[](an)\bigr),\\
 b_{\mathrm{bar}}(m[a|c]n)
 &=
 (-1)^{|m|}(ma)[c]n
 +(-1)^{|m|+|a|+1}m[ac]n\\
 &\qquad
 +(-1)^{|m|+|a|+2}m[a](cn).
\end{aligned}
\]
Applying the first formula to the second gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 b_{\mathrm{bar}}^2(m[a|c]n)
 =
 (-1)^{|a|}
 \bigl(
 ((ma)c-m(ac))\otimes n
 +m\otimes((ac)n-a(cn))
 \bigr)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The terms $(ma)\otimes(cn)$ cancel.  No commutativity assumption enters
this calculation.

For example, take $A=\kk\langle x,y,z\rangle$ with all three generators
odd and differential zero.  Give $M=N=A$ their regular right and left
actions.  Then
\[
 b_{\mathrm{bar}}(z[x|y]1)
 =
 -(zx)[y]1-z[xy]1+z[x]y.
\]
Its second boundary is
\[
 \bigl(-zxy\otimes1+zx\otimes y\bigr)
 +\bigl(zxy\otimes1-z\otimes xy\bigr)
 +\bigl(-zx\otimes y+z\otimes xy\bigr)=0.
\]
The ordered words remain unchanged throughout.

\begin{proposition}
The differential $b$ satisfies $b^2=0$.
The map
\[
 \pi:B(M,A,N)\longrightarrow M\otimes_A N,
 \qquad
 \pi(m[]n)=m\otimes_A n,
 \qquad
 \pi|_{q>0}=0,
\]
is a chain map.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Consider the unsuspended spaces
\[
 C_{q,t}=
 \bigl(M\otimes_\kk\overline A^{\otimes q}\otimes_\kk N\bigr)_t.
\]
Here $t$ is the sum of the unsuspended degrees.
Define $f_0$ by the right action on $M$, define $f_q$ by the left
action on $N$, and let $f_i$ multiply $a_i$ and $a_{i+1}$ for
$0<i<q$.  Each $f_i:C_q\to C_{q-1}$ preserves internal degree.
Put
\[
 \partial|_{C_q}=\sum_{i=0}^{q}(-1)^i f_i
 \quad(q>0),
 \qquad
 \partial|_{C_0}=0.
\]
Associativity and the module identities give
$f_i f_j=f_{j-1}f_i$ for $i<j$.
The corresponding terms in $\partial^2$ have opposite signs,
so $\partial^2=0$.

Let $d_{\mathrm{ten}}$ be the ordinary tensor differential on $C_q$.
The differential graded algebra and module identities say that
every $f_i$ is a chain map.  Hence
$\partial d_{\mathrm{ten}}=d_{\mathrm{ten}}\partial$.
On the direct-sum totalization, with total degree $q+t$, put
\[
 D|_{C_q}=\partial+(-1)^q d_{\mathrm{ten}}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 D^2
 =
 \partial^2
 +(-1)^q\partial d_{\mathrm{ten}}
 +(-1)^{q-1}d_{\mathrm{ten}}\partial
 +d_{\mathrm{ten}}^2
 =0.
\]

The degree-preserving isomorphism
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Phi\bigl(m[a_1|\cdots|a_q]n\bigr)
 &=
 (-1)^{\theta_q}
 m\otimes a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_q\otimes n,\\
 \theta_q
 &=
 q|m|+\sum_{j=1}^{q}(q-j)|a_j|,
\end{aligned}
\]
satisfies $\Phi b=D\Phi$.
For the first face, the change in $\theta_q$ is $|m|$.
For the $i$th interior face it is
$|m|+\sum_{j=1}^{i}|a_j|$.
For the last face it is
$|m|+\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}|a_j|$.
Adding the alternating face signs gives exactly
$b_{\mathrm{bar}}$.

Applying $d_M$, $d_A$ in position $i$, or $d_N$ changes
$\theta_q$ by $q$, $q-i$, or $0$, respectively.
Together with $(-1)^q d_{\mathrm{ten}}$, these changes give
$b_{\mathrm{int}}$.  Thus $b^2=\Phi^{-1}D^2\Phi=0$.

At bar length one, $\pi b_{\mathrm{bar}}=0$ is precisely the
balancing relation.  At greater lengths its image under $\pi$
vanishes by degree in bar length.  At length zero,
$b_{\mathrm{int}}$ induces the stated quotient differential.
Therefore $\pi$ is a chain map.
\end{proof}

Taking $M=N=\kk$, with the actions supplied by $\epsilon$, gives
the reduced bar complex
\[
 B(A)=B(\kk,A,\kk)
 =
 \bigoplus_{q\geq0}(s\overline A)^{\otimes q}.
\]
Its endpoint terms vanish because every $a_i$ belongs to
$\ker\epsilon$.

\begin{proposition}
Under the field hypotheses above, $B(M,A,N)$ represents
$M\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}N$ in the derived category of homological
$\kk$-complexes.  No boundedness assumption on $A$, $M$, or $N$
is required.

If all internal differentials vanish, then
\[
 H_q(B(M,A,N),b_{\mathrm{bar}})_t
 \cong
 \operatorname{Tor}^{A}_q(M,N)_t,
\]
where $q$ is bar length and $t$ is internal degree.
Here $\operatorname{Tor}$ is computed in the category of graded
$A$-modules with degree-zero morphisms.  In total degree $\ell$,
\[
 H_\ell(B(M,A,N),b)
 \cong
 \bigoplus_{q+t=\ell}\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_q(M,N)_t.
\]
In particular, if $A$, $M$, and $N$ are concentrated in degree zero,
then $H_q(B(M,A,N),b)=\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_q(M,N)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set $P=B(A,A,N)$, with the left $A$-action on its first factor.
Its differential satisfies the left module Leibniz identity.
The augmentation
\[
 p:P\longrightarrow N,
 \qquad
 p(a_0[]n)=a_0n,
 \qquad p|_{q>0}=0,
\]
is therefore a morphism of differential graded left $A$-modules.

Put $\overline a_0=a_0-\epsilon(a_0)1$.
The $\kk$-linear maps
\[
 \eta(n)=1[]n,
 \qquad
 H(a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_q]n)
 =
 1[\overline a_0|a_1|\cdots|a_q]n
\]
satisfy
\[
 p\eta=\id_N,
 \qquad
 bH+Hb=\id_P-\eta p.
\]
To verify the second identity, use the unsuspended coordinates
$\Phi$.  In positive bar length, all terms cancel except
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\overline a_0\otimes a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_q\otimes n\\
 &\quad+
 1\otimes
 \bigl(\overline{a_0a_1}-\overline a_0a_1\bigr)
 \otimes a_2\otimes\cdots\otimes a_q\otimes n.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since $\epsilon(a_1)=0$, this expression equals
$a_0\otimes a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_q\otimes n$.
In length zero, the remaining expression is
$a_0\otimes n-1\otimes a_0n$.
The unsuspended insertion commutes with $d_{\mathrm{ten}}$.
The factors $(-1)^q$ in $D$ therefore make its internal terms
cancel.  Transport through $\Phi$ gives the displayed $H$.
Thus $p$ is a quasi-isomorphism.

A differential graded left $A$-module $Q$ is called
$K$-flat if $X\otimes_A Q$ is acyclic for every acyclic
differential graded right $A$-module $X$.
Filter $P$ by bar length:
\[
 F_rP=
 \bigoplus_{0\leq q\leq r}
 A\otimes_\kk(s\overline A)^{\otimes q}\otimes_\kk N.
\]
These are subcomplexes, and their successive quotients are
\[
 F_rP/F_{r-1}P
 \cong
 A\otimes_\kk(s\overline A)^{\otimes r}\otimes_\kk N
\]
with the tensor differential.
For acyclic $X$, tensoring this quotient over $A$ gives
\[
 X\otimes_\kk(s\overline A)^{\otimes r}\otimes_\kk N.
\]
Every acyclic complex of vector spaces is contractible, so this
complex is acyclic.

The filtration splits as graded $A$-modules.
Induction on $r$ therefore proves that $X\otimes_A F_rP$ is acyclic.
Filtered unions of vector spaces are exact, so
$X\otimes_A P$ is acyclic.  This proves that $P$ is $K$-flat.

A quasi-isomorphic $K$-flat replacement computes the derived
tensor product.  The chain isomorphism
\[
 M\otimes_A P\longrightarrow B(M,A,N),
 \qquad
 m\otimes_A\bigl(a_0[a_1|\cdots|a_q]n\bigr)
 \longmapsto
 (ma_0)[a_1|\cdots|a_q]n
\]
proves the first assertion.

When all internal differentials vanish, the unsuspended
$C_q(A,A,N)$ form a free graded left $A$-resolution of $N$.
The same contraction proves its exactness.
Tensoring this resolution with $M$ gives $C_q(M,A,N)$.
The isomorphism $\Phi$ then proves the assertions about
$\operatorname{Tor}$ and the two gradings.
\end{proof}

The augmentation $\pi$ need not be a quasi-isomorphism.
For example, let $A=\kk[e]/(e^2)$ with $|e|=0$ and
$M=N=\kk$ via the augmentation.
Every bar differential vanishes, and
\[
 B_q(\kk,A,\kk)=\kk[e|\cdots|e],
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tor}^{A}_q(\kk,\kk)\cong\kk
 \quad(q\geq0).
\]
For ordinary modules, $\pi$ is a quasi-isomorphism precisely when
all positive $\operatorname{Tor}$ groups vanish.
