**C24 — Ramified pullback and the remembered structural map.** Anchor: §15.7, physical 78–79.

**C25 — Universal periodic root resolution; field-dependent cohomology.** Anchor: §16.1, Theorem 16.1, physical 80–81.

For \(A_m=k[t,x]/(x^m-t)\), put \(u=X-Y\), \(v=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}X^{m-1-i}Y^i\), and \(E=k[X,Y]/(uv)\). Cancellation of the monic polynomials \(u\) and \(v\) gives
\(\operatorname{ann}_E(u)=(v)\), \(\operatorname{ann}_E(v)=(u)\).
Thus the alternating \(u,v\) resolution is exact. This cancellation argument works even over an arbitrary commutative coefficient ring.

After diagonal Hom, differentials alternate \(0,mx^{m-1}\). Over a characteristic-zero field, \(A_m\simeq k[x]\) and multiplication by \(mx^{m-1}\) is injective. Thus
\[
HH_R^0(A_m)=A_m,\quad HH_R^{2r+1}(A_m)=0,\quad
HH_R^{2r+2}(A_m)=A_m/(mx^{m-1}).
\]
The degree-two shift map is periodicity, so its Yoneda class \(\beta\) gives
\(HH_R^*(A_m)=A_m[\beta]/(mx^{m-1}\beta)\).
The degree-\(-1\) bracket vanishes because all classes are even. This does not imply cochain formality.

**C26 — Carry cocycle normalization and the hidden homotopy.** Anchor: §16.2, physical 81.

In the basis \(1,x,\dots,x^{m-1}\), let \(\beta(x^i,x^j)=x^{i+j-m}\) for \(i+j\ge m\), otherwise zero. Then \(\mu_t=\mu_0+t\beta\). Polynomial associativity gives the cocycle and self-bracket identities. The periodic degree-two generator maps to
\(\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}[x^i|x]x^{m-1-i}\).
Its bar differential telescopes to \(v[x]\); the term \([x^m]=[t]\) vanishes in the normalized relative bar. Evaluation by \(\beta\) gives one, fixing the normalization.

For the \(R\)-linear unary cochain \(\delta(x^i)=ix^i\), a nonoverflowing product has no defect. On an overflowing pair, the output basis exponent is lower by exactly \(m\). Hence, on every basis input,
\[
b\delta=mt\beta.
\]
This identity is cochain data, not merely a torsion equality in cohomology.

**C27 — Special-fibre Gerstenhaber algebra.** Anchor: §16.3, physical 81–82.

For \(B_m=k[x]/x^m\), the periodic differential has kernel \((x)\) in odd degrees. The Euler derivation generates this module, and periodicity generates all positive even degrees:
\[
HH^*(B_m)=
k[x,\delta,\beta]/(x^m,x^{m-1}\delta,x^{m-1}\beta,\delta^2),
\quad|\delta|=1,\ |\beta|=2.
\]
The odd square vanishes because \(2\) is invertible. Direct insertion gives
\[
[\delta,x]=x,\qquad[\delta,\beta]=-m\beta,\qquad
[\beta,x]=[\beta,\beta]=[\delta,\delta]=0.
\]
For the second identity the Euler output exponent is \(m\) less than the sum of input exponents. Symmetry cancels the two insertions in \([\beta,x]\). The two nested carry terms agree: both are nonzero precisely for total exponent at least \(2m\). These formulas respect the relations, and the cohomological biderivation identity determines every remaining bracket.

**C28 — Proved derived base change and the refinement obstruction.** Anchor: §16.4, Theorem 16.2, physical 82–83.

Normalized cochains are finite free \(R\)-modules in each nonnegative arity. Multiplication by \(t\) is termwise injective; quotient cochains are exactly those of \(B_m\), with identical insertions. Resolve \(k=R/(t)\) by \(K=R[\eta]\), \(|\eta|=-1\), \(d\eta=t\), \(\eta^2=0\). C14 applies because \(K\) has two coefficient degrees. The map
\(K\otimes_R C_R(A_m)\to C_k(B_m)\)
is a quasi-isomorphism: its underlying comparison is the cone of injective multiplication by \(t\) to its quotient. It also preserves cup and brace operations. Therefore
\[
k\otimes_R^{\mathbb L}Z_R(A_m)\simeq Z_k(B_m)
\]
as \(E_2\)-algebras.

An \(R\)-linear \(E_\infty\) refinement would remain \(E_\infty\) after this extension. Its restricted \(E_2\) Gerstenhaber bracket on cohomology would vanish, contradicting \([\delta,x]=x\ne0\). Thus no such refinement exists. The conclusion excludes a commutative zero-bracket formality model; it does not exclude a cohomology module equipped with nontrivial higher operations.

**C29 — Connecting map.** Anchor: §16.5, physical 83.

**C30 — Entire two-root cochain algebra and every brace.** Anchor: §16.6, physical 83.

For \(m=2\), the one reduced letter gives \(F_n(x^n)=1\), \(E_n(x^n)=x\), each in actual degree \(n\). Interior bar products are scalars and vanish after normalization. The two exterior terms give
\[
bF_n=\begin{cases}2E_{n+1}&n\text{ odd}\\0&n\text{ even},\end{cases}
\quad
bE_n=\begin{cases}2tF_{n+1}&n\text{ odd}\\0&n\text{ even}.\end{cases}

Cup multiplication gives \(F_pF_q=F_{p+q}\), \(F_pE_q=E_{p+q}\), \(E_pE_q=tF_{p+q}\).
Thus the strict associative model is
\[
R[x]\otimes T(a)/(x^2-t),\quad |a|=1,\quad da=2xa^2.

Here \(a^2\ne0\), \(\delta=xa\), \(\beta=a^2\), and \(d\delta=2t\beta\).

and output of the same outer type in arity \(p+\sum q_j-r\). These are the expanded input positions in the ordinary insertion rule, including nullary inputs.

**C31 — Strict Gerstenhaber shortcut is impossible.** Anchor: §16.7, physical 84.

A strict \(R\)-linear Gerstenhaber biderivation retaining \(x^2=t\) and \([\delta,x]=x\) would imply
\([\delta,x^2]=2t\) but \([\delta,t]=0\), a contradiction over the specified field. The actual nonclosed cochain has defect

**C32 — Exact refinement locus of a finite family.** Anchor: owned §17.1, physical 85; complete supporting context §§17.2–17.4, physical 85–87, owner P006.
