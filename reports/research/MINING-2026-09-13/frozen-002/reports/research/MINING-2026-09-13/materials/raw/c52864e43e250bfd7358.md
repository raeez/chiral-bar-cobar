1. **C01 — Associative normalization and braces: survives under the displayed algebraic and completion hypotheses.**  
   VI §§36.1–36.4, with continuation in U180. Let \(R\) be a commutative characteristic-zero ring, \(A\) a unital \(R\)-algebra with split unit inclusion, and \(\bar A=A/R1\). The normalized cochains are
   \[
   \operatorname{Hom}_R(\bar A^{\otimes_R n},A)\subset
   \operatorname{Hom}_R(A^{\otimes_R n},A),
   \]
   with arity zero retained. For the two-sided simplicial bar module,
   \[
   P_n=(1-s_0d_1)\cdots(1-s_{n-1}d_n)
   \]
   acts rightmost first. The simplicial identities prove its image is
   \(\bigcap_{i=1}^n\ker d_i\), its kernel is the degenerate summand, and the resulting differential is \(d_0\).

   An independent deciding computation over \(A=\mathbb Q[e]/(e^2)\) at bar degree two used \(1\otimes e\otimes1\otimes1\). Correct ordering sends it to zero. Reversed ordering gives
   \[
   -1\otimes1\otimes e\otimes1+1\otimes1\otimes1\otimes e,
   \]
   whose \(d_1\) is nonzero. Thus the stated ordering is material.

   The filtered degeneracy contraction and degreewise projective splitting justify the cochain quasi-isomorphism for the stated free coefficient modules. Brace closure follows by locating an external unit either inside an inserted positive-arity cochain or in the outer cochain; either contribution vanishes. Nullary insertions consume no external input and preserve the argument. Continuous extension requires the stated degree-preserving contraction and specified completion. This does **not** establish vacuum normalization for vertex cochains in a sesquilinear, diagonal-quotiented coefficient module.

2. **C02 — Holomorphic two-form non-exactness: survives; the apparent sign/type defect is extraction loss.**  
   VI §36.5, U180–U181. On a compact complex surface without boundary, a nowhere-degenerate holomorphic \(2\)-form satisfies
   \[
   \omega^2=0,\qquad \int_X\omega\wedge\overline\omega\ne0.
   \]
   For \(\omega=f\,dz_1\wedge dz_2\), the latter integrand is
   \(4|f|^2\,dx_1\,dy_1\,dx_2\,dy_2\) in complex orientation. Both \(\omega\) and its conjugate are closed. If \(\omega=d\alpha\) for a global smooth complex \(1\)-form, Stokes gives a contradiction. The original PDF correctly places the conjugates. Compactness is essential; on \(\mathbb C^2\), \(dz_1\wedge dz_2=d(z_1dz_2)\).
