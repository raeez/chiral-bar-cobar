\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}
\label{ch:preface}
\markboth{Preface}{Preface}

A chiral algebra is governed by collisions.  Two points carry the
binary operation; three points carry its first coherence; the Ran
space assembles every finite collision into one global object.  This
volume studies the bar and cobar constructions in that geometry.

The order of construction matters.  One begins with a factorization
category and a chosen object, passes to an augmented algebra chart,
forms its complete conilpotent bar coalgebra, and then constructs the
derived centre.  A cyclic trace introduces loop contractions.  Stable
graphs and clutching enter at that stage.  Scalar modular quantities
appear after the trace.  This order fixes the type of every object and
the hypotheses of every comparison.

\section*{1.  The first collision}

Let \(U\subset X\) be a coordinate disc on a smooth curve, with
coordinate \(z\).  On the ordered configuration space
\(\operatorname{Conf}_3(U)\), set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pf1-eta}
 \eta_{ij}:=d\log(z_i-z_j).
\end{equation}
Here an affine coordinate chart, or equivalently its formal tangent
screen at the collision, represents the logarithmic normal form along
the diagonal.  The expression \(d\log(z_i-z_j)\) is its local
coordinate representative.  Over an overlap, the transition cocycle
compares these representatives.  At positive
genus the same logarithmic singularity is represented by the KZB/theta
propagator in genus one and by a prime-form representative in arbitrary
genus.  These replacement data belong to the global comparison
package; the calculation below takes place on the affine screen.

\begin{proposition}[The three-point Arnold relation;
\ClaimStatusProvedHere]
\label{prop:preface-arnold}
\textup{[Type signature: Open quadrant; logarithmic de Rham
presentation; Beilinson level \(2\); coordinate-disc hypothesis
\(H_{\mathrm{Arn}}\).]}
The logarithmic forms satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preface-arnold}
 \eta_{12}\wedge\eta_{23}
 +\eta_{23}\wedge\eta_{31}
 +\eta_{31}\wedge\eta_{12}=0
 \qquad\text{on }\operatorname{Conf}_3(U).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(x=z_1-z_2\) and \(y=z_2-z_3\).  Then
\[
 \eta_{12}=\frac{dx}{x},\qquad
 \eta_{23}=\frac{dy}{y},\qquad
 \eta_{31}=\frac{dx+dy}{x+y}.
\]
The coefficient of \(dx\wedge dy\) in the left-hand side of
\eqref{eq:preface-arnold} is
\[
 \frac1{xy}-\frac1{y(x+y)}-\frac1{x(x+y)}=0.
\]
\end{proof}

Arnold's calculation is geometric
\cite{Arnold69}.  The algebraic half of the collision differential is
associativity for an \(\Eone\)-chart and the Jacobi--Borcherds identity
for a chiral bracket.  The signs belong to the orientation lines of
the collision strata.

\begin{definition}[Local collision package;
\ClaimStatusDefinitional]
\label{def:preface-local-collision-package}
The package \(H_{\mathrm{loc}}(\cA;U)\) consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item the nested-set incidence model for the boundary of
 \(\operatorname{FM}_n(U)\); in arity three its codimension-two
 corners are \(D_{123}\cap D_{ij}\);
\item the full logarithmic de Rham complexes on the
 Fulton--MacPherson spaces \(\operatorname{FM}_n(U)\), with
 coefficients in the derived external tensor powers of
 \(s^{-1}\bar\cA\);
\item one total-degree convention and its induced desuspension,
 de Rham, residue, and orientation signs;
\item signed residue maps along every collision divisor, valued in
 the corresponding orientation lines;
\item compatibility of the internal differential with the chiral
 operation;
\item the Jacobi--Borcherds identity on nested three-point collisions;
\item a declared compactification model---algebraic logarithmic
 residues or real oriented blow-up---together with its corresponding
 residue/Stokes identity and boundary-of-boundary identity;
\item a comparison map from the affine Arnold--Kohno screen to the
 nested residue complex, compatible with the preceding signs.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Local square-zero criterion;
\ClaimStatusConditional]
\label{prop:preface-local-square-zero}
\textup{[Type signature: Open quadrant; ordered logarithmic
Fulton--MacPherson presentation; Beilinson level \(2\); hypothesis
package \(H_{\mathrm{loc}}(\cA;U)\).]}
Let
\[
 d_{\bar B}=d_{\cA}+d_{\mathrm{dR}}+d_{\mathrm{res}}
\]
be the total differential on the completed direct product of the
local collision complexes.  Under
\(H_{\mathrm{loc}}(\cA;U)\), one has \(d_{\bar B}^{\,2}=0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The internal and de Rham squares vanish.  Compatibility gives the
mixed cancellation with \(d_{\cA}\).  The selected residue/Stokes
identity identifies the mixed de Rham--residue terms with oriented
boundary terms.  The nested-set boundary-of-boundary identity cancels
the codimension-two corners.  The comparison map carries the
Arnold--Kohno relation
\eqref{eq:preface-arnold} to the nested residue complex, while the
Jacobi--Borcherds identity supplies the coefficient relation.
\end{proof}

The proposition is a criterion for a chain model.  The global
Beilinson--Drinfeld object is coordinate-free: it is formed from
chiral operations of right \(\mathcal D_X\)-modules on
\(\operatorname{Ran}(X)\).  The coordinate calculation above is its
first local test.

\section*{2.  The point model}

Let \(A=k\oplus\bar A\) be an augmented dg associative algebra.  In
the cohomological convention used here, its reduced bar construction
has underlying conilpotent coalgebra
\[
 B(A)=T^c(s^{-1}\bar A)
\]
and differential \(d_A+d_\mu\), where \(d_\mu\) is induced by
multiplication.  For the square-zero algebra \(A=k\oplus M\), the
product on \(M\) vanishes and
\[
 B(A)=T^c(s^{-1}M),\qquad d_\mu=0.
\]
This elementary example fixes the object type: the bar construction
is a coalgebra, and its cobar is an algebra.

\begin{proposition}[Arity-three associative bar cancellation;
\ClaimStatusProvedHere]
\label{prop:preface-associative-bar-three}
\textup{[Type signature: Open quadrant; tensor-coalgebra
presentation over a point; Beilinson level \(2\); dg associativity
package \(H_{\mathrm{Ass}}\).]}
For homogeneous \(a,b,c\in\bar A\), the multiplication part of the
bar differential satisfies
\[
 b_\mu^2[a|b|c]
 =(-1)^{|b|}\bigl[(ab)c-a(bc)\bigr]=0.
\]
The mixed terms with the internal differential cancel by the
derivation law.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
There are two length-two contraction orders.  The desuspension signs
give the displayed difference, and associativity identifies its two
terms.  The graded Leibniz rule gives the mixed cancellation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bar--cobar resolution, Loday--Vallette;
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere]
\label{thm:preface-point-bar-cobar}
\textup{[Type signature: Open quadrant; dg algebra/dg coalgebra
presentation over a point; Beilinson levels \(1\leftrightarrow2\);
augmentation and conilpotence package \(H_{\mathrm{pt}}\).]}
For an augmented dg algebra \(A\) and a conilpotent dg coalgebra
\(C\), the counit and unit
\[
 \Omega B(A)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }A,
 \qquad
 C\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }B\Omega(C)
\]
are quasi-isomorphisms
\cite[Corollary~2.3.4]{LV12}.
\end{theorem}

Koszulness adds a second grading.  For a connected quadratic algebra
it means diagonal concentration of
\(\operatorname{Tor}^A(k,k)\).  For example,
\(A=\operatorname{Sym}(V)\) has
\[
 \operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{p,q}(k,k)=0\quad(p\ne q),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{p,p}(k,k)\cong\Lambda^p(V),
\]
the classical Koszul calculation
\cite[Proposition~3.4.13]{LV12}.  The bar coalgebra
\(B(A)\), the resolution \(\Omega B(A)\), the quadratic coalgebra
\(A^{\mathrm i}\), its graded dual algebra \(A^!\), and the
Hochschild cochain complex
\(C^\bullet(A,A)\) are five typed constructions linked by explicit
maps.

\begin{theorem}[Quadratic Koszul recognition, Loday--Vallette;
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere]
\label{thm:preface-quadratic-recognition}
\textup{[Type signature: Open quadrant; connected quadratic
weight-graded presentation over a point; Beilinson levels
\(1\leftrightarrow2\); canonical twisting datum
\(\tau_{\mathrm i}\colon A^{\mathrm i}\to A\).]}
Let \(A=T(V)/(R)\) be connected and quadratic, and form the quadratic
coalgebra
\[
 A^{\mathrm i}:=C(s^{-1}V,s^{-2}R)\subset T^c(s^{-1}V).
\]
The canonical twisting morphism
\(\tau_{\mathrm i}\colon A^{\mathrm i}\to A\) induces maps
\[
 q_A\colon A^{\mathrm i}\longrightarrow B(A),
 \qquad
 \Omega(A^{\mathrm i})\longrightarrow A.
\]
The following conditions are equivalent: \(\tau_{\mathrm i}\) is a
Koszul twisting morphism; \(q_A\) is a quasi-isomorphism;
\(\Omega(A^{\mathrm i})\to A\) is a quasi-isomorphism; and the left
and right twisted tensor products determined by
\(\tau_{\mathrm i}\) are acyclic.  Equivalently, the bigraded bar
homology of \(A\) is concentrated on the quadratic diagonal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fundamental theorem for twisting morphisms gives the equivalence
between the two comparison maps and the two twisted tensor products
\cite[Theorem~2.3.2]{LV12}.  The quadratic recognition
theorem identifies these conditions with diagonal bar homology
\cite[Theorem~3.4.6]{LV12}.
\end{proof}

The universal resolution \(\Omega B(A)\to A\) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:preface-point-bar-cobar} exists for every augmented
algebra.  Theorem~\ref{thm:preface-quadratic-recognition} asks whether
the smaller presentation-dependent coalgebra \(A^{\mathrm i}\)
already computes the full bar coalgebra.

\section*{3.  From a point to the Ran space}

Francis and Gaitsgory place factorization algebras inside the larger
category of chiral commutative coalgebras and express their comparison
as chiral Koszul duality
\cite{FrancisGaitsgory2012}.  Completion and conilpotence determine
the subcategories on which bar and cobar become inverse.

\begin{theorem}[Enhanced associative Ran reconstruction,
Francis--Gaitsgory; \ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere]
\label{thm:preface-enhanced-ran-reconstruction}
\textup{[Type signature: Open quadrant; enhanced associative Ran
presentation; Beilinson levels \(1\leftrightarrow2\); pro-nilpotent
Francis--Gaitsgory ambient; reduced associative operad.]}
Let
\[
 \mathcal C_X=\bigl(D(\operatorname{Ran}X),
 \otimes^{\mathrm{ch}}\bigr).
\]
For the reduced associative operad, enhanced bar and cobar are inverse
equivalences between nonunital associative algebras and divided-power
conilpotent coalgebras in \(\mathcal C_X\).  After re-unitalization,
the unit and counit give equivalences
\[
 C\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }B_X^{\mathrm{enh}}\Omega_X^{\mathrm{enh}}C,
 \qquad
 \Omega_X^{\mathrm{enh}}B_X^{\mathrm{enh}}A
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }A.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Francis--Gaitsgory's enhanced bar--cobar equivalence is
\cite[Proposition~4.1.2]{Francis2012}; the proof of
\cite[Theorem~5.1.1]{Francis2012} supplies pro-nilpotence of the
chiral monoidal category on the Ran space.  Applying the reduced
theorem to the augmentation ideal and restoring the unit gives the
displayed maps.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Completed Ran bar--cobar package;
\ClaimStatusDefinitional]
\label{def:preface-ran-package}
Fix the pro-nilpotent Francis--Gaitsgory ambient
\(\mathcal C_X=(D(\operatorname{Ran}X),\otimes^{\mathrm{ch}})\).
The completed factorization package
\[
 H_{\mathrm{BC}}(X):=
 H_{\mathrm{Fact}}+H_{\mathrm{fact}}+H_{\mathrm{conv}}
\]
consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item chosen complete augmented factorization algebras and complete
 conilpotent factorization coalgebras on \(\operatorname{Ran}(X)\);
\item continuous bar and cobar functors between these subcategories;
\item factorization base change and disjoint-union compatibility;
\item a conservative family of exact symmetric-monoidal realizations
 carrying the associated-graded unit and counit to the ordinary
 augmented/conilpotent bar--cobar maps;
\item an exhaustive complete filtration whose comparison spectral
 sequences converge;
\item continuity of tensor products and inverse limits on the chosen
 subcategories.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Completed chiral bar--cobar comparison
\textup{(}Theorem A\textup{)}; \ClaimStatusConditional]
\label{thm:preface-theorem-a}
\textup{[Type signature: Open quadrant; Ran/factorization
presentation; Beilinson levels \(1\leftrightarrow2\); hypothesis
package \(H_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)\).]}
Under \(H_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)\), chiral bar and cobar form a derived
adjoint equivalence on the chosen subcategories:
\[
 \Omega_X^{\mathrm{cont}}\colon
 \operatorname{CoFact}^{\mathrm{conil},\mathrm{comp}}(X)
 \rightleftarrows
 \operatorname{Fact}^{\mathrm{aug},\mathrm{comp}}(X)
 :B_X^{\mathrm{ch}}.
\]
The derived unit and counit are equivalences.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Loday--Vallette's ordinary bar--cobar resolution
\cite[Corollary~2.3.4]{LV12} identifies the realized
associated-graded unit and counit as quasi-isomorphisms.  Conservativity
gives the same conclusion in the associated-graded Ran category.
Completeness and convergence lift these equivalences through the
filtration.  Continuity and factorization base change place the
resulting maps in the chosen Ran subcategories.
\end{proof}

Every family application requires completed objects, a compatible
filtration, and a convergence proof.  The
Loday--Vallette theorem supplies the pointwise model; the Ran
hypotheses carry that model through factorization.

For an equivariant finite window on ordered configurations,
symmetrisation is expressed by homotopy descent:
\[
 \bigl(Q^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\cA,N}\bigr)_{h\Sigma_n}
 \longrightarrow Q^{\mathrm{sym}}_{\cA,N}.
\]
Monodromy is incorporated by the corresponding local system and
twisted homotopy coinvariants.  The descent quasi-isomorphism and its
nearby-cycle extension form the package
\(H_{\mathrm{fact}}^R\).

The six Beilinson levels are a sequence of constructions.

\begin{tabular}{@{}c p{0.32\textwidth} p{0.48\textwidth}@{}}
\textbf{Level} & \textbf{Object} & \textbf{Reconstruction datum}\\
\hline
\(0\) &
factorization dg-category \(\cC^{\mathrm{op}}\) with a chosen object
\(b\) &
Morita generator and compactness package\\
\(1\) &
\(A_b=R\operatorname{End}_{\cC}(b)\), an augmented
\(\Eone\)-chart &

\(2\) &

\begin{definition}[Verdier--Ran comparison package;
\ClaimStatusDefinitional]
\label{def:preface-verdier-package}
For a complete augmented factorization algebra \(\cA\), the package
\(H_{\mathbb D}(\cA)\) consists of
\(H_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)\), dualizable filtered pieces, continuity of
\(\mathbb D_{\operatorname{Ran}}\), compatibility with factorization
base change, and convergence of the filtered dual.
\end{definition}

Under \(H_{\mathbb D}(\cA)\), Verdier duality carries the complete
bar coalgebra to the augmented factorization algebra
\[
 \cA^!_\infty
 :=\mathbb D_{\operatorname{Ran}}B_X^{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA).
\]
An identification \(\cA^!_\infty\simeq\cA^!\) with a quadratic
Koszul partner is supplied by a separate comparison
quasi-isomorphism.  The locus \(\mathrm{KSDual}\) consists of objects
equipped with a coherent equivalence
\(\cA\simeq\cA^!_\infty\); its coherence identifies the square of the
duality with the identity.

\section*{5.  Positive genus}

Loop edges contract two inputs.  Their construction therefore begins
with cyclic data.  Let \(\cA\) carry a perfect invariant pairing of a
fixed cohomological degree.  The Getzler--Kapranov Feynman transform
organizes the resulting stable-graph contractions with determinant
twists and graph automorphism coinvariants
\cite[\S5]{GetzlerKapranov98}.

\begin{definition}[Modular chain package;
\ClaimStatusDefinitional]
\label{def:preface-modular-package}
The package \(H_{\mathrm{mod}}(\cA)\) consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item a perfect cyclic pairing and its orientation convention;
\item chain models for the stable compactifications;
\item signed residue and proper pushforward maps along every clutching
 morphism;
\item compatibility with separating and non-separating gluing;
\item a complete stable-graph filtration;
\item a continuous square-zero total differential
 \(D_{\cA}\) extending the genus-zero differential \(d_0\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

For a datum satisfying \(H_{\mathrm{mod}}(\cA)\), write
\(\fg_{\cA}^{\mathrm{mod}}\) for the complete modular convolution dg
Lie algebra.  The positive-genus part of the differential is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preface-theta}
 \Theta_{\cA}:=D_{\cA}-d_0
 =\sum_{[\Gamma]\in\mathsf{Gr}^{\mathrm{st}}}
   \operatorname{ins}_{\Gamma}(\theta_{\cA,\Gamma}),
 \qquad
 \theta_{\cA,\Gamma}\in
 \bigl(\orline{\Gamma}\otimes
 \operatorname{Hom}(\bar\cA^{\otimes\operatorname{Leg}(\Gamma)},k)
 \bigr)_{h\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)}.
\end{equation}
The sum converges in the stable-graph filtration.

\begin{proposition}[Maurer--Cartan identity;
\ClaimStatusProvedHere]
\label{prop:preface-mc-formal}
\textup{[Type signature: Open modular quadrant; complete convolution
presentation; Beilinson level \(5\); hypothesis package
\(H_{\mathrm{mod}}(\cA)\).]}
The element \(\Theta_{\cA}\) satisfies
\[
 d_0\Theta_{\cA}
 +\frac12[\Theta_{\cA},\Theta_{\cA}]=0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expand \(D_{\cA}^{\,2}=(d_0+\Theta_{\cA})^2\) in the complete
convolution algebra.  The identities
\(D_{\cA}^{\,2}=d_0^2=0\) leave the displayed equation.
\end{proof}

The construction separates an extension obstruction from a
characteristic operation.  For a partial lift
\(\Theta_{\le r}\) in a filtered dg Lie algebra, the next obstruction
is the class
\[
 o_{r+1}(\Theta_{\le r})
 \in H^2\bigl(\operatorname{gr}^{r+1}\fg_{\cA}^{\mathrm{mod}}\bigr).
\]
A full Maurer--Cartan element carries higher graph components as
operations.  A trace maps those operations to cohomology classes or
numbers.

\begin{principle}[The four-test interface]
\label{princ:four-test-interface}
\ClaimStatusDefinitional
A modular comparison is carried by four explicit tests:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item the chain identity \(D_{\cA}^{\,2}=0\);
\item homotopy descent from ordered to symmetric configuration data;
\item perfectness and compatibility of the cyclic pairing;
\item clutching compatibility for every stable-graph boundary map.
\end{enumerate}
Each test is an identity between specified chain maps.
\end{principle}

\section*{6.  Chiral Hochschild support}

The derived chiral centre is computed from a two-sided diagonal
resolution.  A family comparison with bounded vertex cochains is an
additional quasi-isomorphism.

\begin{theorem}[Bakalov--De Sole--Kac benchmarks;
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere]
\label{thm:preface-bdsk-benchmarks}
For the universal Virasoro vertex algebra
\(\operatorname{Vir}_c\), for every central charge \(c\),
\[
 \dim H^n_{\mathrm{ch},b}(\operatorname{Vir}_c)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 1,&n\in\{0,2,3\},\\
 0,&n\notin\{0,2,3\}.
 \end{cases}
\]
For the free superboson \(B_{\mathfrak h}\),
\[
 H^n_{\mathrm{ch},b}(B_{\mathfrak h})
 \cong
 \bigl(S^n(\Pi\mathfrak h)\bigr)^*
 \oplus
 \bigl(S^{n+1}(\Pi\mathfrak h)\bigr)^*.
\]
These are Theorems~7.2 and~7.4 of
Bakalov--De Sole--Kac
\cite{BDSK21}.
\end{theorem}

For a one-dimensional even \(\mathfrak h\), the parity shift
\(\Pi\mathfrak h\) is odd, so the second formula gives dimensions
\((2,1,0,\ldots)\).

\begin{definition}[Family support datum;
\ClaimStatusDefinitional]
\label{def:preface-family-support}
For a finite set \(S\subset\mathbb Z\), the datum
\(H_H(\cA;S)\) consists of a complete filtered complex
\(Q_{\cA}\), a quasi-isomorphism
\[
 \gamma_{\cA}\colon Q_{\cA}
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 C^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA,\cA),
\]
a complex \(K_{\cA,S}\) supported in \(S\), and continuous maps
\[
 K_{\cA,S}
 \mathop{\xrightarrow{\ \iota_{\cA}\ }}Q_{\cA}
 \mathop{\xrightarrow{\ p_{\cA}\ }}K_{\cA,S},
 \qquad
 h_{\cA}\colon Q_{\cA}\longrightarrow Q_{\cA}[-1],
\]
satisfying
\[
 p_{\cA}\iota_{\cA}=\operatorname{id},\qquad
 d h_{\cA}+h_{\cA}d
 =\operatorname{id}-\iota_{\cA}p_{\cA}.
\]
For inverse-limit models, the datum includes the corresponding
Mittag--Leffler comparison.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Family-indexed chiral Hochschild support
\textup{(}Theorem H\textup{)}; \ClaimStatusConditional]
\label{thm:preface-theorem-h}
\textup{[Type signature: Open quadrant; completed chiral cochain
presentation; Beilinson level \(3\); hypothesis package
\(H_H(\cA;S)\).]}
The composite
\[
 K_{\cA,S}\xrightarrow{\ \gamma_{\cA}\iota_{\cA}\ }
 C^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA,\cA)
\]
is a quasi-isomorphism.  Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{Supp}
 \operatorname{ChirHoch}^{\bullet}(\cA)\subseteq S.
\]
For a translation-equivariant vertex algebra \(V\), a
quasi-isomorphism from bounded vertex cochains to the chosen chiral
chart transports the benchmarks of
Theorem~\ref{thm:preface-bdsk-benchmarks}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The deformation-retract identities identify
\(K_{\cA,S}\) with \(Q_{\cA}\) in the derived category.
Composition with \(\gamma_{\cA}\) gives the asserted
quasi-isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\section*{7.  The five reconstruction problems}

The letters \(A,B,C,D,H\) name five mathematical problems.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.8em,style=nextline]
\item[\(\mathbf A\).]
Construct the complete Ran bar--cobar adjunction and verify
\(H_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)\).  The point model is
Theorem~\ref{thm:preface-point-bar-cobar}; the Ran theorem is
conditional on the displayed package.

\item[\(\mathbf B\).]
Construct a chiral Comparison Lemma for a typed twisting datum
\((\cA,C,\tau)\).  Under convergence, it compares acyclicity of the
twisted tensor products with
\(C\to B_X^{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA)\) and
\(\Omega_X C\to\cA\).  The choice \(C=\cA^{\mathrm i}\) gives
quadratic Koszul recognition.  For a fixed coalgebra \(C\), the
coderived/completed problem compares its comodule and contramodule
categories.

\item[\(\mathbf C\).]
Represent Verdier--Koszul duality by a cochain involution
\(\sigma\).  In characteristic zero, the projectors
\[
 e_\pm=\frac{1\pm\sigma}{2}
\]
give the formal eigenspace decomposition.  A perfect
anti-invariant pairing supplies the Lagrangian upgrade.

\item[\(\mathbf D\).]
Construct \(H_{\mathrm{mod}}(\cA)\), hence the stable-graph
Maurer--Cartan element.  Family traces then produce scalar genus
classes.

\item[\(\mathbf H\).]
Construct \(H_H(\cA;S)\) and the bounded-to-chart comparison.
The set \(S\) and the cohomology dimensions are family data.
\end{description}

This formulation keeps bar resolution, quadratic Koszul duality,
derived-centre cohomology, cyclic modular extension, and scalar trace
in their natural categories.

\section*{8.  Route through the volume}

Part~I fixes the factorization category, the algebra chart, and the
Ran-space conventions.  Part~II develops twisting morphisms,
completion, and the bar--cobar comparison.  Part~III constructs the
derived chiral centre and its family comparison complexes.  Part~IV
tests the packages on explicit vertex-algebra families.  Part~V adds
cyclic traces, stable graphs, and clutching.  Part~VI formulates the
reconstruction programme and its comparison functors.

Volumes~II and~III supply further targets.  The holomorphic-topological
bulk comparison is carried by an explicit open--closed functor.  The
Calabi--Yau-to-chiral passage is carried by a two-stage specialization
functor with its geometric choices.  Their numerical specializations
belong to the corresponding comparison theorems, after the functors
and their hypothesis packages have been constructed.
