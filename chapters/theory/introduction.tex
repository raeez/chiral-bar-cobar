\chapter{Introduction}

\section{From a point to a curve}
\label{sec:volume-one-route}

The order is forced by the first collision. A bare point has the
classical bar complex; a formal disk has OPE modes; a curve has
configuration spaces and a logarithmic normal form along each
diagonal. On an affine genus-zero collision screen this normal form is
represented by \(\eta_{ij}=d\log(z_i-z_j)\). Fulton--MacPherson
boundary strata turn that local representative into the residue part
of the ordered bar differential; Arnold's three-term relation is the
affine/formal nilpotence check; the Borcherds identity is the
selection condition on the OPE. The governing chain is
\[
\eta_{ij}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}_X(\cA)
\;\longrightarrow\;
\Theta_\cA
\;\longrightarrow\;
\operatorname{av}(\Theta_\cA)
\;\longrightarrow\;
\{A,B,C,D,H\}.
\]

For a smooth algebraic curve~$X$, the Fulton--MacPherson
compactifications $\FM_n(X)$ of ordered configuration spaces have
boundary strata encoding factorisation of collisions. This geometry
supplies the ordered factorisation/cooperadic input for a chiral
algebra~$\cA$ and hence the ordered bar coalgebra
\[
\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}_X(\cA)\;=\;T^c(s^{-1}\bar\cA)
\]
with deconcatenation coproduct. Its differential is
\[
D=d_{\mathrm{int}}+d_{\mathrm{res}}+d_{\mathrm{dR}},
\]
where $d_{\mathrm{res}}$ is built from the logarithmic propagator
$\eta_{ij}$, represented on an affine/formal genus-zero screen by
\(d\log(z_i-z_j)\). On affine genus-zero triple collision screens the
Arnold relation is exactly the logarithmic-form identity needed for
$D^2=0$; on $\mathbb P^1$ the coordinate representative is glued with
the projective linear relations, and on positive-genus curves it is
replaced by prime-form/KZB representatives and the
Totaro--K\v{r}\'{\i}\v{z}--Bezrukavnikov period terms.  On the
trivial-braiding locus, homotopy \(\Sigma_n\)-coinvariants give
\[
\barB^{\Sigma}_X(\cA)\;=\;\Sym^c(s^{-1}\bar\cA),
\]
whose commutative shuffle coproduct is the averaged image of
deconcatenation.  For a general \(E_1\)-chiral algebra the symmetric
object is the \(R\)-twisted homotopy descent
\[
\barB_X^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)_{h\Sigma_n^R};
\]
its comparison with \(\Sym^c(s^{-1}\bar\cA)\) belongs to the
\(R\)-descent hypothesis.  The all-genus corrected differential is written
$D_\cA=d_0+\Theta_\cA$; the equation $D_\cA^2=0$ is the
Maurer--Cartan identity for~$\Theta_\cA$.  Averaging maps the ordered
complex to its symmetric image; its homotopy fibre carries the
ordered \(r\)-matrix, braid, and associator data.
The five theorem packages are the invariants of this loss: universal
Ran reconstruction, quadratic Koszul recognition, scalar-centre
complementarity, the modular scalar characteristic, and chiral
Hochschild support.  Specialization to a point,
trivial braiding, and genus zero gives classical Koszul duality:
$\Omega(B(A))\simeq A$ is
bar-cobar inversion under the stated hypotheses,
$K_X(\cA):=\mathbb{D}_{\operatorname{Ran}}\barB_X(\cA)$ is the
Verdier algebra, the chosen-pair map
$\nu_\cA\colon K_X(\cA)\to\cA^!$ produces the Koszul partner under
the stated hypotheses, and
$R\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\cA\otimes\cA^{\mathrm{op}}}(\cA,\cA)$ is
the derived-centre component.

The obstruction is geometric. On an algebraic curve~$X$, the bar
differential is an integral transform whose local kernel is the
logarithmic normal form along the diagonal, represented in an affine
coordinate by $d\log(z_i - z_j)$ on the corresponding
Fulton--MacPherson collision screen. The transition cocycle under
coordinate change is part of the Virasoro/projective-connection data,
and at positive genus the representative is replaced by elliptic KZB
or prime-form data. This already separates chiral Koszul duality from
the classical point theory at genus~$0$. A vertex algebra is a
formal-disk object. Passing from the disk~$D$ to a point discards the
completion, growth, and thickening data carried by the
$\mathcal{D}$-module structure.

After choosing retraction data, the affine line~$\mathbb{A}^1$
deformation-retracts onto a point. Relating the bar construction on
$\mathbb{A}^1$ to the classical bar over a point therefore requires a
specified retraction and its homotopy transfer. The configuration
spaces $\mathrm{Conf}_n(\mathbb{A}^1)$ carry Fulton--MacPherson
compactifications and the Arnol'd algebra
$H^*(\mathrm{Conf}_n(\mathbb{A}^1))$, generated on the affine chart by
the forms $\omega_{ij} = d\log(z_i - z_j)$ subject to the Arnold
relation.
The point theory carries the trivial configuration-space algebra.
On~$\mathbb{P}^1 = \mathbb{A}^1 \cup \mathbb{A}^1$, compactness
changes the homotopy type again. The classical theory enters through
formal-disk restriction and homotopy transfer as a specialization.
Even at genus~$0$, the ordered bar, the unordered bar, and the
configuration-space geometry are distinct structures. At
genus~$g \geq 1$, the Hodge bundle $\mathbb{E}$ on
$\overline{\mathcal{M}}_g$ introduces curvature into the bar
differential. The result is the central extension, conformal anomaly,
and curved $A_\infty$ structure absent from the point model.

The classical theory begins with an augmented dg algebra $A$.  Its
bar construction
\[
B(A)=\bigl(T^c(s^{-1}\bar A),d_B\bigr)
\]
has deconcatenation coproduct; $d_B$ is the coderivation induced by
the internal differential and multiplication of $A$.  For a
conilpotent dg coalgebra $C$, the cobar algebra
$\Omega(C)$ is free on the shifted coaugmentation coideal, and its
differential is induced by the internal differential and reduced
coproduct of $C$.  Loday--Vallette's unit--counit theorem gives
\[
\varepsilon \colon \Omega(B(A)) \longrightarrow A
\]
as a quasi-isomorphism for every $A$
\cite[Corollary~2.3.4]{LV12}.

Now let $A=T(V)/(R)$ be quadratic.  The quadratic datum $(V,R)$
defines a conilpotent quadratic coalgebra $A^i$ and a canonical
comparison
\[
q_A\colon A^i\longrightarrow B(A).
\]
Priddy's Koszul condition is the assertion that $q_A$ is a
quasi-isomorphism; equivalently,
$\Omega(A^i)\to A$ is a quasi-isomorphism
\cite{Priddy70,LV12}.  Under local finite-dimensionality its dual is
\[
A^!=(A^i)^\vee=T(V^*)/(R^\perp).
\]
Thus full bar--cobar reconstruction and quadratic compression are two
successive operations.

Over a curve, the Ran space supplies the geometric ambient for the
same two operations.  Let $X$ be a smooth projective curve
over~$\mathbb{C}$. A \emph{chiral algebra}~$\cA$ on~$X$
(Beilinson--Drinfeld~\cite{BD04}) is a $\mathcal{D}_X$-module equipped
with a chiral bracket
\[
\mu^{\mathrm{ch}} \colon j_* j^*(\cA \boxtimes \cA) \longrightarrow \Delta_!(\cA),
\]
where $j \colon X^2 \setminus \Delta \hookrightarrow X^2$ is the complement of the diagonal and $\Delta_! \colon D(\mathcal{D}_X) \to D(\mathcal{D}_{X^2})$ is the pushforward. In local coordinates the chiral bracket encodes the operator product expansion:
\[
a(z) b(w) \;\sim\; \sum_{n \geq 0} \frac{(a_{(n)} b)(w)}{(z - w)^{n+1}}.
\]
The Borcherds identity, the defining axiom of a vertex algebra, is the chiral-algebraic Jacobi identity: it couples all pole orders in a single constraint.

\begin{remark}[CG opening and the $\Eone$ atom]
\label{rem:two-strata}
\index{two strata|textbf}
Two reference algebras organize the theory
(see Chapter~\ref{ch:algebraic-foundations}, \S\ref{sec:dictionary} for the formal definitions).
The \textbf{Heisenberg} algebra is the \emph{CG opening}: its singular
OPE is purely double-pole, so collisions are symmetric and unordered
($\Einf$-chiral). It is the commutative base case in which every
clause of the reconstruction thesis is computable. It lies outside the
$\Eone$ atom.
The \textbf{Yangian} is the basic $\Eone$ test object: simple poles
give collisions a canonical ordering ($\Eone$-chiral, genuinely
nonlocal), and the finite RTT/evaluation-generated core is the
proved nonlocal generator in the present $\Eone$-chiral Koszul
theory.
The standard landscape in this volume is the finite atlas consisting of
the Heisenberg, affine Kac--Moody, $\beta\gamma$, Virasoro/principal
$\mathcal W$, and finite-rank even-lattice charts, always with the
genericity and completion hypotheses stated in the theorem using the
chart. These charts sit between the CG opening and the $\Eone$ atom.
The ordered bar complex on higher-genus curves assigns to each chart its
Koszul, shadow-depth, and modular-residue invariants. The displayed
finite atlas is the domain of this stratification.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Type correctness of the core ordered-bar datum;
\ClaimStatusProvedHere]
\label{lem:intro-core-ordered-bar-type-correct}
\index{ordered bar!type correctness}
\textup{[}Type signature:
$(\mathrm{Open},\mathrm{chain},
\mathrm{levels}=0\text{--}5,
\mathrm{hypothesis\ package}=\mathrm{finite\ type}
\ +\ \mathrm{PBW\text{-}Kosz}
\ +\ \mathrm{strict\ ML}
\ +\ \mathrm{equivariant\ descent})$\textup{].}
Let \((X,D)\) be a smooth curve or an snc curve-pair, let
\(\mathsf C\) be a stable presentable factorization category with
factorization compact generator \(b\), put
\(\cC^\op=(\mathsf C^\omega)^\op\), and set
\(A_b=\RHom_{\mathsf C}(b,b)\).  Let \(\cA\) be an augmented

\(\cA=A_b\).  Then the core symbols used in the Modular Ordered Bar
Theorem have the following source and target types before any
reconstruction theorem is invoked.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item
\[

 =
T^c(s^{-1}\bar\cA)\widehat\otimes
\Omega^\bullet_{\log}(\FM^{\mathrm{ord}}_\bullet(X,D))
\]
is a level~2 complete pro-conilpotent factorization coalgebra in completed
chains.
\item \(B_X^\Sigma(\cA)\) is the homotopy
\(\Sigma_n\)-coinvariant symmetric bar coalgebra in the same completed
chain ambient.  The averaging map
\(\Av\colon B_X^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)\to B_X^\Sigma(\cA)\) is a
coalgebra morphism under the finite-type/equivariant-descent and
completion hypotheses.
\item A connected positive quadratic presentation
\(\cA=T_X(V)/(R)\) defines the level~2 quadratic coalgebra
\(\cA^{\mathrm i}=C_X(s^{-1}V,s^{-2}R)\) and the canonical comparison
\[
q_\cA\colon\cA^{\mathrm i}\longrightarrow B_X^\Sigma(\cA).
\]
Theorem~B studies this symmetric comparison under

\item Under \(H_{\mathbb D}(\cA)\), continuous Verdier duality defines

\item Positselski's co/contra correspondence begins with a fixed

\item The scalar quantities \(\kappa(\cA)\), \(K^\kappa(\cA)\), and

\item The physical bulk/OCA object and its boundary-to-bulk morphism
belong to the physical category selected by the OCA hypothesis.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the theorem below has a typed domain, codomain, centre,
and scalar-shadow lane before any comparison between these objects is
made.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The suspension \(s^{-1}\) lowers cohomological degree by one and the
reduced tensor coalgebra \(T^c(s^{-1}\bar\cA)\) carries the standard
deconcatenation coproduct.  Completed tensoring with logarithmic
Fulton--MacPherson forms preserves conilpotence on finite
conformal-weight windows and passes to strict Mittag--Leffler limits by
the stated completion hypothesis; this proves \textup{(i)}.  The
symmetric object in \textup{(ii)} is the homotopy coinvariant quotient
for the \(\Sigma_n\)-action on the ordered chains, so the displayed
averaging map has the asserted source and target precisely when
finite-type equivariant descent makes the coinvariant projection
continuous.  The inclusion of the quadratic corelations gives
\textup{(iii)}.  Contravariance and continuity of Verdier duality give
\textup{(iv)}.  Chiral Hochschild cochains are the derived endomorphism
cochains of the diagonal chiral bimodule, giving \textup{(v)}.
Positselski's fixed-coalgebra source supplies \textup{(vi)}.  The
quantities in \textup{(vii)} are numbers or tautological Hodge classes
extracted by trace and clutching.  Clause~\textup{(viii)} records the
domain of the OCA comparison.  Thus all maps used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:modular-ordered-bar-theorem} are type-correct before
the theorem asserts any reconstruction or scalar survival.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The Modular Ordered Bar Theorem;
\ClaimStatusConditional]
\label{thm:modular-ordered-bar-theorem}
\index{Modular Ordered Bar Theorem|textbf}
\index{Signed Log-FM Cooperad Theorem}
\index{Typed Verdier--Koszul Firewall Theorem}
\index{Completed Chiral Positselski Theorem}
\index{Curve-Level Chiral Hochschild Amplitude Theorem}
\textup{[}Type signature:
$(\mathrm{Open},\mathrm{ordered/symmetric\ chiral\ bar},
\mathrm{levels}=0\text{--}5,
\mathrm{hypothesis\ package}=\mathrm{LogFM}_{\mathrm{sgn}}
\ +\ \mathrm{PBW\text{-}Kosz}
\ +\ \mathrm{strict\ ML}
\ +\ \mathrm{OCA\ when\ physical\ bulk\ is\ named})$\textup{].}
Let \(X\) be a smooth complex algebraic curve, or a smooth curve in an
snc pair \((X,D)\), and let
\[
\cA\in
\operatorname{Alg}^{\mathrm{aug,PBW,hol,ft,comp}}_{\chirAss}(D_X)
\]
be an augmented \(E_1\)-chiral algebra, finite type in each
conformal-weight window, with PBW filtration, holonomic
\(\mathcal D_X\)-module input, and strict Mittag--Leffler completion
tower.  Assume the following five clauses.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Roman*)}]
\item \emph{Signed Log-FM Cooperad Theorem}
\textup{(}Theorem~\ref{thm:signed-logfm-cooperad-theorem}\textup{).}
The logarithmic
Fulton--MacPherson chain object
\(\mathsf C^{\log\mathrm{FM}}_{\mathrm{mod}}(X,D)\) is equipped with
signed Gysin residues, determinant-line orientations, proper
pushforwards, automorphism normalisations, and homotopy-coherent
cocompositions.  Under the signed package it is a homotopy dg modular
cooperad and the six-component differential is uncurved; the curvature
of the five-term truncation is precisely the planted-forest defect
cancelled by \(d_{\mathrm{pf}}\).
\item \emph{Typed Verdier--Koszul comparison.}  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:typed-verdier-koszul-firewall}, the five objects
\[
\cA,\qquad B_X^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA),\qquad
\cA^{\mathrm i},\qquad
\cA^!,\qquad Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA)
\]
live respectively in augmented chiral algebras, complete conilpotent
chiral coalgebras, quadratic coalgebras, post-Verdier completed
chiral algebras, and chiral Hochschild cochain complexes.  The
quadratic presentation supplies
\[
q_\cA\colon\cA^{\mathrm i}\longrightarrow
B_X^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA),
\]
an equivalence under
\(H_{\mathrm{CL}}(\cA,\cA^{\mathrm i},\tau_{\mathrm i})\), equivalently
\(H^*\operatorname{Cone}(q_\cA)=0\).  Theorem~A's bar--cobar counit
recovers \(\cA\); continuous Verdier duality constructs \(\cA^!\);
chiral Hochschild cochains construct
\(Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA)\).
\item \emph{Completed Chiral Positselski Theorem.}  Fix the curved bar
coalgebra~\(C\).  On finite windows its comodule/contramodule comparison
is a finite conilpotent chain theorem.  On class-\(\mathsf M\) and
other infinite towers the strict Mittag--Leffler completion yields the
weight-completed coderived/contraderived equivalence.
\item \emph{Curve-level chiral Hochschild amplitude.}  For a finite
set \(S\subset\mathbb Z\), an amplitude theorem consists of a strong
deformation retract
\[
K_{\cA,S}\mathop{\xrightarrow{\ \iota\ }}C^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA,\cA)
\mathop{\xrightarrow{\ p\ }}K_{\cA,S},
\qquad
dh+hd=\mathrm{id}-\iota p,
\]
with \(K_{\cA,S}\) supported in \(S\).  The set \(S\) depends on the
algebra and coefficient theory.  Bakalov--De Sole--Kac obtain
\(S=\{0,1\}\) for the rank-one even free superboson and
\(S=\{0,2,3\}\) for the Virasoro vertex algebra
\cite[Theorems~7.4 and~7.2]{BDSK21}.  Comparisons among chiral,
algebraic, topological, and categorical Hochschild theories are
carried by named functors.
\item \emph{Ordered-to-symmetric modular trace.}  The ordered bar
\[
B_X^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)
=T^c(s^{-1}\bar\cA)\widehat\otimes
\Omega^\bullet_{\log}(\mathrm{FM}^{\mathrm{ord}}_\bullet(X,D))
\]
has differential \(d_B=d_\cA+d_{\mathrm{dR}}+d_{\mathrm{res}}\), and
the modular logarithmic enhancement has total differential
\[
D_{\mathrm{mod}}^{\log}
=d_{\check C}+d_{\mathrm{ch}}+d_{\mathrm{coll}}
+d_{\mathrm{sew}}+d_{\mathrm{pf}}+\hbar\Delta .
\]
Clause~\textup{(I)} gives
\((D_{\mathrm{mod}}^{\log})^2=0\).  Under finite-type or completed
equivariance and descent, the averaging map
\[
\mathrm{av}\colon B_X^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)\longrightarrow B_X^\Sigma(\cA)
\]
is a chain map.  Its image consists of homotopy-coinvariant operator
classes.  A specified normalized
residue or trace functional produces \(\kappa(\cA)\) and the higher
scalar shadows; the ordered fibre retains collision-kernel, braid,
and associator data.
\end{enumerate}

Then the universal modular class
\[
\Theta_\cA\in
\operatorname{Conv}_\infty\!\left(
\mathsf C^{\log\mathrm{FM}}_{\mathrm{mod}}(X,D),
\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{ch},\infty}(\cA)\right)
\]
satisfies
\[
D\Theta_\cA+\frac12[\Theta_\cA,\Theta_\cA]=0.
\]
Every physics, \(\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}\), \(E_3\), CY-source,
K3 Hall--Borcherds, or arithmetic statement in this volume is either a
proved functorial image of this theorem under a named comparison map,
or a labelled comparison conjecture.
\end{theorem}

At genus~$g \geq 1$, the ordered bar differential enters a curved
$A_\infty$/CDG package: the raw chain identity is
$m_1^2=[m_0^{(g)},-]$, and the ordered curvature element has
operator-valued collision kernel $K_\cA^{\mathrm{coll}}(z)\cdot\omega_g$ in
$\operatorname{End}_\cA(2) \otimes H^{1,1}(\mathcal{C}_g)$.
The homotopy coinvariant
$q_2(K_\cA^{\mathrm{coll}})\cdot\omega_g$ remains operator-valued;
the normalized modular trace gives the scalar diagonal equation
$\operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{diag}}(m_0^{(g)})
= \kappa(\cA) \cdot \omega_g$, where
$\omega_1 = c_1(\mathbb{E})$ at genus~$1$, while the higher-genus
Hodge classes are $\lambda_g = c_g(\mathbb{E})$ on
$\overline{\mathcal{M}}_g$, and
$\kappa(\cA)$ is the modular
characteristic
(Theorem~\ref{thm:modular-characteristic}).

The curvature generates an infinite tower.
The ordered bar construction on curves defines an
$E_1$-modular convolution algebra~$\mathfrak{g}^{E_1}_\cA$,
whose Maurer--Cartan element~$\Theta_\cA$ encodes the full genus
expansion with its ordered collision data intact. Under
$\Sigma_n$-coinvariance, $\Theta_\cA$ projects to a pronilpotent
dg Lie algebra~$\gAmod$ (the modular convolution algebra). The
finite-order projections $\Theta_\cA^{\leq r}$ extract successively
finer operator classes: the binary collision kernel at degree~$2$,
the ternary class at degree~$3$, and the quartic class at degree~$4$.
Homotopy coinvariants retain these operators, and the functionals
$\tau_{\cA,r}^{\mathrm{res}}$ produce
$\kappa_{\mathrm{dp}},\mathfrak C,\mathfrak Q,\ldots$. The
\emph{shadow depth} $r_{\max}(\cA)$, the
largest degree at which the shadow is nonzero, equals
the $L_\infty$ formality level of the genus-$0$ part of $\gAmod$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow-formality-identification}).

On the finite atlas above, the tower separates canonical chart
witnesses into the six-row archetype spine on the non-critical locus,
with a companion stratum at the critical level. This is a
row-by-row invariant stratification in fixed quadrant, presentation,
Beilinson level, and hypothesis package; its domain is the displayed
finite atlas of chiral algebras.
The Heisenberg witness has $r_{\max} = 2$ (formal,
$\Delta = 0$, class~$\mathsf G$); affine Kac--Moody algebras at non-critical
level have $r_{\max} = 3$ (class~$\mathsf L$); the $\beta\gamma$ system has
$r_{\max} = 4$ with nilpotent $R$-matrix (class~$\mathsf C$); Virasoro
and principal $\cW_N$ have $r_{\max} = \infty$ via principal
Drinfeld--Sokolov (class~$\mathsf M$); Bershadsky--Polyakov $\mathrm{BP}_k$
and hook-type $\cW$-algebras from subregular Drinfeld--Sokolov reduction
have $r_{\max} = \infty$ with even $G^\pm$ fields representing
the extended subregular class beyond the Virasoro subcomplex
(class~$\mathsf M^{\mathrm{ext}}$);
the K3 Borcherds--Kac--Moody comparison target
$\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ supplies the conjectural CY-specialisation row
under $(H_{\mathrm{chart}},H_{\mathrm{KD}},H_{\mathrm{scalar}},
H_{\mathrm{mod}},H_{\mathrm{quant}})$.  Its proposed Vol~I coordinates
are $r_{\max}=5$ and $K^\kappa_{\mathsf B}=2c_+(L)=8$; the Borcherds
root tower records the denominator algebra
(class~$\mathsf B$). At the critical level $k=-h^\vee$, the
Feigin--Frenkel centre
$\operatorname{Fun}(\operatorname{Op}_{{}^L\fg})$ replaces the
Sugawara presentation and $\ChirHoch^\bullet$ has unbounded amplitude
(Feigin--Frenkel stratum~$\mathsf{FF}$). The
\emph{critical discriminant} $\Delta = 8\kappa S_4$ forces
$r_{\max}$ to lie in $\{2, 3, \infty\}$ on any one-dimensional
primary slice (Theorem~\ref{thm:single-line-dichotomy}); the
fourth class $r_{\max} = 4$ arises from stratum
separation.
The algebraic depth has the exact range
$d_{\mathrm{alg}} \in \{0, 1, 2, \infty\}$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:depth-gap-trichotomy}).
Swiss-cheese formality ($m_k^{\mathrm{SC}} = 0$ for
$k \geq 3$) holds if and only if the algebra belongs to
class~$\mathbf{G}$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sc-formal-iff-class-g}).
For a free-field algebra $\cA$, the scalar formula

$\delta F_g^{\mathrm{cross}}$ vanishes identically

The logarithmic boundary adds a separate combinatorial stratification.
For Mok's logarithmic Fulton--MacPherson space on an snc pair
$(X,D)$, a boundary type is a planted forest
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{FM}}
=(F_P\to F_{\Sigma_X},m;\,T_{v_1},\ldots,T_{v_k})
\]
with codimension
$\sum_iw_i+\sum_j(|V(T_{v_j})|-1)$. In the relative universal family
the corresponding modular type has refined degree
\[
\mu=(g,r;\,b,s,\ell,c,p),
\]
where $s,\ell$ record stable-curve separating and non-separating
nodes, while $c,p$ record the relative Mok crossing and pure
planted-forest contributions. The full log codimension is
$s+\ell+c+p$; the coarse computational tridegree is $(g,r,c+p)$.
This tuple is the logarithmic boundary filtration, distinct from
Hochschild degree and shadow depth. It is the filtration by which
logarithmic boundary strata refine the modular
bar--cobar object before averaging. Mok supplies the geometric
strata and degeneration formula; the chain cooperad, the
$d_{\mathrm{pf}}$ term, and ambient $D^2=0$ are conditional on signed
Gysin residues, determinant-line orientations, and coherent proper
pushforwards.
When this package is fixed, the logarithmic filtration is compatible
with bar--cobar:
\[
\operatorname{gr}_F\Omega\barB^{\mathrm{mod},\log}(\cA)
\;\cong\;
\Omega\bigl(\operatorname{gr}_F\barB^{\mathrm{mod},\log}(\cA)\bigr).
\]


Three depths measure distinct aspects of the tower.
The \emph{shadow depth} $r_{\max}$ is the largest degree at which the shadow is nonzero.
The \emph{generating depth} $d_{\mathrm{gen}}$ is the smallest degree at which all higher operations are determined recursively.
The \emph{algebraic depth} $d_{\mathrm{alg}}$ records whether the tower terminates
(Definition~\ref{def:generating-depth}).
These are independent invariants: Virasoro has
$d_{\mathrm{gen}} = 3$ and $d_{\mathrm{alg}} = \infty$; $m_3$
generates the higher tower recursively, with a nonzero operation in every arity.

The tower is governed by three structure theorems, all projections of a single object: the Maurer--Cartan element
$\Theta_\cA \in \MC(\gAmod)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mc2-bar-intrinsic}).
\emph{Algebraicity}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:riccati-algebraicity}).
On each primary line~$L$, the shadow generating function satisfies
$H(t)^2 = t^4 Q_L(t)$,
where $Q_L$ is a quadratic polynomial in three invariants $(\kappa, \alpha, S_4)$. The
tower is the Taylor expansion of~$\sqrt{Q_L}$, algebraic of degree~$2$.
The scalar discriminant~$\Delta$ governs the branch structure of this
primary-line square root after the support packet has been fixed.
Together with the contact flag, the discriminant classifies the support
packet: the flag separates the finite contact class, while a nonzero
scalar discriminant on the mixed Virasoro line witnesses the
non-terminal wheel.

\section{The selection principle}
\label{sec:selection-principle}
\index{selection principle|textbf}
\index{Borcherds identity!as selection criterion}

The bar construction of \S\ref{sec:volume-one-route} accepts
any $\mathcal{D}_X$-module~$V$
with a meromorphic pairing
$\mu \colon j_* j^*(V \boxtimes V) \to \Delta_!(V)$.
Place sections at points of the Fulton--MacPherson compactification
$\overline{C}_n(X)$, extract residues along
boundary divisors, and assemble a graded object
$\barB_X(V, \mu)$ with candidate differential
$d = d_{\mathrm{internal}} + d_{\mathrm{residue}}
+ d_{\mathrm{dR}}$.
The construction is geometric; the algebraic axiom emerges from the
nilpotence question:
\begin{center}
\emph{For which inputs $(V, \mu)$ does
$d^2 = 0$?}
\end{center}

\noindent
Expanding $(d_1 + d_2 + d_3)^2$, the terms
$d_1^2$, $d_3^2$, and all mixed anticommutators vanish
for formal reasons (cochain complex, de~Rham,
residue exact sequence). The content is
$d_2^2 = 0$: residue extraction is required to be nilpotent
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bar-nilpotency-complete}).
Three cases arise from index overlap in
$d_2^2 = \sum_{i<j}\sum_{k<\ell}
\Res_{D_{k\ell}} \circ \Res_{D_{ij}}$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \emph{Disjoint indices}
 ($\{i,j\} \cap \{k,\ell\} = \emptyset$).
 Residue operations commute; cancellation is by the
 Koszul sign rule. The condition on~$\mu$ is automatic.

\item \emph{One shared index} (say $j = k$).
 Three boundary strata meet at the codimension-$2$ locus
 $D_{ij\ell}$ of triple collision. The cyclic sum of
 iterated residues
 \[
 \sum_{\textup{cyclic}}
 \Res_{D_{j\ell}} \circ \Res_{D_{ij}}
 \bigl[\mu(\mu(\phi_i, \phi_j), \phi_\ell)
 \otimes \omega\bigr]
 = 0
 \]
 vanishes exactly when two independent inputs are present: the Arnold
 relation cancels the logarithmic two-form coefficients, and the
 Borcherds identity cancels the corresponding OPE-mode coefficients.
 This is the associativity of the chiral product, coupling all OPE pole
 orders simultaneously.

\item \emph{Same pair}
 ($\{i,j\} = \{k,\ell\}$).
 $\Res_{D_{ij}}^2 = 0$ because the first Poincar\'e residue removes the
 unique logarithmic normal factor along \(D_{ij}\); after restriction to
 \(D_{ij}\) the logarithmic normal factor has already been consumed.
\end{enumerate}
Cases~(i) and~(iii) are automatic.
Case~(ii) imposes exactly the Borcherds identity
(equation~\eqref{eq:borcherds-axiom},
Definition~\ref{def:chiral-algebra}).
The FM bar
construction produces $d^2 = 0$ if and only if the OPE
satisfies the Borcherds identity. This is the \emph{selection principle}:
configuration space geometry determines the algebraic axioms.

The two simplest families verify the principle by computation.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Heisenberg} ($\alpha(z)\alpha(w) \sim
k/(z-w)^2$, purely double-pole singular part). The simple-pole
coefficient vanishes, so
$d_{\mathrm{bracket}} = 0$. The full differential reduces to
$d_{\mathrm{curvature}}$, which produces scalars:
$d_{\mathrm{curvature}}^2 = 0$ automatically. The Arnold
relation handles the form coefficient in case~(ii), while the
coefficient side is central/vacuum and the Borcherds identity reduces to
the corresponding vacuum-mode identities.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Kac--Moody} ($h(z)\,e(w) \sim
2e(w)/(z-w) + \cdots$, simple \emph{and} double poles).
The bracket component $d_{\mathrm{bracket}}$ is now nonzero, and
$d_{\mathrm{bracket}}^2 \neq 0$, computed explicitly for all
$2048$ sign conventions
(\S\ref{subsec:frame-sl2-bar-low-degree},
Remark~\ref{rem:frame-why-dbracket-fails}). The obstruction is
proportional to the level~$k$: it is the double-pole data that
$d_{\mathrm{bracket}}$ ignores. The Borcherds identity absorbs
this obstruction:
\[
d_{\mathrm{bracket}}^2
= -\{d_{\mathrm{bracket}},\, d_{\mathrm{curvature}}\}.
\]
The curvature is the obstruction; the Borcherds identity is the
integrability condition
(Proposition~\ref{prop:pole-decomposition}).

\subsection{The Heisenberg algebra}
\label{subsec:five-theorems-heisenberg}

\begin{warning}[Three Hochschild theories]
\label{warn:three-hochschild-intro}
\index{Hochschild cohomology!three variants}%
Three inequivalent notions of ``Hochschild cohomology'' coexist
in this theory; conflating them is a persistent source of
error.
\emph{Chiral Hochschild}
\[
\ChirHoch^\bullet(\cA,\cA)
=
\mathbf R\!\operatorname{Hom}_{\cA^e}(\cA,\cA)
\]
is the derived endomorphism object of the diagonal bimodule.  A family
datum $H_H(\cA;S)$ computes it by a complete model and a strong
deformation retract supported in~$S$.
\emph{Topological Hochschild} $\mathrm{HH}^*_{\mathrm{top}}$
is the factorization homology on the circle $S^1$; it lives
in dimension~$1$ higher and governs the $\Eone$-chiral bar of
Volume~II.
\emph{Categorical Hochschild} $\mathrm{HH}^*(\mathrm{Rep}(\cA))$
is the categorical Hochschild cohomology of the module category; it governs
the Drinfeld center and the $E_2$ structure.
See Principle~\ref{princ:geometric-origin-hochschild}
(Chapter~\ref{ch:algebraic-foundations}) for the geometric
origin that distinguishes all three.
\end{warning}

\begin{principle}[Five facets of $\Theta_\cA$]
\label{princ:five-facets}
\index{MC element!five facets|textbf}%
Let\/ $\cA$ be a modular Koszul chiral algebra on a smooth projective
curve~$X$, and let\/ $\Theta_\cA := D_\cA - d_\cA^{(0)} \in \gAmod$
be the bar-intrinsic Maurer--Cartan element
\textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:mc2-bar-intrinsic}}\textup{)}.
The five main theorems characterize five
\emph{facets} of~$\Theta_\cA$: existence, quadratic recognition,
decomposition, leading coefficient, and coefficient ring.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Alph*)}]
\item \emph{Arena}
 \textup{(}Theorem~A\textup{)}.
 $\Theta_\cA$ \textbf{exists}.
 The adjunction $\Omega_X^{\mathrm{ch}}\dashv B_X^{\mathrm{ch}}$
 supplies the unit
 $C\to B_X^{\mathrm{ch}}\Omega_X^{\mathrm{ch}}(C)$ and the counit
 $\Omega_X^{\mathrm{ch}}B_X^{\mathrm{ch}}(A)\to A$; with this arena
 in place, $\Theta_\cA$ arises as $D_\cA-d_\cA^{(0)}$.

\item \emph{Quadratic recognition}
 \textup{(}Theorem~B\textup{)}.
 The chosen quadratic core \textbf{represents the full bar} precisely
 on the Koszul locus. Under $H_{\mathrm{CL}}$, the comparison
 $q_\cA\colon\cA^{\mathrm i}\to B_X(\cA)$ is a quasi-isomorphism
 precisely when $H^*\operatorname{Cone}(q_\cA)=0$.

\item \emph{Decomposition}
 \textup{(}Theorem~C\textup{)}.
 $\Theta_\cA$ \textbf{splits}.
 The chiral derived centre has the scalar Verdier complementarity
 $K^\kappa(\cA,\cA^!)=\kappa(\cA)+\kappa(\cA^!)$; ordinary centres
 and categorical Drinfeld centres retain their respective ambients.

\item \emph{Modular trace}
 \textup{(}Theorem~D\textup{)}.
 For $g\geq2$, the genus-$g$ component first determines
 $\operatorname{Obs}^{\mathrm{def}}_g(\cA)\in H^2(\Def_g(\cA))$.
 The pointed identity is
 $\operatorname{tr}_1\operatorname{Obs}^{\mathrm{def}}_{1,1}(\cA)
 =\kappa(\cA)\lambda_1$ under~$H_D^1$.
 The package~$H_D^K$ gives
 $\mathfrak O_g^K(\cA)=\kappa(\cA)\lambda_{-1}(\mathbb E_g)$ and
 $\operatorname{ch}_g(\mathfrak O_g^K(\cA))=
 (-1)^g\kappa(\cA)\lambda_g$; $H_D^{\mathrm{tr}}$ supplies the
 comparison with the deformation class.  The graph package gives
 $F_g=\kappa\lambda_g^{\mathrm{FP}}+\delta F_g^{\mathrm{cross}}$.

\item \emph{Family support}
 \textup{(}Theorem~H\textup{)}.
 A package $H_H(\cA;S)$ retracts the complete chart cochain complex
 onto $K_{\cA,S}$ and gives
 $\operatorname{Supp}\ChirHoch^\bullet(\cA)\subseteq S$.  Finite
 graded pieces give the family Hilbert polynomial over which
 $\Theta_\cA$ varies.  De Sole--Kac variational Poisson cohomology
 is nonvanishing in unboundedly many degrees for Virasoro-type
 Poisson vertex algebras \cite{BDSK21}, so the concentration lives
 strictly on the stated Koszul/bounded locus, with the
 bounded-to-chart comparison part of the package.
\end{enumerate}
\end{principle}

\begin{theorem}[Central charge complementarity]\label{thm:central-charge-complementarity}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
\index{central charge!complementarity|textbf}
\textup{[Regime: curved-central on the Koszul locus
\textup{(}Convention~\textup{\ref{conv:regime-tags})}.]}

For affine and principal \(\mathcal W\)-families on the stated
Koszul/DS lane, let
\[
k'=\iota_{\mathrm{crit}}(k):=-k-2h^\vee
\]
be the critical-level reflection. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] \emph{Affine Kac--Moody.}
 $c(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_k) + c(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k'})
 = 2 \dim \mathfrak{g}$.
\item[\textup{(b)}] \emph{Principal $\mathcal{W}$-algebras:}
 $c(\mathcal{W}^k(\mathfrak{g})) + c(\mathcal{W}^{k'}(\mathfrak{g}))
 = 2\operatorname{rank}(\mathfrak{g}) + 4h^\vee \dim \mathfrak{g}$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ in
part~\textup{(b)} gives $c + c' = 26$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part~(a).} The Sugawara central charge is
$c(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_k) = k \dim\mathfrak{g}/(k + h^\vee)$.
Since $k' + h^\vee = -(k + h^\vee)$:
\[
c(k) + c(k') = \frac{k \cdot d}{k+h^\vee}
+ \frac{(k+2h^\vee) \cdot d}{k+h^\vee}
= \frac{2(k + h^\vee) \cdot d}{k+h^\vee} = 2d
\]
where $d = \dim\mathfrak{g}$.

\emph{Part~(b).} The Drinfeld--Sokolov central charge is
$c(\mathcal{W}^k(\mathfrak{g})) = r - 12|\rho|^2 (t-1)^2/t$
where $r = \operatorname{rank}(\mathfrak{g})$ and
$t = k + h^\vee$.
The involution sends $t \mapsto -t$:
\[
c(k) + c(k') = 2r + 12|\rho|^2 \cdot
\frac{(t+1)^2 - (t-1)^2}{t}
= 2r + 48|\rho|^2.
\]
The Freudenthal--de~Vries identity
$|\rho|^2 = h^\vee \dim\mathfrak{g}/12$
(under normalization $(\theta, \theta) = 2$;
see~\cite{Kac}, Ch.~13)
yields $c + c' = 2r + 4h^\vee d$.

\emph{Verification.}
$\mathfrak{sl}_2$: $2(1) + 4(2)(3) = 26$.
$\mathfrak{sl}_3$: $2(2) + 4(3)(8) = 100$.
$\mathfrak{sl}_N$:
$2(N{-}1) + 4N(N^2{-}1) = 2(N{-}1)(2N^2{+}2N{+}1)$.
\end{proof}

\end{proof}

\begin{principle}[The $\Eone$ primacy thesis]
\label{princ:e1-primacy}
\index{averaging map!information loss}
Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:e1-primacy}} admits a concise restatement.
The $\Eone$/ordered structure is the level-$2$ chain surface before
symmetrisation. The $\Einf$/symmetric/modular structure is its
$\Sigma_n$-coinvariant shadow under~$\operatorname{av}$.
The five main theorems A, B, C, D, and~H are the invariants that survive
averaging. The surjective conductor flows from the rich $\Eone$ side,
which contains the $R$-matrix, the
KZ associator, the Yangian/quantum-group comparison on the
evaluation-generated core, and the spectral line-operator data,
to the coarser $\Einf$ side, which contains the modular
characteristic~$\kappa$, the shadow obstruction tower, and the
genus expansion of the closed sector.
\end{principle}

\paragraph{Five reasons for the inversion.}
\textsc{Operadic primacy.} In the hierarchy of $E_n$-operads,
$\Eone = \mathrm{Ass}$ is the primitive associative operad and
$\Einf = \Com$ arises from it as the colimit
$\Com = \colim_n E_n$ or, equivalently, by
$\Sigma_n$-coinvariants on each degree. At the level of cofree
coalgebras, Stasheff's theorem identifies $T^c$ (deconcatenation)
as the universal cofree conilpotent coalgebra; the symmetric
cooperad $\mathrm{Sym}^c$ is recovered from $T^c$ by external
$\Sigma_n$-coinvariants. The bar functor that respects this
universality is $\barB^{\mathrm{ord}} = T^c(s^{-1}\bar\cA)$; the
symmetric bar is its coinvariant image.

\textsc{Information content.} An equivariant
$\End(V^{\otimes n})$-valued meromorphic function strictly
contains its scalar trace. The averaging map
$\operatorname{av}$ is surjective and has kernel: the kernel
records the non-symmetric components of the line-operator data.
For Heisenberg, $\Omega_{\cH}=J\otimes J$ is rank-one and
flip-invariant.  The collision kernel is
$K_{\cH}^{\mathrm{coll}}(z)=k\Omega_{\cH}/z$, and
$\tau_{\cH}q_2(K_{\cH}^{\mathrm{coll}})=\kappa(\cH_k)=k$.
For affine Kac--Moody,
$K_{k,\mathfrak g}^{\mathrm{coll}}(z)=k\Omega_{\mathrm{tr}}/z$
retains the ordered tensor data, while
$\tau_{k,\mathfrak g}q_2(K_{k,\mathfrak g}^{\mathrm{coll}})
=k\dim\mathfrak g/(2h^\vee)$.  Adding the Sugawara shift gives
$\kappa(\widehat{\mathfrak g}_k)
=(k+h^\vee)\dim\mathfrak g/(2h^\vee)$; the tensor class supports the
Drinfeld--Kohno and Yangian comparison surfaces.

\textsc{Physical primacy.} The natural physical objects are
ordered. Three-dimensional holomorphic-topological theories
have line operators along the topological direction $\R_t$
(Costello--Gaiotto~\cite{CostelloGaiotto2020}); these line operators
form the ordered convolution algebra ${\gAmod}^{\Eone}$ on
$\C_z \times \R_t$, and their fusion is the
$\Eone$-multiplication. Open-string field theory and
factorization on bordered curves carry intrinsic boundary
orderings. Integrable lattice models have $R$-matrices that
satisfy the ordered Yang--Baxter equation; their symmetric projection
is the conductor shadow. In every physical setting where the bar complex
appears, the line/boundary direction is ordered before any
$\Sigma_n$-symmetrization is taken.

\textsc{Categorical primacy.} The categorical objects of
representation theory are ordered. Quantum groups, Yangians, and
braided tensor categories are the natural framework for chiral
algebras coupled to a spectral parameter; their structure
constants are matrix-valued and obey ordered identities (CYBE,
pentagon, hexagon, mixed Yang--Baxter). Symmetric monoidal
categories are obtained from braided ones by the
$\Sigma_n$-coinvariant procedure on the braid groupoid; this is
exactly the categorical analogue of the averaging
map~\eqref{eq:intro-averaging}.

\textsc{Operadic primacy of Swiss-cheese.} The Swiss-cheese
operad $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$ governs the
open/closed derived center pair $(\cZ^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA), \cA)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:thqg-swiss-cheese}); the bar complex itself
is a single-coloured $\Eone$-chiral coassociative coalgebra
over $(\mathrm{ChirAss})^!$. The SC structure emerges on the
derived centre, while the bar complex remains the single-coloured
coalgebra. The closed sector of any
Swiss-cheese algebra is recovered from the open sector by passing
to $\Sigma_n$-coinvariants along the open-to-closed boundary
operator. The closed sector is intrinsically the resulting derived
quotient of the open sector.

\paragraph{Historical context.}
The manuscript developed from the Beilinson--Drinfeld theory of
factorization algebras on the Ran space~\cite{BD04}, which is
unordered: a factorization algebra assigns sections to finite
subsets of points, indexed independently of an ordering. That framing made the
symmetric bar complex the default starting point and treated the
ordered bar as a refinement. The reframing
recorded here reverses this: the ordered bar is the
universal object, and the symmetric bar is its
$\Sigma_n$-coinvariant. The five-theorem statements and proofs retain
their form; the dependency DAG is read in the opposite direction.
Concretely:
Chapter~\ref{chap:e1-modular-koszul} presents the ordered theory
in full, and the Introduction and
Chapter~\ref{chap:bar-cobar} onward present the av-images of the
ordered constructions.

\begin{definition}[Three-tier binary-kernel classification]
\label{def:three-tier-r-matrix}
\index{R-matrix!three-tier classification|textbf}%
\index{R-matrix!pole-free tier (a)}%
\index{R-matrix!vertex-algebra tier (b)}%
\index{R-matrix!Eone@$\Eone$-primitive tier (c)}%
The binary collision kernel $K_\cA^{\mathrm{coll}}(z)$ of a chiral
algebra $\cA$ falls into
exactly one of three tiers, according to how its singular structure is
determined by the underlying data.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item \emph{Pole-free commutative tier.}
 $K_\cA^{\mathrm{coll}}(z)=\tau$, the Koszul-signed flip on
 $V\otimes V$, is constant
 in~$z$.  Every collision has trivial scattering: the
 represented exchange operator is the identity up to sign, and the associated Yangian
 is the symmetric algebra on a finite-dimensional vector space.

\item \emph{Local OPE tier.}
 $K_\cA^{\mathrm{coll}}(z)$ has poles at $z = 0$, and its structure is determined
 by the local OPE of $\cA$ by the residue formula
 \eqref{eq:intro-kappa-from-r}: the pole orders are one less than
 the OPE pole orders, and the matrix
 coefficients are the OPE modes $a_{(n)}b$. The tier
 is closed under Drinfeld--Sokolov reduction and under free-field
 realizations.

\item \emph{Genuinely $\Eone$ tier.}
 $K_\cA^{\mathrm{coll}}(z)$ has poles at $z=0$ and belongs to an
 ordered chiral structure with independent braiding data.  A
 representation together with the classical or quantum
 Yang--Baxter relation supplies the $r$- or $R$-matrix. The natural algebraic
 framework is the quantum vertex algebra of
Etingof--Kazhdan~\cite{EtingofKazhdan00} or the Yangian double,
and the associated convolution algebra lives on ordered rather
than unordered configurations.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Three-tier examples and the three-leaps hierarchy]
\label{rem:three-tier-examples}
\index{R-matrix!three-tier classification!examples}%
Tier (a) is the commutative extreme. Finite-dimensional Lie algebras
at level $0$, commutative vertex algebras, and the trivial chiral
algebra $\omega_X$ all sit here. $\kappa = 0$, the shadow
obstruction tower is empty, and the bar complex collapses to the
Koszul resolution of a polynomial ring. Pole-free commutative is the
boundary stratum; every richer tier specialises to it when all
singular modes vanish.

Tier (b) contains every standard-landscape vertex algebra:
the Heisenberg $\cH_k$ with
$K_{\cH}^{\mathrm{coll}}(z)=k\Omega_{\cH}/z$
\textup{(}rank-one coefficient $k/z$\textup{)}, affine Kac--Moody
$\widehat{\mathfrak g}_k$ with
$K_{\widehat{\mathfrak g}_k}^{\mathrm{coll}}(z)
=k\,\Omega_{\mathrm{tr}}/z$, Virasoro $\mathrm{Vir}_c$ with
$K_{\mathrm{Vir}_c}^{\mathrm{coll}}(z)=(c/2)/z^3+2T/z$,
the $\mathcal{W}_N$-algebras, the $\beta\gamma$ system, and the
lattice vertex algebras $V_\Lambda$. In every case the collision
kernel is derived from the OPE by residue extraction.  A represented
$r$-matrix requires $H_{\mathrm{CYBE}}^{\mathrm{rep}}$.
Tier (b) is still $\Einf$-chiral in the three-leaps classification of
Volume~II: the algebra is commutative up to quasi-isomorphism, and
homotopy coinvariants recover the degree-$2$ operator
$q_2(K_\cA^{\mathrm{coll}})$.  Its normalized trace is $\kappa$ on
abelian and scalar families and $\kappa_{\mathrm{dp}}$ on the
non-abelian affine row; the full affine
modular characteristic adds the Sugawara shift $\dim(\fg)/2$.

Tier (c) is the genuinely $\Eone$-primitive extreme. The Yangian
$Y(\mathfrak g)$ and the Etingof--Kazhdan quantum vertex algebras
sit here: their represented braiding operators are supplied together
with the quantum Yang--Baxter relation, and their semiclassical terms
give classical $r$-matrices under
$H_{\mathrm{CYBE}}^{\mathrm{rep}}$. The associated convolution algebra
${\gAmod}^{\Eone}$ is strictly richer than its
$\Sigma_n$-coinvariant shadow, and the kernel of the averaging map
(Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-primacy}(iv)) is non-trivial at degree~$3$,
where the Drinfeld associator $\Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}$ records the kernel
of the symmetric projection. Tier (c) is where the $\Eone$
primacy thesis acquires independent braiding input.

The three tiers organise Part~\ref{part:seven-faces} (Seven Faces): tier (a) is the
trivial-scattering base case, tier (b) populates the seven-face
comparison table for every standard family, and tier (c)
activates the $\Eone$-refined conditions of
Chapter~\ref{chap:yangians}. The jump from (b) to (c) is the
single irreversible information step in the three-volume
dimensional reduction of
Remark~\ref{rem:three-volume-dimensional-reduction}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Three tiers as boundary-bulk coupling complexity]
\label{rem:three-tier-boundary-bulk}
\index{open/closed comparison!three-tier coupling}%
\index{Drinfeld double!boundary-bulk realizations}%
Read through the boundary-bulk comparison thesis, the three
tiers of Definition~\ref{def:three-tier-r-matrix} correspond to
increasing complexity of the coupling between the boundary algebra
and the cochain-level closed-sector slot.
\emph{Tier~(a)} is the free-field comparison surface: the boundary is
commutative, the scattering term vanishes, and the Drinfeld double
degenerates to the tensor product $\cA \otimes \cA^!$ with vanishing
cross-term.
\emph{Tier~(b)} is the interacting but $\Einf$-chiral comparison
surface: the boundary has OPE poles, but its local OPE determines the
ordered residue used by the comparison. Every standard vertex algebra
(Heisenberg, affine Kac--Moody, Virasoro, $\mathcal{W}_N$, lattice,

\emph{Tier~(c)} is the genuinely $\Eone$-primitive surface: the
braiding operator is independent input data, and ordered operators have
non-trivial time ordering beyond the local boundary-OPE residue. The
Yangian double and the Etingof--Kazhdan quantum vertex algebras
are model targets for this tier. Here the comparison object is
determined by the boundary algebra together with the $\Eone$ structure
of the ordered bar, and the Drinfeld
double $U_\cA$ is constructed on ordered configurations.
The three tiers are therefore three levels of ordered comparison
complexity: free, $\Einf$-interacting, and time-ordered.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Topologization: from $\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ to $\Ethree$]
\label{rem:topologization-intro}
\index{topologization theorem!introduction}%
The $\Eone$-chiral bar construction, combined with
$\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$, produces an $\Etwo$-algebra
via the operadic center. When the chiral algebra admits a
conformal vector together with a BRST primitive of holomorphic
translation (for affine Kac--Moody, the Sugawara antighost at
non-critical level), the holomorphic $\Etwo$ structure is promoted to
topological $\Etwo$: holomorphic translations become $Q$-exact,
hence homotopically trivial. Dunn additivity then gives
$\Etwo^{\mathrm{top}} \otimes \Eone^{\mathrm{top}} =
\Ethree^{\mathrm{top}}$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:topologization}
(\S\ref{sec:topologization}) establishes this for affine
Kac--Moody $V_k(\fg)$ at non-critical level, where the
Sugawara element and its antighost contraction provide the
inner conformal-vector datum explicitly. This null-homotopy is the bridge from the
chiral ($n = 2$) row of the dimensional ladder to the
Chern--Simons ($n = 3$) row. For general chiral algebras
with conformal vector (Virasoro, $\cW$-algebras),
Conjecture~\ref{conj:topologization-general} isolates the
missing datum: the bulk BRST complex. At critical
At level $k = -h^\vee$, the Feigin--Frenkel oper centre replaces the
Sugawara element, and the $\Etwo$ structure remains holomorphic.
\end{remark}

At tier~(b), the passage from chiral algebra to quantum group

\begin{convention}[Semantic levels: homotopy, model, shadow; \ClaimStatusDefinitional]
\label{conv:hms-levels}
\index{semantic levels|textbf}
\index{H/M/S convention|see{semantic levels}}

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{H-level (homotopy/native).}
The statement in the stable $\infty$-category of factorization
(co)algebras on $\operatorname{Ran}(X)$, or in the associated
formal moduli / shifted-symplectic language.  Its equivalence class is
the native object; its unbased self-equivalences form the homotopy
automorphism space of that object.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{M-level (model).}
An explicit dg / $L_\infty$ / filtered complex. The geometric
bar complex $\bar{B}_X(\cA) = (T^c(s^{-1}\bar{\cA}), d)$ built
from configuration space integrals on $\overline{C}_n(X)$ is the
distinguished model used in this volume.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{S-level (shadow).}
The cohomological, Ext, numerical, or generating-function
consequence: bar cohomology dimensions, Hilbert series,
$\kappa(\cA)$, free energies $F_g(\cA)$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Notation.}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\simeq$\,: equivalence in the homotopy/$\infty$-categorical
 sense (H-level).
\item $\cong$\,: strict isomorphism of chosen dg models (M-level).
\item $=$\,: reserved for definitions and literal equalities.
\end{itemize}
The proved results are proved at
M-level and stated at H-level.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Based model comparison.}
Fix the bar object~$\mathcal B_X(\cA)$ and structure maps
$u_i\colon B_i\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal B_X(\cA)$ from two admissible
dg factorisation-coalgebra presentations.  In the slice over the fixed bar
object, the based comparison space between
$(B_1,u_1)$ and~$(B_2,u_2)$ is contractible
(Proposition~\ref{prop:model-independence}).  After forgetting the
maps~$u_i$, the equivalence component is a torsor under
$\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal B_X(\cA))$.  A chosen based
quasi-isomorphism transports cohomology, spectral sequences, and
generating functions.
\end{convention}
